## Evidence → design boundary

- Adult identity: earless rounded head; compact short-tailed form; heavy tarsi; dark rufous/chocolate-brown upperparts; barred scapular/wing direction rather than New Britain's fine spangles; dark face without a conspicuous white eyebrow; small plain pale throat; darker upper-breast bars and irregular rufous bars over paler lower underparts; yellowish bill/feet. Eye colour is variable brown-to-yellow, so use subdued amber-yellow rather than a saturated universal rule.
- Sex: no measured, sex-linked external direction found. Male and female stay near equal scale and palette; do not impose female-larger.
- Juvenile: active searches found no species-specific nestling/fledgling plumage sequence, exact age, iris chronology, wing/tail development or flight threshold. Use an explicitly age-unknown older juvenile, eyes closed and resting, with down-softened head/body, short compact softly bounded wing and tail, and both feet supported. Do not call it a nestling or fledgling.
- Habitat/support: New Ireland humid lowland/hill forest edge is a range-compatible presentation, not a nest-site claim. Use one natural mossy branch; no cavity, eggs, nest lining or unsupported action.
- v1 integrity: repository record says 2,848,145 bytes and SHA-256 `c9f855...`; the available exported file is 786,445 bytes and SHA-256 `bfa7c3...`. It decodes, but its provenance differs, so v1 remains preserved and is excluded as an ImageGen visual source.

- Adult barred (not finely spangled) identity; no prominent brow; pale throat; amber-yellow eyes; yellowish bill/feet; compact short tail/heavy tarsi.
- Adults near equal scale; juvenile distinct by softness/stage, not a miniature fully feathered adult.

- No nest inference or unsupported flight. Science remains false until species-specific development and sex evidence exist.

## Problem → evidence → change → recheck

- **Problem:** first master gave the older juvenile a long pointed layered wing with crisp white adult-like marks.
- **Evidence:** none of the read sources supplies species-specific juvenile remex geometry; the saved boundary explicitly forbids mature wing inference.
- **Change:** one center-juvenile-only edit removed crisp spots, shortened and rounded the wing, softened it into body down and concealed the tail while preserving bilateral branch grip and closed eyes.
- **Recheck:** full 1536×1024 image plus 570×720 juvenile and 1020×600 adult/support crops were opened. The wing now ends well above the feet and reads as compact/down-softened; title, exactly three birds, adult identity, near-equal adult scale and natural supports remain intact. `PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS`; science remains false.
- **Applied iteration rule:** for evidence-poor *Ninox* young, an instruction to “avoid adult remiges” is insufficient by itself; the edit prompt must also prohibit crisp pale covert rows and specify a body-blended wing edge ending above the feet. This was actually applied and visually effective here, but it is a visual-control rule—not new biological evidence—and should be checked against earlier evidence-poor juvenile plates rather than applied to species with documented formed wings.
