## Problem → evidence → edit → recheck

1. **Problem:** the initial centre juvenile's viewer-left wing read as a regularly layered, developed flight-feather stack.
2. **Evidence:** the primary study fixes day-27 behaviour and place but does not justify mature remiges; the bird was still at its hatch position.
3. **Edit:** juvenile-only bounded edit; shortened and rounded both wings, added down cover, retained only very short close pin-feather tips. Adults, support, environment, layout and text were held invariant.
4. **Recheck:** full plate plus adult and juvenile crops were viewed. The juvenile is securely supported, not flying or flapping, and no long adult-style remex stack remains. Adult identity marks and typography remain intact.

## Remaining unknowns

- The paper's age is estimated from the observed nest history, not a directly witnessed hatch.
- Exact day-27 feather tract sequence, juvenile sex and subspecies transfer are unknown.
- One captive-adjacent resort pair does not establish population-wide sexual dimorphism or universal nest choice.
- The concept remains a research-bounded prototype, not a scientifically verified reconstruction.

## Iteration rule applied

When a source strongly dates behaviour but weakly documents feather chronology, constrain the pose and support to the dated observation and render feather development conservatively. Do not infer long layered remiges from an older juvenile or subadult photograph. This rule was applied here and the revised crop was visually rechecked; its wider validity remains to be tested on later species.
