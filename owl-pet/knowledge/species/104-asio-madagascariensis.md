## v2 revision — 2026-10-25

Fresh evidence-bounded rebuild using the previously read Rene de Roland & Goodman chapter, dated Vassen juvenile photograph and Mikkola p.509, cross-checked against current Cornell/eBird habitat/identity and USFWS taxonomy records. Adults retain orange irises, black bills, long widely separated tufts, golden-brown mottling and sparse fine lower-belly shaft streaks. A targeted edit extended tan featherlets from the tarsi onto proximal adult toe bases while leaving distal dark toe tips and claws exposed.

The center age-unknown older mesoptile juvenile is white-to-cream and fluffy with a blackish mask, dark-appearing eyes, short tufts, compact formed folded wings, a modest partly concealed tail and two-foot branch support. This preserves the photographed developmental stage instead of inventing a newborn.

Exact age, juvenile iris colour, continuous feather sequence, flight ability, toe-by-toe feather boundary, nest generality and fixed sex-size ratio remain unknown; scientifically_verified=false.
