## Sources actually read (2026-11-07)
1. **NSWO-MCCULLOUGH-CONWAY-2017** — McCullough & Conway, “Breeding behavior of Northern Saw-whet Owls in Oregon,” *Journal of Raptor Research* 51? / USGS publication page and abstract; DOI 10.3955/046.091.0211. The accessible abstract reports 858 h of nest video, two boxes, one successful nest, and 30-day incubation and nestling periods. Limitation: one successful nest; it supports a late-cavity-nestling boundary, not an exact age for a pictured individual.
2. **NSWO-PROJECT-OWLNET-HY** — Project Owlnet, “Hatching Year,” section “Hatching Year Northern Saw-whet Owls.” Near-fledging young acquire fulvous/chocolate juvenal plumage in the cavity; juvenal flight feathers are retained through the first prebasic molt. Limitation: ageing/banding guidance, not a controlled development series.
3. **NSWO-CORNELL-GALLERY** — Cornell Lab of Ornithology, All About Birds photo gallery, juvenile/adult captions. Juvenile reference images show cinnamon belly, dark unspotted brown back and bold pale facial V; adults show white crown streaking and white-spotted brown upperparts. Limitation: selected field photographs, locality/age vary.
4. **NSWO-PAXTON-WATTS-2008** — Paxton & Watts, “Mass variation in Northern Saw-whet Owls,” *Journal of Field Ornithology* 79(1):53–57, DOI 10.1111/j.1557-9263.2008.00145.x. Recaptures (n=372) show mass may change substantially and mass-based sex assignments can change; wing-chord thresholds overlap. Design use: no fixed display ratio.
5. **NSWO-PRIESTLEY-ETAL-2026** — Priestley et al., “Classifying the Sex of Northern Saw-whet Owls Using Morphometrics,” *Journal of Raptor Research* 60(3), DOI 10.3356/jrr2573, abstract/results. DNA-sexed Alberta migrants (n=445) differed on average in mass and wing chord, but the authors require regional calibration. Design use: female may be subtly fuller, never an exaggerated deterministic scale.

## Evidence facts and design boundary
- Adult: very small, compact, round head, no ear tufts; orange-yellow eyes and blackish bill; brown upperparts with white spotting and a white-streaked crown; pale underparts with warm rusty/brown streaking.
- Sex: plumage is visually similar. Morphometric means differ in one recent regional sample, but distributions overlap and mass varies. Render adults near equal in height; female only subtly fuller.
- Juvenile stage: late cavity nestling / near-fledging juvenal plumage. Dark chocolate/fulvous head and back, cinnamon belly, bold white V/brows; **no adult crown streaking and no ordered adult white spots on back or wing**. A short rounded formed juvenal wing is allowed because flight feathers develop in the cavity; do not imply demonstrated flight.
- Support: belly and both feet supported on an old woodpecker/flicker cavity lip; tail short and not used as an unsupported prop.
- Nest/habitat: secondary cavity in mature conifer or mixed woodland. Do not invent nest lining, exact cavity height, clutch contents or exact age.

## V1 problem → evidence → v2 correction
- **Problem:** archived v1 juvenile has conspicuous pale spots on the exposed wing, reading too much like adult upperpart pattern; adults also read too uniformly spotted.
- **Evidence:** Project Owlnet and Cornell juvenile references separate plain dark juvenal upperparts from adult crown/upperpart spotting.
- **Correction:** preserve layout and painterly prototype style, but give the late nestling a plain chocolate rounded wing/back, cinnamon belly and bold white facial V; reserve organized white crown streaking/upperpart spots for adults.
- **Validation target:** exact title/name, exactly three birds/labels, all feet supported, no unsupported flight claim.
