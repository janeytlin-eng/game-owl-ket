## Evidence facts

1. Mikkola 2013, printed p.282 / uploaded PDF p.284: c.43 cm and c.500 g; females appear only a little heavier, without sexed weights. Short, weakly erect tufts; gray-brown upperparts densely vermiculated; fine dense dark vermiculation below; dark-brown eyes with fleshy reddish lids; lead-gray pale-tipped bill; feathered tarsi and partly bare gray-brown toes. Down white; mesoptile barred whitish and brown and retains dark-brown eyes. Open/semi-open savanna, parks and gardens; avoids dense rainforest.
2. Barlow, Mikkola, Wink, Brohaugh & Brohaugh 2022, Malimbus 44:19–29, abstract and breeding summary read: full-species split supported by mitochondrial cytochrome-b; two ground nests in eastern Gambia, two eggs, 35–43 day nestling period, fledgling plumage grayish and parent-like; breeding may occur year-round.
3. Mikkola/Kuyok Torit record as reproduced with the 2022 paper figure: mango-tree nest close to a house, one young fledged 13 March 2015; one adult was darker and slightly smaller and identified as a melanistic male. This is individual evidence, not a universal sex-colour rule.
4. Avibase current concept and synonymy page read 2026-10-27: Avilist/IOC/Clements/BirdLife recognize *B. cinerascens*; earlier lists include *B. africanus cinerascens*.
