Freshly read `KOEP-REO2019-P31` reports Loja-basin observations at 2,092–2,285 m, including urban eucalyptus and Andean forest edge, chicks rescued in 2015/2017, a breeding window broadly spanning January–June, nesting in crevices among *Phoenix canariensis* fronds, and an approximately three-month chick-to-adult-plumage interval. This is an abstract/proceedings record: the rescued chicks are not individually staged or sexed, the interval is approximate, and the palm site is one recorded site rather than a universal nest.

The paper describes gray-white barred residual nessoptiles over the body, brown chest, black facial borders, white brows and yellow eyes; the young perched 3–4 m up on *Podocarpus*. Exact age, sex, wing timetable and first-flight threshold remain unknown. This is the principal juvenile endpoint for v5.

## Design boundaries

- Exactly three complete birds: adult male left, age-unknown fledged juvenile center, adult female right. All six feet have visible natural support.
- Adults near equal in apparent size; no generic “female larger” direction. Rich-brown is a conservative concept choice, not a subspecies claim.
- Adult identity: short distinct ear tufts, yellow eyes, greenish/blue-gray bill and cere, gray facial disc edged darker, white throat, irregular streaks/mottling, feathered tarsi and gray-brown toes. Avoid a conspicuous white nuchal collar and uniform decorative patterning.
- Juvenile: rounded tuftless crown; gray-white barred residual down/nessoptiles, brown chest, black face edge, white brows, yellow eyes and muted blue-gray bill. The folded wing is a small compact formed cluster, mostly hidden by loose down—no long adult-like scapular/flight-feather ladder and no long tail.
- Action: juvenile turns its head slightly upward toward an adult and leans gently into a supporting twig; both feet remain on the branch. No flight, jump, prey handling or unsupported display.
- Habitat: dusk in Loja/Ecuador Andean shrub or alder/eucalyptus forest edge, with a broad mossy *Podocarpus*-like branch and a secondary twig. Do not depict the palm-frond nest as universal; no egg or invented nest interior.
- Unknowns remain explicit: precise age, sex, subspecies, normal day-by-day feather sequence, exact wing/tail competence, first-flight threshold and sex-size direction.
