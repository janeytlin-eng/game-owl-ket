## Question

Does current evidence justify visibly larger or more heavily barred females in the v3 Forest Owlet concept?

## Evidence actually read

1. **FOW-MEHTA-RSD-2021** — Mehta et al. 2021, *Journal of Asian Ornithology* 37:60–65, open PDF. Twenty-seven females and nineteen males were measured live. All ten body measurements were non-significant at p<.05; mass, wing chord, head-to-tail and tail means differed by less than 3%. Female mean talon length was 11.4% longer but p=.071 and not a validated visual sex marker. PCA overlapped strongly. This directly rejects a visible female-size rule.
2. **FOW-ISHTIAQ-2005** — original Wiley article, DOI 10.1111/j.1474-919x.2004.00389.x, pp.197–205. Fledging at about 40 d and dependence for roughly another 45 d; female incubates and male supplies. Reused for developmental limits, not visible sex dimorphism.
3. **FOW-JATHAR-2004** — repository-indexed BNHS primary breeding account, pp.53–55. Reused for a 28–30 d recently emerged young bird with incompletely developed primaries/secondaries and weak, reluctant flight.
4. **FOW-KHAN-2023-HABITAT** — original article, DOI 10.5751/JFO-00244-940201. Large trees are relevant without requiring dense closed canopy; open agricultural cover can occur in the landscape.
5. **FOW-MIKKOLA-2013** — repository-recorded printed pp.421–423. The user-uploaded guide itself was not accessible in this run, so no fresh-page claim is made.
6. **FOW-KOPARDE-2018** — reused for the species-specific adult identity boundary.

## Corrected design inference

- **Source fact:** the larger live-bird sample did not detect significant adult size dimorphism.
- **Design inference:** male and female are rendered at equal apparent height, bulk, head size and wing length. They share the same species plumage. Pose only differentiates them.
- **Rejected inference:** female larger or more heavily barred. The non-significant talon trend is not visualized.
- **Juvenile boundary:** retain the documented 28–30 d recently emerged stage; supported crouch at the cavity lip or a short adjoining branch, dark/blotched ventrum, stumpy tail and short incomplete remiges. No flight pose and no adult covert rows.
- **Habitat boundary:** central-India dry deciduous teak with a large cavity tree and open agricultural edge, not closed rainforest.
- **Unknowns:** exact age of any single photographic individual, juvenile iris chronology, sex-specific juvenile development and a diagnostic adult plumage difference remain unknown.
- **Scientific result:** concept revision may pass visual production review but remains `scientifically_verified=false`.
