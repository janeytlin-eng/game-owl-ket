Questions
How does text to 3D work?
In two stages. The description is painted as a clean studio photo of one object on a plain background, then that photo goes through the same image-to-3D engine as an upload. You get both, so if the picture is right but the mesh is not, you can build the mesh again from the same picture.
Can I see the picture before the model is built?
Yes. Tick "Check the picture first" under the description. The run stops once the picture is painted: build the model from it, or change the description and paint again until it looks right. The model follows the picture, so that is where you get the look right.
What makes a good description?
One object, named plainly, with a material and a colour: 'a worn leather armchair', 'a ceramic teapot with blue flowers'. Style words help less than you would think; the image stage already knows to make a product photo. Scenes, people and text on the object do not work. One thing at a time.
Why did I get a different model the second time?
Each run paints a new photo from a new seed, so each run is a new object. That is on purpose: generate again until you like it, or tick "Check the picture first" to settle the picture before any model is built.
Can I get an STL to print?
Yes. Every result downloads as STL too, and Repair seals it into one solid that slices. Describe one chunky object with a clear silhouette, ideally on a base: 'a sitting cat figurine', 'a chess rook'.
Is it free, and who owns it?
Free, no sign-up required, and yours. Both models, FLUX.2 klein for the picture and TRELLIS.2 for the mesh, are open weights with licences that put no claim on their output.
Do I need an account or to sign up?
No. No sign-up, login or email is required: open the page and use it. The AI runs on a free daily allowance per visitor, and when it is used up the page says when it comes back; the tools that run in your browser keep working in the meantime.
