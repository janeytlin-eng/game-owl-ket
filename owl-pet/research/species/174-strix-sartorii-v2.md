## Evidence actually read

### CIO-PIEPLOW-ETAL-2020-P221-227
Pieplow, Spencer, Sanchez & Grosselet 2020. “Changes in the Status and Distribution of the Cinereous and Fulvous Owls in Mexico.” *Western Birds* 51:221–227. DOI https://doi.org/10.21199/WB51.3.3 . Printed pp.221–223 read 2026-11-01.

Facts: surveys in 2016, 2017 and 2019 detected the species only at Rancho La Noria/Cerro San Juan, Nayarit. Historical sites ranged about 600–3,000 m; the modern Nayarit site is humid pine–oak forest near 1,650 m. Limits: distribution/status and habitat source, not a developmental or sex-dimorphism study.

### CIO-EARBIRDING-2015-FLEDGLING
Pieplow & Spencer, “Mexico’s Mystery Owl,” 7 June 2015, https://earbirding.com/blog/archives/4905 . Direct field account and dated photographs/recordings re-read 2026-11-01.

Facts: the Nayarit young bird is explicitly called a begging fledgling; observers watched it again before dawn, recorded apparent feeding, and directly reported that it flew out of sight. This corrects the v1 boundary: exact age remains unknown, but flight ability is not unknown for this photographed stage. Limits: one fledgling and one adult at one site; no early-down sequence, nest or population-wide sex difference.

## Fact → design decisions

- Use cold gray/blackish-sepia adults, pale gray unringed facial discs, dark eyes, yellowish bill and fully feathered toes.
- Adults remain near equal in display size and identical in plumage; female fullness is subtle and non-quantitative.
- Centre bird is an **AGE-UNKNOWN FLIGHT-CAPABLE FLEDGLING** because flight was directly observed, but it remains fully supported in the plate.
- Use humid Nayarit pine-oak forest near 1,650 m. Do not invent a nest, clutch or earlier chick.
