## Current-name and historical-name boundary

ITIS retains valid *Asio grammicus* (Gosse, 1847) and maps original *Ephialtes grammicus* plus later *Pseudoscops grammicus* as synonyms. Repository #154 under *Pseudoscops grammicus* is therefore a historical-name duplicate, not an additional living species. This revision remains #142.

## Evidence synthesis

Gosse's original adult account on printed pp.19–23 was directly read in the Project Gutenberg transcription. The adult description supports hazel irides, pale blue-grey bill, dull lead feet, blackish-grey cere, dusky-brown to umber plumage with medial dark marks and undulated crossbars, c.1-inch conical ear tufts, a pale-umber face edged by a black ruff, minutely pencilled back/scapulars/wing coverts, bright-umber breast with wavy mottling and dark shaft dashes, plain fawn belly/thighs/vent, and a fully feathered tarsus. Gosse explicitly says Robinson's adult male agreed with his adult female except that Robinson called the warm colour cinnamon; this is evidence against inventing a strong sex-colour split.

The same primary account records two immature females described as downy. One downy immature was disturbed from a tree and flew to another at short distance; another youth was shaken from a small tree, fell to the ground and lay helpless by day. These are individual observations, not a universal chronology. They establish that "downy/immature" does not necessarily mean wingless, but they do not establish exact age, a standard first-flight threshold, or one universal action ability. Mikkola's older-juvenile description and July caption add a pale grey-brown back, dull cinnamon-buff remainder and retained head down. The BirdsEye downy-young photograph remains a useful texture/support comparator with no exact age or flight metadata.

The v3 juvenile is an almost wingless, uniform fluff ball. That was a conservative correction to an initially adult-hard wing, but it now conflicts with the stronger stage boundary above: an older down-remnant juvenile may have a small formed wing without being rendered flight-ready. V4 therefore restores one compact, softly edged, partly down-veiled folded wing and a little pale grey-brown dorsal surface while keeping the tail short/concealed and the body securely supported. No flight is depicted.

Peregrine Fund's species account supports lowland open woodland/forest-edge/garden/park/agricultural habitat, tree-cavity or tree-fork nesting, and explicitly says nestling growth, first-flight age and parental-care details are unknown. It is used to preserve these unknowns and select a leafy Jamaican edge habitat, not to invent a nest scene.

## Design boundary

- Exactly three birds: adult male left, older down-remnant juvenile center/front-lower, adult female right.
- Adults near-equal displayed height and mass; different natural poses, no imposed female-larger rule or colour morph split.
- Adult identity: dark/hazel-brown eyes; warm umber/tawny body; long but natural mottled ear tufts; black-edged pale face; irregular fine dark pencilling/wavy marks; dark shaft dashes on breast; plainer fawn lower belly; no large white scapular shields; blue-grey bill/cere; tarsi feathered to toe bases; dull lead/grey toes.
- Juvenile: clearly smaller/lower, pale grey-brown back, dull cinnamon-buff down-remnant body, wispy retained head down, dark/non-diagnostic irides, pale grey bill, one small rounded partly formed wing with soft irregular coverts and only restrained developing feather edges. Short tail concealed. Two-foot plus belly/branch-fork support. No spread wing, flight, fall, exact-age or independence claim.
- Habitat: leafy lowland Jamaican woodland/garden edge with a load-bearing fork; no nest, eggs, cavity-interior claim, prey or human prop.

## Unknowns and limits

Exact juvenile day age, iris chronology, sex, continuous feather sequence, first-flight threshold, degree of independence, nest construction detail, population-level sex-size ratio and individual pattern geometry remain unknown. Gosse's two immature observations demonstrate variation among individuals but do not quantify age or generalize ability. The resulting plate is a bounded concept and cannot be marked scientifically verified.
