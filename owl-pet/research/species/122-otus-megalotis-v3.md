## Evidence actually read for v3

### PSO-MIRANDA-2011 — primary taxonomy and adult morphology

- Miranda, H. C. Jr., Brooks, D. M. & Kennedy, R. S. 2011. “Phylogeny and Taxonomic Review of Philippine Lowland Scops Owls.” *Wilson Journal of Ornithology* 123(3):441–453. https://blog.hmns.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/PhilippineScopsEprint.pdf
- Printed pp.444 and 448–450 were read. Methods included only adult known-sex specimens: 11 male and 10 female *megalotis*. The paper supports full-species treatment of the Luzon unit, scapular stripes and tarsal feathers extending onto the upper foot. It reports the nominate as ashy-brown below with a brownish nape; the sample also contained a minority red morph.
- Sex boundary: female group measurements trend larger, but the paper does not supply a universal display-height formula. The concept may make the female subtly fuller, not dramatically taller.

### PSO-MIK2013-P170 — uploaded guide baseline

- Heimo Mikkola, *Owls of the World: A Photographic Guide* (2013), printed p.170 / uploaded PDF p.172, read directly from the user-uploaded file on 2026-10-31.
- Adult baseline: 23–28 cm, 200–310 g, wing 165–210 mm; long ear tufts; gray and rufous morphs; warm orange-brown eyes; horn-coloured bill; feathered tarsi; dense/secondary woodland habitat. The gray morph has a blackish crown, pale eyebrow toward the tufts and mottled/barred gray-brown upperparts.
- Juvenile summary: rufous-buff head/neck finely barred black, rufous face, faint pale facial shaft streaks, and fine lower-belly/thigh bars. This is a secondary synopsis and does not establish an exact age, flight threshold or literal folded-wing geometry.

### PSO-PROJECTNOAH-2023 — first-person backyard observation

- “Philippine Scops Owl — *Otus megalotis*,” photographed 5 May and published 9 May 2023. https://projectnoah.org/spottings/philippine-scops-owl-1
- Field notes state that a baby/juvenile fluttered down from a mango tree, controlled its descent with still-developing wings, could not fly back to the mother and sibling, later moved across the yard, climbed a mahogany trunk and was high in that tree the next day. Habitat was a backyard mango tree; the mother continued feeding.
- Limitation: community platform and one first-person event; no age, measurements or continuous feather chronology. It supports a mobile, branch-/trunk-supported pre-competent flyer and rejects a fully adult-performance pose, but cannot define exact wing length.

### PSO-OWLPAGES-2012 — dated juvenile photograph metadata

- “Philippine Scops Owl (*Otus megalotis*) juvenile,” Bram Demeulemeester; Mount Data, Luzon, April 2012. https://www.owlpages.com/owls/species.php?i=82
- The page identifies a juvenile securely perched on a moss/lichen branch. It is a useful real-bird visual comparator for support and general fluffy juvenile proportions.
- Limitation: no age, sex, behavior sequence or measurements; identification/photo metadata only. It cannot establish flight ability or a universal feather map.

## Source facts versus design inference

- Source fact: a fuzzy/juvenile individual may have formed flight feathers and can grip/climb, yet at least one documented individual could not fly back to the canopy.
- Design inference: choose an age-unknown mobile brancher with a compact, rounded, soft-edged folded wing embedded in body down, short/concealed tail and both feet gripping. Do not depict a newborn and do not erase all flight feathers.
- Source fact: adult females trend larger in the measured sample; exact standing-height ratio is not established.
- Design inference: adults are near equal in skeletal height, with the female only subtly fuller.
- Unknown: exact juvenile age, iris development, first-flight threshold, continuous feather sequence, literal tarsal/upper-foot boundary and a universal sex-size ratio.
