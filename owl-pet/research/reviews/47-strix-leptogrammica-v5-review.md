## Evidence-based check

- Identity: both adults show a warm rufous-brown crown and upperparts, a broad dark facial-disc border, dark brown eyes, no ear tufts and pale barred underparts, consistent with Samarawickrama et al. 2006 pp.73–74.
- Sex treatment: the sources read for this revision do not establish a useful display-scale dimorphism. The final composition therefore keeps the adults near-equal; the female's remaining difference is presentation/fullness, not a scientific size claim.
- Juvenile: designed against the primary paper's labelled 34-day cavity chick, not the later ready-to-fly bird. It is downy, supported by both feet on the cavity rim, has no flight pose, and does not expose a mature wing or tail silhouette.
- Nest and habitat: a deep fruit-tree cavity and wet-zone mixed tree/cultivation context follow the jackfruit-cavity account at the Sri Lankan site. The exact tree is illustrative rather than a claim that all nests use jackfruit.
- Composition: exactly three birds; adult male left, juvenile centre, adult female right; all three labels and the scientific-name title are legible; natural branch/cavity support is visible.

## Problem → evidence → modification → recheck

1. Initial image made the female conspicuously larger than the male.
2. The checked sources did not justify a strong size difference, and the repository rule forbids a blanket “female larger” shortcut.
3. One imagegen edit reduced only the female's apparent scale/fullness while preserving identity, layout, labels and supports.
4. Full-frame and adult crop recheck found the adults now near-equal enough for a non-dimorphism claim; no second edit was required.

## Remaining limitations / unknowns

- Exact day-34 remige geometry, flight threshold and iris chronology are not resolved by the read sources.
- Hong Kong rescue plates provide a cross-population stage gate, not a direct Sri Lankan age equivalence.
- Subspecies use remains conservative: current species name is shown, while `ochrogenys` and `indranee` are retained only as indexed taxonomic context.
- This is a concept-art review, not an ornithologist sign-off.

## Iteration rule

For owls without evidence of visible sexual dimorphism, do not encode sex chiefly through body size. Use near-equal geometry and, if needed, restrained pose/feather-fullness differences. This rule was applied and visually verified on #47 v5 only; other completed *Strix* sheets should be backchecked before claiming broader validation.
