# 157 · Vermiculated Fishing Owl · *Scotopelia bouvieri*

## Taxonomy
Current scientific name: *Scotopelia bouvieri* Sharpe, 1875. Historical combination: *Bubo bouvieri*. U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service confirms the current name and TSN 555424.

## Evidence read on 2026-10-23
- VFO-BM-CAT-1875 — original British Museum description, pp.11–12 and Plate I: dark brown adult with tawny vermiculations, black shaft lines, pale shoulder patch, barred flights/tail, and a single adult measurement series.
- VFO-IBIS-YOUNG-1904 — museum specimen report, pp.102–103: near-adult female with mostly white underparts, black flank bars and rufous thigh bars; juvenile specimens are documented but no detailed juvenile feather sequence is provided.
- VFO-OISEAUX-LIFE-2026 — 46–51 cm, no ear tufts, dark eyes, cream/yellow bill, pale yellow bare legs; breeding biology poorly known, old large stick nests reported, and some young retain mesoptile plumage at about three months.
- VFO-BIRDLIFE-2026 and VFO-FWS-TAX-2026 — current name and river-associated west-central African range/taxonomy.

## Design decisions for v1
Adults: fairly large, earless, rounded head; dark brown eyes; cream-yellow bill with darker tip; yellow bare lower legs and feet; warm cinnamon to yellow-brown upperparts densely vermiculated dark brown, distinct pale shoulder band, whitish underparts with dense dark streaks/bars, barred flights and tail. No stable visual sex dimorphism was established, so the male and female remain near-equal in height and differ only subtly in posture/fullness.

Juvenile: an age-unknown, older mesoptile-stage bird supported in a broad abandoned stick nest. It is darker and softer-textured than adults, with fuzzy contouring, short rounded wings and tail, and only diffuse vermiculation. Exact age, feather-tract sequence and flight ability are not asserted.

Habitat: humid Guineo-Congolian riverine forest beside a broad shaded river; low fishing perch and large tree canopy.

## Scientific boundary
Breeding biology, exact juvenile age/feather sequence, sex-specific linear measurements and regional colour variation remain insufficiently documented. The old-stick-nest and slowly maturing mesoptile stage are treated as conservative design boundaries, not full scientific validation. scientifically_verified: false
