## Sources actually read

1. `ELE-SAWADA-IWASAKI-ETAL-2021-RSD` — Sawada et al., “Reversed sexual size dimorphism in the Ryukyu Scops Owl *Otus elegans* on Minami-daito Island,” *Ornithological Science* 20:15–26, DOI 10.2326/osj.20.15. Full PDF read, especially printed pp.15–18 and abstract. Dataset: 770 individuals/17 years. Females were larger in body mass, culmen, bill depth/width, tail and flattened wing, while males were larger in tarsus and head; several directions/degrees varied by year. Incubation is 26–30 d and fledging 28–32 d (p.18). This does not support a fixed visible female-taller ratio.
2. `ELE-AKATANI-MATSUO-TAKAGI-2011-JRR` — Akatani, Matsuo & Takagi, “Breeding Ecology and Habitat Use of the Daito Scops Owl (*Otus elegans interpositus*) on an Oceanic Island,” *Journal of Raptor Research* 45(4):315–323, DOI 10.3356/JRR-10-82.1. Full PDF read. Printed pp.315–316: 30 nests in introduced *Casuarina* cavities and two vertical hollows in broken native fan palms; pp.316–319: fragmented shelterbelts/cane landscape and cavity measurements. Supports cavity-lip and Minami-daito environment, not chick feather geometry.
3. `ELE-BIRD-RESEARCH-NEWS-2011-P1-2` — Bird Research News 8(7), 2011, species account for *Otus elegans*. Full two-page PDF read. Printed p.1: Minami-daito adults 18 cm; wing means male 155.3 mm/female 155.7 mm with overlapping ranges; body mass male 84.46 g/female 92.44 g; sexes same colour; gray facial disc with rufous edge, yellow-brown iris, dark-gray bill, shoulder white spots, gray-white bare toes. Printed pp.1–2: *Casuarina* cavity nesting and shelterbelt/cane habitat. Useful as institutional synthesis, not a substitute for individual primary study.
4. `ELE-SAWADA-AUTHOR-GROWTH-2021` — Akira Sawada’s author research summary for Sawada, Akatani & Takagi 2021, “Growth curves of Ryukyu Scops Owl nestlings…,” *Ardea* 109. Sections “結果：リュウキュウコノハズクの成長” and growth-curve figure read. It reports 99 chicks measured 2004–2008: by about 30 d/fledging, body mass and tarsus were near adult values while wing length was still growing. No printed-page claim is made because the full article PDF was not exposed in this run.
5. `ELE-ITIS-555377-2026` — ITIS TSN 555377, accessed 2026-11-06. Current valid *Otus elegans*; synonym/original combination *Ephialtes elegans*; recognizes *O. e. interpositus*. Taxonomy only.

## Source facts vs design inference

### Source facts
- Minami-daito adults overlap strongly in wing length; females are heavier on average, but RSD direction varies among traits and years.
- Near fledging, body mass/tarsus can be near adult while wing length is still growing.
- The focal population nests mainly in *Casuarina* cavities within shelterbelts/cane-field mosaic.
- Adult sexes are similarly coloured; the species has bare gray-white toes in the Minami-daito account.

### Conservative design decisions
- Keep adult crowns almost level and equal in standing height; female only subtly fuller, not uniformly taller.
- Preserve an **age-unknown late nestling / pre-fledging study stage**: near-adult head/torso, fluffy mesoptile, compact rounded developing wings ending above the cavity lip, concealed/very short tail, both feet supported. Do not label an exact day or claim sustained flight.
- Preserve Minami-daito *Casuarina* cavity/shelterbelt setting. Do not invent a nest bowl or unsupported prey.
- Preserve v3 adult bare-gray toes and species-specific facial/shoulder/underpart pattern.

## Unknowns and limits

Exact individual age, sex, continuous feather-tract sequence, sustained-flight threshold, universal juvenile iris colour, quantified standing-height ratio, and fine toe geometry remain unknown. One island population cannot calibrate every subspecies. Concept may be science-calibrated but is not scientifically verified.
