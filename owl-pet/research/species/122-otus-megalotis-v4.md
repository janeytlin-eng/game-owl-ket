## Evidence actually read

### PSO-SANCHEZ-2012 — primary field study / printed pp.96–114

- Michael S. Sanchez. 2012. “Relative Abundance of the Philippine Scops-Owl *Otus megalotis megalotis* (Walden) in Marinduque and Mt. Makiling, Philippines.” *Our Nature* 10:96–114. https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=8264d81298a04051a2e00b774e1aea544d619d6c&repid=rep1&type=pdf
- Actually opened and read printed pp.96–100, including Fig.1 on p.99. The study documents lowland/secondary-forest survey settings in Marinduque and Mt. Makiling. Its adult synopsis reports a gray phase with blackish crown, white eyebrow, gray-brown upperparts, pale line, orange-red eyes, ivory-horn bill, dirty-white/flesh-gray feet and a heavily feathered tarsus; it reproduces an adult/juvenile figure.
- Limits: adult appearance is partly synthesized from du Pont/Kennedy; the paper predates the later split and uses a broad older taxonomic frame. Generic owl egg/hatch timing on p.100 is not accepted as species-specific juvenile chronology. Fig.1 is a comparator, not a dated growth sequence. High for actual survey habitat; moderate for compiled adult colors; low for development.

### PSO-GMA-PENRO-2015 — agency-attributed rescue report

- GMA News Online, 29 May 2015, “Baby Scops Owl rescued in La Union.” https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/scitech/science/495242/baby-scops-owl-rescued-in-la-union/story/
- Actually opened and read lines 3–31. A weakened young owl found after landing on a house had difficulty flying; PENRO’s initial assessment called it under one year and noted short wings before taking it into care.
- Limits: the page’s displayed image is explicitly an archive adult, not the rescued juvenile; health was abnormal, age was broad, and there are no measurements or serial photographs. It cannot define a healthy fledging threshold or literal feather map. It is used only as a warning against equating “some formed wing” with competent flight.
