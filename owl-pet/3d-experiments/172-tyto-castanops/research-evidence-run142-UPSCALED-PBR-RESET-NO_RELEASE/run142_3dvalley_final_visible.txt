The long version, with what the model can and cannot know from one photo, is in how image to 3D actually works. No photo? Describe the object and one is painted for you. The models and what each costs are below.

The textures are read off the pixels the photo has, so a small or blurry picture comes back as a smooth, vague model. Upscale it first with Upsampler's free image upscaler, from the same team as this site, and drop the sharper picture in here.

What you get

A detailed mesh with PBR textures (colour, roughness and metallic), in the GLB that game engines, Blender and every web viewer open directly. It is a starting point, not a scan: the engine sees one photo and infers the shape, so detail on the far side is invented and fine geometry such as spokes, lettering and thin handles is smoothed over. For a prop, a print or a placeholder, that is usually exactly enough.

Once it is generated, the other tools take over: check the printability and cost, remesh it for a game, paint new textures on it, or convert it to whichever format the next program wants. For the next step in a modeller, see how to take a generated model into Blender, Unity or Unreal. Looking for something that already exists? Search the free 3D models first.

Questions
What kind of photo works best?
One object, most of the frame, plain background, lit from the front and slightly from above. The background is removed automatically. The engine only sees one side, so the back is a guess. Symmetric objects come out best.
How long does it take?
About a minute on a free GPU, plus a short queue when they are busy.
Can I turn an image into an STL?
Yes: every result downloads as STL too, and PNG, JPG, WebP and HEIC all work. It is a full 3D object, round on every side, not a picture raised off a flat plate; for a logo or a lithophane you want an extrusion tool instead. For printing, Repair seals the model into one solid first.
Can I 3D print it?
After one click. A generated mesh comes out with small holes, edges shared by three faces and loose fragments, which slicers reject. Repair, under Continue with, patches it and, where patching is not enough, rebuilds it as one sealed solid with the same shape; the STL you download there slices. It is scaled to millimetres, and the print cost calculator sets the size and shows the filament cost.
What happens to my photo?
It is sent to the GPU for the run and discarded afterwards. 3D Valley does not store it, and the result comes straight back to your browser. Every download after that, STL, OBJ or GLB, is made on your device.
Is it really free, and who owns the result?
Free, no sign-up required, and the model is yours. The free engine is an open-weights model whose licence puts no claim on what it generates.
Do I need an account or to sign up?
No. No sign-up, login or email is required: open the page and use it. The AI runs on a free daily allowance per visitor, and when it is used up the page says when it comes back; the tools that run in your browser keep working in the meantime.
