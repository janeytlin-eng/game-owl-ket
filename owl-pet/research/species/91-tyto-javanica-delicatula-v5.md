## Current/old name mapping

ITIS TSN 1063605 treats *Tyto javanica delicatula* (Gould, 1837) as a valid subspecies of *T. javanica*. Synonyms are *Tyto alba delicatula*, *Strix delicatulus*, *Tyto delicatula* and *Tyto delicatula delicatula*. These are lookup names, not additional production species. #91 remains distinct from #01 European *T. alba* and #90 American *T. furcata*.

## Evidence actually read

1. **JAV-MCLAUGHLIN-DEBUS-2025-P116-123** — McLaughlin & Debus, “Breeding biology and behaviour of the Eastern Barn Owl *Tyto javanica delicatula* in north-western Victoria,” *Australian Field Ornithology* 42:116–124, DOI 10.20938/afo42116124; publisher PDF read.
   - Printed p.116 and pp.118–119: 14 nests in semi-arid Victorian Mallee wheat-belt; 13/14 nests were tree hollows; estimated nestling period c.46–57 d, but fledging definition and asynchronous hatching mean exact ages are approximate.
   - Printed pp.117–118: adult-like fledglings retain obvious mesoptile down for at least their first week outside and are more heavily spotted ventrally; plumage differences between sexes are slight and birds were not individually sexed.
   - Printed pp.120–121: at c.31–45 d, nestlings solicit food; by c.40 d they ascend inside the hollow and look out, then back down when fed; wing-stretching/exercise occurs at the entrance. A displaced chick climbed back using bill, claws and possibly wings.
   - Printed p.121: from c.46 d, young may climb/jump/flap out and fly around the nest tree; near-fledging birds may doze on one foot. Therefore a c.40-day entrance bird is shown supported and grounded, not flying.
   - Printed p.123: authors caution Australian biology need not match European/African/American populations; taxonomy note supports *T. javanica* complex split.

## Applied boundaries

- Adults stand near-equal in visible height. Male modestly paler/cleaner; female modestly warmer/darker with somewhat larger/more ventral and leg flecks. Do not claim reliable visual sexing or impose a fixed ratio.
- Juvenile is c.40-day, still in the nestling period: adult-like facial structure developing, clear residual white mesoptile down on crown/shoulders/flanks, substantial emerging body plumage, developed fresh but not fully sleek wings and short tail.
- Action: both feet grip separate broad hollow-lip surfaces; bird leans outward in a lookout/food-soliciting posture with folded wings. No flight, hovering, unsupported balance or one-foot doze for this selected c.40-day stage.
- Habitat: large natural oil-mallee hollow in semi-arid Victorian wheat-belt/open Mallee woodland; no eggs, nest box or human shed.
- Unknown: exact individual age, sex, tract chronology, pair-specific ratio, precise flight threshold and regional transfer.
