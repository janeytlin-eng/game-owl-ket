# #161 Blakiston's Fish Owl — *Ketupa blakistoni*

## Taxonomy
- Current project/IOC name: *Ketupa blakistoni* (Seebohm, 1884).
- Historical combination used by Mikkola and older literature: *Bubo blakistoni*.
- The IOC taxonomic update explicitly records the genus transfer.
- Scope remains the project's fixed 251 extant species; this card does not recalculate checklist totals.

## Evidence facts
- Adult total length 660–685 mm in Hayashi's cited sample; male mass 3150–3450 g, female 3360–4600 g.
- Sexes are similar in plumage. Adult identity includes massive build, sideways-slanting tousled ear-tufts, faded lemon-yellow eyes, grayish bill/cere, ashy-brown upperparts with dark shaft streaks, pale gray/yellow-brown underparts with dark shaft streaks and fine wavy cross-bars, a white throat, 7–8 pale tail bars, cream-feathered tarsi and bare lead-gray toes.
- Natal down is white; a near-black mask develops from about 10–12 days; mesoptile is smoke-brown/gray with faint transverse spotting.
- Hayashi's Photo 2 directly shows an approximately 50-day late nestling. Usual fledging is 48–60 days, with one cited 69-day case.
- Habitat is riverine broadleaf/mixed forest or moist taiga near rivers/lakes/marshes; open water that remains ice-free is important. Natural nests are generally very large broadleaf-tree cavities; nest boxes and rarer alternatives also occur.

## Design decision
Use a supported approximately 50-day cavity nestling, not an unverified hatchling. It may have formed developing wings because it is near the usual fledging window, but wings remain shorter/rounder/softer than adults, with fluffy mesoptile, a short tail and diffuse—not crisp adult—pattern. Do not claim flight. Adults share plumage; female is fuller/heavier but not assigned an exaggerated fixed height difference.

## Unknowns / conflicts
- The small sex mass ranges overlap, while Mikkola summarizes females as averaging more than 1 kg heavier. Direction is supported; a universal visual ratio is not.
- Exact 50-day flight skill, toe exposure, subspecies palette and continuous juvenile feather chronology are unknown.
- The selected natural cavity is evidence-supported habitat context, not a claim of one standardized geometry.

## v2 science revision — 2026-10-28

Archived v1 was actually opened at 1536×1024. Its composition, labels, supports, female-fuller direction and late-nestling stage pass; v2 targets overly repetitive/high-contrast adult folded-wing marks, makes the feathered-tarsus/bare-toe boundary clearer, and explicitly labels the center bird as both juvenile and approximate late nestling.

Movin et al. (2022, DOI 10.1016/j.avrs.2022.100021) analyzed 192 duets from 22 pairs and proposed separating continental *doerriesi* from the insular nominate form. Their p.5 plumage discussion identifies a white back patch and near-white adult tail in continental birds. Because the retained scene is Hokkaido and the project still follows the current repository/IOC name *Ketupa blakistoni*, this is a negative design boundary: do not add those continental traits; do not claim the proposal is universally adopted.

New adult cross-check: The Owl Pages lines 64–69 agree on yellow iris, grayish bill, deep-brown wings with buff-yellow bars, 7–8 cream-yellow tail bars, cream-feathered tarsi and bare lead-gray toes. This secondary account refines checking but does not replace the primary/guide juvenile anchors.

Unknowns remain exact individual plumage map, universal sex ratio, exact 50-day flight ability and continuous feather chronology. Scientific status remains pending.


## v3 science revision — 2026-11-07

The October 2026 Clements/eBird update now splits continental Northern Fish-Owl *Ketupa doerriesi* from monotypic Hokkaido/insular *K. blakistoni*. V3 therefore narrows to Hokkaido *K. blakistoni* sensu stricto and treats older broad-sense *Bubo/Ketupa blakistoni* records as scope-sensitive. Do not borrow the conspicuous continental white nape/back patch or near-white tail.

Hayashi/Japan Bird Research 2010 printed pp.3–4 was re-read: large broadleaf cavities are the normal natural nest; incubation is 35–38 days; usual fledging is 48–60 days; Photo 2 identifies a nestling at about 50 days. The juvenile remains a fully supported late nestling with soft short down-veiled wings and short/hidden tail; exact flight ability is unknown. Hayashi 1997 supports Hokkaido riparian dependence and prolonged natal-area use, not plumage detail.
