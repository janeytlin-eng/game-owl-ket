## Problem → evidence → edit → re-check

1. **Problem found in initial output:** recent fledgling's folded wing carried repeated pale adult-like spots and an overly hard layered-primary panel.
2. **Evidence boundary:** Khoury, Alshamlih & Dheib (2021), printed pp.286–287, directly describe two recent fledglings with little ear tufts, a rather short tail, and fine breast/belly cross-barring without streaks. No exact age or full wing-feather formula is supplied.
3. **Edit applied:** changed only the center juvenile's folded wing/tail edge; removed repeated pale spots and hard adult-like layers; made the wing shorter, rounder, softly fresh and down-obscured with irregular narrow sandy-grey transverse bars. Adults, typography, head, gape, feet, support, cavity, palms and sunset were held fixed.
4. **Actual re-check:** full plate plus both crops opened. The edited juvenile now reads as a supported recent fledgling rather than a small adult; short-tail impression and open-beak begging action remain. No unsupported flight claim was introduced.

## Iteration rule applied

For rare or sparsely documented owl juveniles, do not fill an evidence gap with a generic mini-adult wing. Anchor the image to the best directly observed stage, depict only reported structural signals, soften or hide wing detail that the source does not resolve, and keep action within observed behaviour. This rule was applied and visually re-checked here; its broader effectiveness remains to be tested on other species.
