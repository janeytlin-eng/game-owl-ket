# 155 · Rufous Fishing Owl · *Scotopelia ussheri*

## Taxonomy
Current scientific name: *Scotopelia ussheri* Sharpe, 1871. Monotypic in the sources read for this cycle. English names include Rufous Fishing Owl, Rufous-backed Fishing-owl, and Ussher's Fishing Owl.

## Evidence read on 2026-10-22
- RFO-BIRDLIFE-2026 — BirdLife International species factsheet: endemic to Upper Guinea forests; associated with forest rivers. https://datazone.birdlife.org/species/factsheet/rufous-fishing-owl-scotopelia-ussheri
- RFO-FWS-TAX-2026 — U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service taxonomy profile confirming current name. https://www.fws.gov/species/rufous-fishing-owl-scotopelia-ussheri
- RFO-WABC-MONTICELLI-2015 — Monticelli, Koroma & Bannah, “Observations of Rufous Fishing Owl Scotopelia ussheri in Sierra Leone,” Bulletin of the African Bird Club 22(2):183–189. Twelve documented visual encounters since 1989; used for Upper Guinea river-forest context, not juvenile feather geometry.
- RFO-ABC-JUV-2022 — “Juvenile Rufous Fishing Owl Scotopelia ussheri in Guinea.” Photographs of a juvenile captured in south-east Guinea in October 1993; supports using a photographed older juvenile rather than inventing a neonatal stage. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359798544_Juvenile_Rufous_Fishing_Owl_Scotopelia_ussheri_in_Guinea
- RFO-OWLPAGES-JUV-2003 — photographed juvenile from Duékoué, Côte d’Ivoire, December 2003; supports formed juvenile plumage and tree-perching ability, but gives no age or sex. https://www.owlpages.com/owls/species.php?i=377

## Design decisions for v1
Adults: no ear tufts; dark brown eyes; pale cinnamon face; rufous upperparts with cream/white mottling; pale underparts with fine dark shaft streaks; barred flight feathers. Sexes shown near-equal, female only subtly fuller because no quantitative dimorphism was verified.

Juvenile: conservative older photographed stage, smaller than adults but already bearing formed, adult-like rufous/cream wing markings; fluffier head/body, shorter tail, two-foot branch support. No neonatal details inferred.

Habitat: humid Upper Guinea lowland forest riverbank. This is an evidence-bounded concept, not an exact locality reconstruction.

## Review
The first generated image was actually inspected. It contains exactly three labeled birds, dark eyes, no ear tufts, credible branch support, and an older juvenile with formed but shorter wing/tail. No immediate edit was required. Exact juvenile age, sex-size ratio, subspecific variation (none recognized in read sources), and fine feather geometry remain unknown.
