## Conflicts and design decisions

- v1 used a saturated orange sunset. Actual review showed that this obscures the species' greyish identity and makes both adults read too warm. v2 uses neutral open-shade daylight; this is a presentation correction, not a new plumage claim.
- Barlow's species account says brown iris, while the MNHN male's field label records olive. v2 uses deep brown with a subdued olive cast and does not encode a sex difference.
- The 35–43-day young is not a generic downy chick: Barlow et al. explicitly describe it as fully feathered and greyish like the parents among exposed roots. Formed folded wings are retained, but texture and contour stay softer/rounder than adults.
- No robust quantitative sex-size ratio was found. Adults remain near equal; female only subtly fuller. Exact within-range feather chronology, flight ability on departure day, universal young soft parts and population-wide colour variation remain unknown. scientifically_verified=false.
