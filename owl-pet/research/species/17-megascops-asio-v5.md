## Evidence and design decisions

Mikkola printed pp.207–209 / uploaded PDF pp.209–211 supplies the adult diagnostic pattern, feathered tarsi, gray/red morph context, female mass direction, and the very finely barred gray mesoptile direction. Cornell Life History and ID supply the late brancher action boundary: approximately 26–30 d young may leave the cavity for nearby branches before competent flight and climb with feet plus wing flutter. The USDA FEIS synthesis places initial flight-feather development around the same late-nestling window. The USGS primary article confirms a studied 22–28 d age band but is not used for plumage inference.

The v5 juvenile is therefore an approximately 27–30 d, fully supported brancher with short incomplete wings, short concealed tail, fine gray barring, talon climbing and one balancing flutter. It is not a white hatchling and not an airborne miniature adult. Both adults use the northern gray morph; the female is subtly fuller, not taller.
