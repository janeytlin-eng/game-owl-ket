# #148 *Ninox rumseyi* v2 evidence and design boundary

- Current name: *Ninox rumseyi* Rasmussen et al., 2012; Cebu Boobook / Cebu Hawk Owl.
- Historical mapping: formerly the Cebu population of *Ninox philippensis spilonota* within the broad Philippine Hawk Owl complex.
- Production intent: preserve v1 and make a v2 science revision; this remains one unique species in the 251-species roster.

## Evidence actually read

### CEB-RASMUSSEN-2012-FORKTAIL

Rasmussen et al. 2012, *Forktail* 28:1–20; full PDF read 2026-11-01 at https://www.asianscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/Vocal-divergence-and-new-species.pdf . The *N. rumseyi* diagnosis/type account is printed pp.16–17. The diagnosis uses the female holotype and photographs of at least nine living birds. Adults are medium-large, long-tarsed and relatively small-billed, with rounded heads, compact sleek plumage, short whitish supercilia, dark ear-coverts and a prominent white throat bordered by dark. Crown marks form pale speckled rows between dark bars; upperparts are strongly and narrowly barred with mostly white dark-edged scapular spots. Underparts vary from pinkish-buff to fulvous and may be weakly/irregularly marked or broadly white-streaked. Irides are pale to bright lemon-yellow; bill/cere dull pale olive with yellower tip; feet bright yellow, tarsi about half feathered and claws mostly black with paler bases. The source does not establish a male/female visual size ratio or a juvenile series.

### CEB-JAKOSALEM-COLLAR-GILL-2012

Jakosalem, Collar & Gill, DOI 10.1017/S0959270912000317; full HTML methods/results read 2026-11-01 at https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bird-conservation-international/article/habitat-selection-and-conservation-status-of-the-endemic-ninox-hawkowl-on-cebu-philippines/632843592EFE90CC32B1279D61BE1BFE . Five adult males, four adult females and one field-classified juvenile were radio-tracked for only 3–5 days; male/female home-range estimates did not differ significantly. The juvenile occupied forest patches and adjacent farmland, but the paper does not describe its plumage or state a flight threshold. Roosting and foraging sites combined tall trees, understorey and abundant branches. Two Alcoy nests below 700 m were natural trunk cavities about 10×20 cm and 2–2.5 m above ground, near farmland clearings; one live *Melanolepis* held two eggs in 2011, while a dead *Vitex* cavity had held a recently hatched young in 2005. The text does not depict that hatchling, so no hatchling anatomy is inferred.

### CEB-COMMONS-JARVIS-2024

Forest Jarvis adult field photograph, 15 August 2024, CC BY 4.0: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cebu_Boobook.jpg . Metadata and displayed pixels were read. It supports the compact rounded adult, short white brow marks, dark facial sides, bright-yellow irides, white throat, warm underparts, dark barred upperparts and natural branch grip. It is an unsexed individual and does not support scale, sex ratio or development.

## Design inference and unknowns

- Adults stay near equal visible size: neither the female holotype nor unsexed field photos establish a fixed female-larger direction.
- Correct v1's long continuous V-shaped white brows to short separate marks; strengthen narrow upperpart barring; reduce the repetitive broad underpart rows; shift the bill/cere toward dull pale olive and preserve mostly dark claws with paler bases.
- Use an age-unknown older juvenile on a fully supporting branch, with eyes closed, compact soft feathering and only a short formed wing/tail mostly hidden by fluff. Field telemetry confirms a juvenile was mobile in habitat but does not justify a precise age, iris colour, flight claim or adult-like remiges.
- A natural cavity is documented, but this triptych uses a branch/leafy edge context so the design does not invent cavity contents or hatchling details.
- Scientific verification remains false. Image outcome may pass only with limitations after actual pixel review.
