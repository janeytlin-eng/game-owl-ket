## New primary source actually discovered and read

### NBBOO-GURNEY-1882-IBIS

Gurney, J. H. 1882, “Notes on the Raptorial Birds collected in New Britain,” *Ibis* ser. 4, vol. 6, pp. 126–133; species account pp. 131–133 and measurement table p. 133. BHL page: https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/8609831 ; full OCR: https://archive.org/download/ibis36brit/ibis36brit_djvu.txt ; accessed 2026-11-12.

The original text states that Richards supplied two males and one female, all labelled New Britain, July 1879, with yellow iris, drab feet and ash bill. The three-row table gives the two male wings as 7.40 in and the female as 7.60 in; tarsus 1.30/1.40/1.40 in and middle toe s.u. 0.90/1.10/1.10 in. OCR damages the sex symbols and prints the last wing value as “7-GO”; the row assignment is resolved against the paragraph’s explicit two-males/one-female statement.

**Boundary:** this tiny historic series supports only a very subtle female-longer linear direction (about 2.7% in wing), not a mass difference, broad plumage dimorphism or universal female-larger rule.

## Reused and reread evidence

- **NBBOO-GOULD-SHARPE-1884-TEXT:** sexed male adult description; pale chocolate upperparts, narrow pale crown bars, separate white brows, chocolate ear-coverts, white throat, irregular scapular/greater-covert white marks, predominantly pale underbody with narrow brown streaks. No juvenile/female comparison.
- **NBBOO-IUCN-2018:** 22 cm small owl, fine-spotted chocolate upperparts, pale mottled underparts, white eyebrows/throat, New Britain forest/modified habitats. No juvenile sequence.
- **NBBOO-COMMONS-MALINOWSKI-2019:** unsexed field photo supports compact earless form, warm chocolate/russet range, orange-yellow eyes, pale brows/throat and branch support.
- **NBBOO-ITIS-555491-2026:** current name authority, naming only.

## Design decisions

1. Adult female only about 3% larger/longer-winged than male; do not exaggerate.
2. Keep the adult specimen colours and predominantly pale, narrow-streaked underbody from v3.
3. Juvenile remains age unknown, closed-eyed, down-dominant, short-winged and fully supported because no developmental description was found.
4. Vary posture safely: male scanning left; female front-three-quarter; juvenile low dozing crouch with two-foot support.
5. No nest, cavity, hatchling or flight reconstruction.
