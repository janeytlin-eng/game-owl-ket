### EGO-ABSA-BIH2 — Australian Bird Study Association, *Bird in the Hand* (Second Edition), Eastern Grass Owl, p.1

- Original URL: https://absa.asn.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/Eastern-Grass-Owl.pdf
- Accessed: 2026-10-31
- Type: professional bird-study association banding/ageing sheet, compiled by J. W. Hardy from HANZAB 4 with BirdLife Australia drawings
- Taxonomy on source: *Tyto capensis longimembris*; mapped here to the project/current *Tyto longimembris*.
- Facts actually read: male wing 305–324 mm, tail 106–125 mm, mass 256–390 g; female wing 316–346 mm, tail 109–129 mm, mass 312–478 g. Females are stated larger and darker; male facial disc typically white to patchy cream, female cream-buff to orange-buff and patterned. Tarsus 72.1–82.2 mm, with lower portion/toes only hair-like feathered rather than fully feathered.
- Design implication: retain overlap and avoid a fixed giant height multiplier, but v4 may make the female modestly, visibly fuller/larger and darker than the male; keep the long distal leg visibly bare.
- Limits: the sheet is not a population model and its juvenile ageing cues do not establish the Dunlop chick’s exact feather chronology or flight ability.

## Correction to v3 record

The v3 note said an ABSA morphometrics lead could not be opened. The PDF was actually opened and read in this cycle, so it is now verified evidence. The previous cautious near-equal composition remains defensible because the measurement ranges overlap, but the newly read evidence supports a modest visible female size/fullness direction rather than treating sex size as wholly unknown.

## Design boundary

Use one Australian nominate plate. Keep the adult male paler, the adult female darker and modestly fuller/larger, and the approximately 55-day captive-reference mesoptile low and fully supported. Preserve rich golden-brown down, partial pink face, dark eyes and pale bill; soften any crisp adult-like side wing layering. Do not claim wild-age equivalence, precise flight readiness, universal sex ratio, or a continuous feather sequence.
