## Current and historical names

Current project/checklist name: *Otus pembaensis* Pakenham, 1937. ITIS TSN 686653 and IOC 15.1 treat it as a valid species. Peters-style historical treatment `Otus rutilus pembaensis` is retained as a synonym/crosswalk only; it does not create an additional project species.

## Evidence read this cycle

1. **PESO-MELO-ETAL-2022-P7-S47** — Melo et al., “A new species of scops-owl … from Príncipe Island,” *ZooKeys* 1126:1–54, 2022, DOI 10.3897/zookeys.1126.87635. Printed p.7 Table 1 and Supplementary material 4 (printed p.47) were read. The adult comparison includes seven Pemba museum specimens; available wing lengths are 150–155 mm and tails 73–80 mm. Most comparative specimens were not sexed and the study pooled sexes, so these measurements support compact proportions but not a male/female body-size rule. The supplementary plumage matrix is adult comparative material, not a juvenile sequence.
2. **PESO-VIRANI-2005-P12** — Munir Virani, “Re-evaluating the Status of the Pemba Scops Owl,” *The Peregrine Fund Newsletter* 36, 2005, p.12. Direct week-long call-response survey and adult observation. The photographed/observed adult is russet-brown and compact; surveys found the owl mainly in the indigenous Ngezi and Msitu Mkuu forests, with lower densities in old-growth clove forest. This is habitat/adult evidence only.
3. **PESO-NAHONYO-ETAL-2005-P54-55** — Nahonyo et al., *Ngezi–Vumawimbi Forest Reserves Biodiversity Inventory Report*, University of Dar es Salaam / Zanzibar forestry authorities, July 2005, printed pp.54–55. Calls were detected from mid-canopy; the account lists dense forest, clove plantation, mangrove and graveyard forest patches. It provides habitat and perch-height context, not nests, sex or juvenile development.
4. **PESO-ITIS-686653-2026** — ITIS TSN 686653, read 2026-11-01. Valid species *Otus pembaensis*; ITIS references Clements 2021, HBW/BirdLife 2022 and IOC 15.1. Taxonomy only.
5. **PESO-IUCN-2025** — BirdLife International 2025, *Otus pembaensis*, IUCN Red List e.T22688679A208663317, assessment date 2024-09-06. Endemic resident of Tanzania/Pemba; forest and artificial-terrestrial habitat; 2,500–3,000 mature individuals, decreasing. Population/habitat only; no morphology, nest or development claim.
6. **PESO-MIKKOLA-2013-P135** — reused from the v1 repository record, printed p.135 / uploaded-PDF p.137. The user-uploaded guide file was not accessible this cycle, so no new page claim is added. Existing page-backed facts retained: very small owl, short tufts, two adult colour morphs, yellow adult eyes, pale scapular line; male folded wing slightly longer; described mesoptile whitish with vague yellow-brown barring, unstreaked white-barred crown and somewhat banded tail. Downy chick undescribed.

## Evidence boundary and conflicts

- Adult morphology can be calibrated from Mikkola plus museum measurements; Melo et al. do **not** provide sex-specific body-size evidence because most comparative specimens were unsexed.
- Use near-equal adult display height and bulk. A subtly longer male folded wing is the only sex direction retained from Mikkola; do not turn it into a larger male body.
- The only young stage with descriptive support is the age-unknown mesoptile. Exact age, flight threshold, juvenile iris/cere/toe chronology, continuous feather sequence and living soft-part colours remain unknown.
- No source read here establishes an occupied nest, cavity geometry, eggs or clutch. Dense mid-canopy habitat does not license a nest depiction.
- Mikkola's downy chick remains explicitly undescribed; do not substitute a generic white hatchling.

## Problem → evidence → correction plan

V1 was generated at full resolution but used the older prompt before the new museum, survey and checklist cross-check. V2 will preserve the mesoptile boundary, strengthen the compact adult proportions and dense mid-canopy Pemba setting, keep the adults near-equal, and prevent a regular adult-hard juvenile wing/tail. The likely recurring model failure is tall dramatic ear tufts and an adult-layered juvenile wing; these require original-detail crop review and, if present, a targeted edit.
