# #103 Eastern Grass Owl — *Tyto longimembris* — v3 research

- Accessed: 2026-10-29
- Direction: nominate Australian concept
- Target: adult male, approximately 55-day captive-reference mesoptile, adult female
- Action: staged science revision of v2; v1–v2 retained
- Scientific status: false

## Evidence

Dunlop & Pain 2016 JZAR pp.169–172/Table 3/Fig.1 remains the stage anchor: the photographed approximately 55-day captive female is rich golden-brown and powder-puff fluffy, with partial pink facial skin, dark eyes and a pale bill. The 69-day bird is a different individual/stage. NSW government lines 198–215 support white-below male, buffier larger female, very long mostly bare legs, tall-grass/sedge wetland, daytime forms and ground nests in trodden grass reached through vegetation tunnels. Mikkola printed pp.102–103 supports adult plumage and the long partly bare legs.

## Limits

The captive photograph does not provide a continuous wing-feather map or exact wild-age equivalence. Exact flight readiness, body geometry and universal sex ratio remain unknown. A search-result lead to an ABSA morphometrics PDF could not be opened and is not treated as verified evidence.

## Design

Keep the juvenile low and fully supported, golden-brown and fluffy, with partial pink face and a wing contour mostly concealed by down. Adults retain species-supported sex-colour direction; female is only modestly fuller at near-equal display height.
