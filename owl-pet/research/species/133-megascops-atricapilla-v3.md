## Sources actually read

### BCSO-DANTAS-2021
- Sidnei M. Dantas et al., 2021, “Multi-character taxonomic review, systematics, and biogeography of the Black-capped/Tawny-bellied Screech Owl (*Megascops atricapilla–M. watsonii*) complex”, *Zootaxa* 4949(3):401–444.
- DOI / URL: https://doi.org/10.11646/zootaxa.4949.3.1
- Read: full article; especially p.429 (printed page; PDF p.29), species account for *M. atricapilla*.
- Facts used: current treatment *M. atricapilla*; historical combinations include *Strix atricapillus* and *Otus atricapillus*; Atlantic Forest distribution; strong geographic and plumage variation; a north–south body-size cline is discussed, but the study does not supply a universal sex-based standing-height multiplier.
- Limitation: taxonomic/morphometric treatment, not a juvenile development series.

### BCSO-WIKIAVES-WA2019052
- Celso B. Almeida, photograph WA2019052, WikiAves; photographed 2016-02-07 in Florianópolis, Santa Catarina, published 2016-02-12.
- URL: https://www.wikiaves.com.br/2019052%26t%3Ds%26s%3D10516%26tag%3DFOTOJOVEM
- Read: record metadata and displayed image.
- Facts used: record explicitly tagged “Jovem”; yellow iris; tiny ear tufts; soft/less-defined head and face; formed folded wings and tail; upright, two-foot branch support.
- Limitation: no exact age, sex, fledging status, or flight ability; one photograph cannot establish the complete plumage sequence. Image is referenced, not copied.

### BCSO-MENQ-ANJOS-2015
- Willian Menq & Luiz dos Anjos, 2015, “Habitat selection by owls in a seasonal semi-deciduous forest in southern Brazil”, *Brazilian Journal of Biology* 75(4 Suppl.1):143–149.
- DOI / URL: https://doi.org/10.1590/1519-6984.07614
- Read: abstract, methods/results context, and species/vegetation associations.
- Facts used: *M. atricapilla* occurred in the Perobas Biological Reserve study and was associated with structurally mature forest variables including taller trees and woody structure. This supports a closed Atlantic-Forest visual context.
- Limitation: habitat association is not evidence for a particular nest cavity, nest material, or juvenile age.

### BCSO-AVESRAPINA-2018
- Aves de Rapina Brasil, 2018, “Corujinha-sapo (*Megascops atricapilla*)”.
- URL: https://www.avesderapinabrasil.com/megascops_atricapilla.htm
- Read: morphology, habitat, and breeding account.
- Facts used as secondary cross-check: 22–24 cm, 115–160 g; female described as heavier; three color morphs; variable adult iris (yellow to dark brown); prominent tufts, dark cap, feathered tarsi; Atlantic-Forest and dense-understory context.
- Limitation: secondary account; the qualitative female-heavier statement is not converted into a fixed visual height ratio. Nest statements are not used in this branch-perch composition.

## Full image-generation prompt

Scientific-educational natural-history concept plate, 3:2 landscape, matching the established owl-pet series: photorealistic but gently stylized field-guide presentation, warm controlled natural light, high feather detail, restrained depth of field, Atlantic Forest moss and epiphytes, no fantasy traits.

Create exactly THREE Black-capped Screech-Owls (Megascops atricapilla) on one continuous naturally supported mossy branch, full bodies visible and separated, left-to-right:
1) ADULT MALE — alert three-quarter pose facing slightly inward, compact screech-owl proportions, strong but moderate ear tufts, nearly black/dark-brown crown cap, dark-edged pale brown facial disc, brown to dark-amber iris, dull greenish-horn bill, brown morph upperparts with pale scapular edging, fine shaft streaks and cross-bars, underparts cream-brown with narrow dark streaking, feathered tarsi, both feet gripping the branch.
2) ADULT FEMALE — a distinct relaxed side-three-quarter pose, near-equal crown height to the male and only subtly fuller/heavier in body, never shorter or obviously smaller, same species characters and brown morph, both feet visibly and naturally gripping the branch. Do not make the adults mirrored clones.
3) JUVENILE — age-unknown later juvenile based on a documented field photograph: bright yellow iris, tiny developing ear tufts, rounder softer/downier head and facial feathers, compact body, formed folded wings and a short visible tail with softer, less regular juvenile barring and spotting; both feet gripping the branch. It is not a hatchling, not in natal-down-only plumage, and not a perfect miniature adult. Do not imply exact age, fledging status, or flight ability.

Adult male and female must read as the same species and near equal standing height; the female may look subtly broader/heavier only. Juvenile wing feathers may be formed because the direct photo shows them, but keep the layering softer and less adult-regular, not long hard white-edged tiles reaching the feet.
