## Fresh evidence actually read

### Confirmed Sumatran nest and adult field diagnosis

Gooddie 2015, *Forktail* 31:70–81, printed pp.74–75 / PDF text lines 616–639, reports the first documented Rajah Scops Owl nest at Danau Ranau, Sumatra on 27 April 2009. The photographed incubating adult was identified by a blackish crown contrasting with a rufous face, white supercilia and white ear tufts, plus a pale yellow-orange iris. The natural cavity was 2.5 m above ground in the main trunk of a 5 m old tree at c.1,300 m; the opening was c.50 cm and the cavity was lined with soft mossy material. Two eggs were present, but v2 does not show eggs because the juvenile stage comes from a different observation and must not be composited as one breeding moment.

### Museum series and design correction

Marshall 1978, *Ornithological Monographs* 25, printed p.20 / PDF text lines 1397–1420, examined Bornean, Javan and Sumatran specimens. For *O. brookii* he describes exceptionally soft plumage, sparse enlarged black pattern, deep fuscous upperparts, nearly immaculate whitish inner ear-tuft webs, a pale hindneck collar, yellow iris, and heavy tarsal feathering extending onto the basal third/fourth toes. Critically, individual colour and size variation in the few specimens prevented him from verifying the Sumatran subspecies morphologically. Therefore the three-collar/broad-streak *solokensis* direction from Mikkola remains useful but must not be treated as universally diagnostic or exaggerated into clean white bands.

### Nomenclature and original description

Hartert 1893, BBOC 1:39–40, introduced *Pisorhina solokensis* from the Solok Mountains; the original page search text begins “Top of head and neck deep brown, nearly blackish.” ITIS TSN 686780 treats *O. brookii solokensis* as a valid subspecies and TSN 1063383 maps the original combination as invalid/currently synonymized. Avibase independently maps the type locality to the Solok/Padang Highlands and recognizes the same subspecies concept.

### Juvenile evidence retained with boundary

The repository’s Mikkola p.168 excerpt and 2014 Sumatra field photograph metadata support an older, branch-perched Sumatran mesoptile: reddish-brown with fine dark cross-bars, orange-yellow eyes, small tufts, and formed but still soft wing/tail. Exact age, sex and flight ability are not reported. The widely circulated Commons “juvenile” photo from Gunung Gede, Java is not used for *solokensis* because both location and identification are problematic for the Sumatran subspecies.
