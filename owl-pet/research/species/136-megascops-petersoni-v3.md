## Evidence actually read

### CIN-FITZPATRICK-ONEILL-1986
John W. Fitzpatrick & John P. O’Neill, “*Otus petersoni*, a new screech-owl from the eastern Andes, with systematic notes on *O. colombianus* and *O. ingens*,” *Wilson Bulletin* 98(1), 1986, pp.1–14. Full original PDF: https://www.biologicaldiversity.org/campaigns/global_owl_project/descriptions/Megascops/Megascops%20petersoni%20%28Fitzpatrick%20%26%20O%27Neill%29%201986%2C%20Wilson%20Bulletin%2C%2098%2C%20pages%201-14..pdf (accessed 2026-10-31).

Actually read pp.3–6 and the species-comparison section. Diagnosis: small owl; warm buffy-brown throughout; medium tufts; narrow buff nuchal collar; nearly fully feathered tarsi; dark-brown irides; distal tarsus sparsely feathered almost to toes; complete absence of white body/wing feathering, palest colour rich warm buff. Holotype had rich cinnamon underparts, sparse dark shaft streaks and only one to three thin wavy bars distally; bill pale grey-green and toes pale pinkish flesh. Variation spans brown/red but retains rich cinnamon background. Sexual dimorphism, if present, is slight.

One male specimen carried numerous downy feathers paler and more heavily barred than other specimens, interpreted as completing first prebasic body molt; it was nearly in adult plumage on 28 July. No exact age, sex-size formula or flight sequence is given.

**Design boundary:** adults near-equal and not clones; no white markings; a first-year nearly-adult bird may show residual paler heavily barred down mixed with formed adult-like feathers, but exact age/flight ability and literal wing geometry remain unknown.

### CIN-ALARCON-SHANEE-2022
Alejandro Alarcón & Sam Shanee, “Nido de la Lechuza Canela *Megascops petersoni* (Strigidae) en Amazonas, Perú,” *Revista Ecuatoriana de Ornitología* 8, 2022, pp.6–10, DOI https://doi.org/10.18272/reo.v8i1.1791 ; full PDF https://revistas.usfq.edu.ec/index.php/reo/en/article/download/1791/2924/18217 (accessed 2026-10-31).

Actually read pp.6–9. The observed adult used a natural cavity 5 m high in a live *Alchornea* tree; one off-white egg rested on dry *Chusquea* bamboo leaves. The surrounding 1,970-m montane forest had dense understory, high humidity, abundant moss and epiphytes, with small-scale selective logging and nearby pasture. The paper documents an adult/egg, not a chick or first-year feather sequence.

**Design boundary:** moss/epiphyte-rich montane forest is evidence-supported. Because the selected young is a later first-year bird and the source has no chick anatomy, v3 uses a branch and does not invent a family nest, egg or hatchling.
