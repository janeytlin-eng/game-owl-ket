# #163 Burrowing Owl — *Athene cunicularia*

## Evidence facts
- Small, tuftless, long-legged ground owl; yellow eyes, gray-olive bill/cere, prominent white eyebrows and pale face.
- Western North American *A. c. hypugaea* adults are brown/sandy with pale spots, barred pale underparts and long sparsely feathered tarsi with bristled toes; they use open prairie/grassland and mammal burrows.
- Juvenile aging-key photographs provide a regional *hypugaea* series. A 29-day bird is still safely prefledging under both the 41–47-day and 44–53-day reported ranges.
- Mesoptile evidence: softer/downier than adult, unspotted crown, more diffuse underparts, buff breast sides and whitish face with dusky zones outside the eyes.
- Sex-size evidence conflicts: Mikkola reports larger females, Cornell states sexes are the same size.

## Design decision
Use western North American shortgrass prairie and a natural mammal-burrow entrance. Show adults near-equal in displayed height/mass, with no numeric dimorphism. Center a **29-DAY PREFLEDGING JUVENILE** at the burrow entrance, belly and both feet fully supported, soft/downy with short rounded developing wing/tail and no flight pose.

## Unknowns and conflicts
Exact individual flight threshold, universal fledging timing, sex-size ratio, detailed day-29 soft-part color, toe-feather extent and variation beyond *hypugaea*. Mikkola/Cornell size conflict remains unresolved; no universal claim.
