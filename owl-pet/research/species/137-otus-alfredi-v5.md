### FLO-WIDODO-1999
Widodo, Cox & Rasmussen, 1999, “Rediscovery of the Flores Scops Owl *Otus alfredi*…”, *Forktail* 15:15–23. PDF https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c1a9e03f407b482a158da87/t/5c1fc23b2b6a282c991de675/1545585212532/Widodo-Flores.pdf . Read printed pp.15–20, Tables 1–2 and Fig.2. Supports small size; short broad rounded rufous tufts; long rufous bristles; orange-yellow bill; bright rufous un-rimmed face; almost unstreaked/unbanded adult pattern; localized patterned white areas; heavily feathered tarsi with distal third bare. Historical adult sex assignments were uncertain. The one juvenile male (MZB 30.086) was mist-netted 15 May 1994 at 1,400 m in relatively undisturbed wet montane forest; 185 mm total, wing 137 mm, tail 69 mm; retains paler nearly uniform rufous natal down and clearer tail banding. Exact age, iris chronology and flight ability are not reported.

### FLO-HUTCHINSON-2007
Hutchinson et al., 2007, “Observations of Flores Scops Owl…”, *Forktail* 23:184–187. PDF https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c1a9e03f407b482a158da87/t/5c20f94f4d7a9c6cf87abdf2/1545664849106/Hutchinson-FloresScops.pdf . Read printed pp.184–186. Field-observed adults were small/compact, short-rounded-tufted, solid rufous-faced with distinct white brows but no black facial border, yellow-eyed/legged/footed, finely vermiculated below without black streaking, with white scapular marks and small well-spaced pale primary notches. Records were centred on intact primary montane forest; remnant roadside trees were used where adjacent to primary forest. No juvenile sequence or sex-size rule.

## Visual-review correction addendum — 2026-11-12

The primary Widodo et al. paper was re-opened during image review. The accepted edit preserves the documented adult pink/reddish orbital skin but removes the age-unknown juvenile's invented coloured orbital ring and retains dark/non-diagnostic juvenile eyes.
