## Sources actually read

- `MAG-ZAMBRANO-2022`: Zambrano, Revista Chilena de Ornitología 28(1):15–27, printed pp.20–21, Fig.4 B–D (PDF pp.6–7). Two chicks were estimated at approximately one week, still had egg teeth and open eyelids. Automated-camera records showed them remaining until flight feathers developed and departing at approximately 50–56 days.
- `MAG-MIK2013-266`: uploaded Mikkola guide, printed pp.266–267/PDF268–269, reused for adult narrow pointed tufts, yellow eyes, blue-gray small bill, white throat with dark dotted border, fine belly bars and dirty-white feathered toes.
- `S099-ORNNEO-2020-URBAN-NEST`: Novoa & Blanco 2020, pp.31–33, reused for the exceptional Santiago balcony case and approximately 20-day comparison stage.
- `S099-BIRDNET-TAXONOMY`: reused for current name, broad distribution and open/rocky habitat range.

## Design inference and unknowns

The center chick is bounded to the approximately one-week urban-record stage: open eyelids, a small egg tooth, down-covered wing buds and no mature wing/tail. The balcony and dry plant layer reproduce a documented case and are explicitly not treated as the universal nest type. Adults remain near equal display height; no fixed female-size multiplier is inferred. Exact day, chick sex, first-flight threshold, continuous feather sequence, adult sex ratio and universal nest choice remain unknown.

## Review and self-iteration

The first render correctly separated the early chick from a fledgling, but adult toes were too bare for the Mikkola description. One targeted edit extended dirty-white feathering along adult toes nearly to the claws. The edited full image and 560×650 chick crop were reopened at original detail. Chick anatomy, support, title/labels, adult identity, near-equal adult scale and toe-feather correction passed with limitations. The rule applied here is evidence-specific: toe feathering must be checked against the species account rather than generalized across owls.
