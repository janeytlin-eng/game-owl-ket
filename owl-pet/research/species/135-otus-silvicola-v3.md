## Evidence actually read

### WSC-MEES-2006
G. F. Mees, “The avifauna of Flores (Lesser Sunda Islands),” *Zoologische Mededelingen* 80, 2006, pp.113–115, especially p.114. Original PDF: https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/209703/ZM80-05.pdf (accessed 2026-10-31).

The species account states that the available series included only two adults, one of each sex, while other specimens were fledglings with remiges and rectrices basally still in sheath. The type was fully feathered but transitional: loose, finely barred juvenile feathering on head, neck, upper back and breast, with some adult dark-streaked feathers emerging on back, breast and abdomen. Mees found no obvious adult sex-plumage difference and reported a surprisingly large female, about 12% larger from the limited sample.

**Boundary:** use a fully feathered transitional immature rather than invented natal down; permit a moderate female-larger direction, but render only about 8–10% visual difference and state the n=2 adult limitation. Do not claim a universal standing-height formula.

### WSC-MARSHALL-1978
Joe T. Marshall, *Systematics of Smaller Asian Night Birds Based on Voice*, AOU Ornithological Monographs 25, 1978, printed p.26. Original PDF: https://sora.unm.edu/sites/default/files/journals/om/om025.pdf (accessed 2026-10-31).

Marshall diagnoses the large Flores/Sumbawa owl by long ear tufts without a white inner border, absence of a white collar, sparse pattern, white underparts bearing definite crossbars on bold sparse ventral streaks, and dense tarsal/proximal-phalange feathering. Recorded iris was dull orange. Habitat observations include forest ravines, high concealed perches, lowland cultivation edges and virgin rainforest to about 1,600 m.

**Boundary:** no bright-yellow iris, white tuft border or white collar; support both feet on a mossy branch in a Flores forest ravine. Habitat evidence does not authorize an invented nest.

### SLSO-SANGSTER-2013
George Sangster et al., “A New Owl Species of the Genus *Otus* ... from Lombok,” *PLOS ONE* 8(2), 2013, PMC full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3576389/ (accessed 2026-10-31).

The diagnostic comparison describes *O. silvicola* as much larger than the Lombok taxon, with cold greyish-brown/brownish-grey upperparts, broader dark shaft streaks/bars, a pale greyish-cinnamon to pale buffy-grey breast only slightly darker than belly sides, white belly centre, and feathering covering the base of the toes.

**Boundary:** preserve large-bodied identity, cold upperparts and toe-base feathering; do not over-pattern the immature or import Lombok features.
