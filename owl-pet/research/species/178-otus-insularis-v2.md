## Fresh evidence read this cycle

### Sex size and adult cues

Currie et al. 2002, Ringing & Migration 21:16–18, printed pp.16–18, measured 31 adults and sexed 30 molecularly. Table 1 reports means: male/female wing 169.12/174.42 mm, tarsus 37.84/38.45 mm, mass 132.43/154.63 g; wing and mass differences were significant (p<0.01), but all measurements overlapped. Plumage lightness and eye-colour variation were not sex-linked and no discrete colour morphs were found. Therefore v2 depicts a distinctly fuller/heavier female, but only slightly taller/longer-winged—not an exaggerated giant female and not a plumage morph difference. Both sexes have small/near-invisible tufts and fully unfeathered tarsi/feet.

### Young-bird stage resolved

Currie et al. 2004, Bird Conservation International 14:123–137, provides a usable dated stage:

- recent fledgling c.8–12 weeks: fluffy appearance, barred plumage, clumsy flier and “whsst” call (printed pp.125–129, especially Table 1 note and fledgling section);
- mature fledgling c.16–20 weeks: confident flier and adult-like appearance;
- two tracked fledglings left cavities before they could fly, perched 3–5 m above the cavity several days later, and one flew clumsily one month after leaving (p.129);
- fledglings remained and were provisioned on natal territories for at least 3–4 months.

V2 selects the **recent fledgling c.8–12 weeks**, not the mature stage. It may have developed juvenile wings sufficient for clumsy flight, but must retain fluffy head/body and distinctive chest barring, shorter/softer wings and tail than the adults, and full bilateral branch support. Do not show airborne action.

### Nest and habitat boundary

The 2004 study found eight tree-cavity nests 7–25 m high, in narrow hand-sized fissures/splits in *Dillenia ferruginea* and *Pterocarpus indicus*, containing one egg or chick. A restrained natural trunk fissure may appear as context behind the recent fledgling; do not invent lining or put the fledgling inside a nursery pose. Currie et al. 2002 study habitat was mature secondary/mist forest in Morne Seychellois National Park, 150–700 m, with remnant native forest. This supports misty Mahé upland forest and granite/moss context.

### Soft parts

The 2002 adult sample recorded bright yellow, dull green-yellow and orange-red eyes in both sexes; the authors found no age relationship, and some fledglings had bright yellow eyes. V2 uses a muted/dull green-yellow recent-fledgling iris as an evidence-permitted but non-universal choice. Bill/foot details remain bounded by Mikkola printed p.138/PDF p.140 and v1: greyish-yellow bill with dark tip; fully bare tarsi/feet and long toes.

### Taxonomy

ITIS TSN 202272, accessed 2026-11-01, treats *Otus insularis* as valid and records *Gymnoscops insularis* Tristram, 1880 as synonym/protonym; IOC 15.1 is cited as a current source.
