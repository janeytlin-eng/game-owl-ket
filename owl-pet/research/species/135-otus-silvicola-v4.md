## Evidence actually read

### WSC-MEES-2006 (reused, directly reread)
G. F. Mees, “The avifauna of Flores (Lesser Sunda Islands),” *Zoologische Mededelingen* 80 (2006), printed pp.113–115, especially p.114. https://repository.naturalis.nl/document/41318

Lines 8754–8799 say that only two available birds were adults, one of each sex, while other birds were fledglings with remiges and rectrices basally still in sheath. The type was fully feathered and transitional: loose fine barring on head, neck, upper back and most breast, with a few adult dark-streaked feathers emerging on back, breast and abdomen. Lines 8801–8803 report no obvious adult sex-plumage difference. Because the apparent female-size direction rests on one adult of each sex, v4 does not encode a fixed visual sex ratio.

### WSC-MIK2013-183 — newly indexed direct guide read
Heimo Mikkola, *Owls of the World: A Photographic Guide*, 2nd ed. (2013), printed p.183 / searchable PDF text around lines 2972–2977. The account states “No data on sexual size differences”; adults have long ear tufts without white inner borders, no pale collar, cold light grey-brown upperparts, black herringbone shaft streaks, sparse bold ventral streaks/cross-bars, dull orange-yellow eyes, greyish-horn bill/cere, and dense tarsal/toe-base feathering. Juveniles are paler/fluffier, more reddish-brown, with inconspicuous pattern. Secondary specialist synthesis; it does not establish age, flight ability or exact sheath geometry.

### WSC-MARSHALL-1978 and SLSO-SANGSTER-2013 (reused)
Marshall printed p.26 supports long tufts without a white inner border, no white collar, dull orange iris, sparse ventral pattern and dense tarsal/proximal-phalange feathering. Sangster et al. supports the large cold-grey-brown identity, low breast/belly contrast, white belly centre and toe-base feathering.

## Conflict and v4 decision

Mees’s two-adult sample suggests a larger female, while Mikkola explicitly says there are no data on sexual size differences. V4 treats this as unresolved: adults share plumage, differ in pose, and remain at approximately comparable apparent scale. This avoids turning a one-pair observation into a universal rule. The young is a fully feathered transitional immature, not a natal-down chick and not a miniature adult.

## Unknowns

Exact immature age, sex, iris chronology, locomotor/flight ability, continuous feather sequence and literal sheath visibility remain unknown. Scientific verification remains **false** pending expert review.
