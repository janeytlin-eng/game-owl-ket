## Taxonomy and names

- Current valid species: Ninox natalis Lister, 1889 (ITIS TSN 686701; IOC source cited by ITIS).
- Historical combination: Ninox squamipila natalis. Norman et al. (1998) found the three sampled members of the former N. squamipila complex genetically separated at species-level distances; the 2004 recovery plan treats N. natalis as a full species.
- Earlier historical name visible in literature/plates: Ninox forbesi natalis. It is an old combination/usage, not the current roster name.

## Evidence actually read

1. CIB-DCCEEW-HILL-2004 — Hill, R. 2004, Australian Government recovery plan, printed pp.1–3 (PDF pp.5–7; web extraction lines 60–218). Endemic; primary/marginal rainforest, mature forest hollows; all four recorded nests were tree hollows, three marginal rainforest and one primary rainforest, all in Syzygium nervosum. Independent-young ranging data absent. This supports dense rainforest and a branch/hollow context, not an invented nest interior or precise juvenile age.
2. CIB-WAM-JOHNSTONE-2016 — Western Australian Museum information sheet, English p.1 (PDF p.1, lines 11–38). Adult: 26–29 cm, rufous brown, small white spots on upper wing coverts, darker brown barred wings/tail, white lores/short supercilium/chin, yellow eyes, blue-grey bill, pale-yellow legs/feet. Sexes similar; source says female slightly larger. Juvenile is paler and downier/fluffier.
3. CIB-ANDREWS-1900 — C. W. Andrews, A Monograph of Christmas Island, printed pp.47–48 (PDF extraction lines 1193–1204). Adult specimens: sexes do not differ in colour and measured wing ranges overlap (males 7.6–7.8 in; females 7.4–7.8 in); iris and feet bright lemon-yellow. Historical nest report in screw-pine/twigs conflicts with modern four-hollow record and is not used as a definitive nest design.
4. CIB-MONTGOMERY-2006-JUV — Ian Montgomery photo ID 39760, 3 Sep 2006, original 1500×1820, explicitly captioned juvenile on Christmas Island. Actual image opened: orange/rufous face and yellow iris; head and underbody retain loose pale down; wing is formed but short/rounded with irregular small pale marks; the bird is supported by a branch. Exact age, sex, tail length and sustained flight are not given.
5. CIB-NORMAN-1998 — Norman et al., DOI 10.1071/MU98028, publisher abstract actually read. Supports full-species treatment versus former N. squamipila complex; it does not support plumage geometry.
6. CIB-ITIS-2025 — ITIS TSN 686701, current standing valid; IOC v15.1 source acquired 2025. Supports current name only.

## Conflict handling and design inference

- Do not force a large female. WAM's qualitative “slightly larger” is weak and the historical measured wing ranges overlap; v7 uses near-equal displayed height, with only a mild fullness difference that is not presented as a measured ratio.
- Do not paint a generic white chick. The selected subject is the directly photographed older juvenile: pale/down-rich head and body with a formed but shorter, softer wing. Exact day age and sustained-flight competence remain unknown.
- Keep all three naturally supported on one mossy rainforest branch. The recovery plan supports rainforest/tree-hollow ecology, but the plate does not claim the displayed branch is a nest.
- Adult white wing marks must be small spots, not large decorative ovals. Juvenile marks must be fewer, lower-contrast and irregular rather than adult-perfect rows.
- Scientific status remains false: exact feather chronology, sex-size ratio, precise iris/bill ontogeny and action threshold are unresolved.
