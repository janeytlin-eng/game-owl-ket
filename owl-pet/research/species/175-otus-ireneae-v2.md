## Current and historical names

Current project/checklist name: *Otus ireneae*. Historical English names in the repository are Morden’s Scops Owl and Morden’s Owlet. No evidence found for a separate current taxon requiring a new project ID.

## Evidence read this cycle

1. **SSO-VIRANI-1995-P24-27** — Munir Virani, “Who gives a hoot!? The Sokoke Scops Owl and its forest,” *Swara* 18(1), 1995, pp.24–27. Direct field/conservation account. Page 25 includes a photographed family explicitly captioned left-to-right female, male, juvenile. The article supports paired roosting and use of dense lower *Cynometra* canopy. The juvenile is visibly branch-perched with adults, but the account does not provide age, flight status or iris colour.
2. **SSO-VIRANI-2008-P6-9** — Munir Z. Virani, “Diet composition of Sokoke Scops Owl *Otus ireneae* in Arabuko-Sokoke Forest,” *Scopus* 27:6–9. Primary field study. Pages 6–7 describe *Cynometra* woodland, dense lower vegetation, three radio-tracked pairs and roost study; pages 7–8 report insect-heavy diet. Useful for habitat/support, not juvenile plumage, nest geometry or sex size.
3. **SSO-MIKKOLA-2013-P134** — reused from v1/repository record. Adult dimensions 16–18 cm, 45–55 g, no evidence of sexual size difference, three morphs, small tufts, pale-yellow eyes; juvenile “said to resemble adult.” User upload was not accessible this run; online page text was read only to re-check these already-indexed facts.
4. **SSO-ABC-2023-P2-P9-10** — reused v1 field-report evidence for distribution/habitat; not used to invent development.
5. **SSO-IUCN-2025** — current institutional species account read for *Cynometra* habitat and cavity-nesting uncertainty; morphology/development claims were not taken from it.

## Evidence → design boundaries

- Adults: no evidence of sexual size difference; display same height and near-equal bulk. Never apply a generic “female much larger” rule.
- Adult colour: three morphs exist, but morph is not sex-linked. Use close grey/dark-brown adult palettes and do not encode sex as morph.
- Adult traits: very small owl; small inconspicuous tufts; pale-yellow eyes; fine spots/vermiculation; feathered tarsi.
- Juvenile: direct family photograph proves a juvenile perched with its parents; Mikkola only says it resembles the adult. Use an age-unknown older juvenile, smaller and softer, with short soft wings and bilateral branch support.
- Unknown: exact juvenile age, flight ability, iris colour, continuous feather sequence, occupied nest geometry, eggs/clutch, quantified sex ratio. No scientific-pass claim.
- Habitat: dense lower *Cynometra* canopy; no nest/cavity reconstruction.
