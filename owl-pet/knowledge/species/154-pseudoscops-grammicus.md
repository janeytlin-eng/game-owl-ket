# 154 · Jamaican Owl — *Pseudoscops grammicus*

## Evidence mapping
- Repository/list name used for this production slot: *Pseudoscops grammicus*.
- Range: Jamaica endemic; mostly lowland open woodland and forest edge.
- Adult evidence: dark eyes, bright rufous plumage, fine black breast streaks, white/black-edged facial disk and prominent ear tufts.
- Nest evidence: tree cavity or concealed tree fork; up to two eggs.
- Young evidence: species-labelled image index confirms a “Downy young”; exact age, growth sequence and fledging time remain unknown.

Sources: [SOURCE_INDEX](../../research/SOURCE_INDEX.md#2026-10-22--154-jamaican-owl--pseudoscops-grammicus).

## Concept boundary and review
Adults are near-equal in apparent scale because no measured sex difference was found. The age-unknown young bird is pale cinnamon-buff and downy, with short soft wing buds and hidden/very short tail, supported on a tree-cavity substrate. No precise day-age, parental division or mature flight ability is asserted.

## v3 science revision — 2026-11-12

- Current display name: *Asio grammicus*; repository legacy and filenames keep *Pseudoscops grammicus*; protonym *Ephialtes grammicus*.
- Evidence: IOC taxonomy update; Gosse 1847 p.19; Lynx West Indies p.265.
- Adults: near equal; very dark hazel-brown eyes, rufous black/white-edged facial disc, compact tufts, vermiculated umber upperparts and shaft-streaked cinnamon-fawn underparts.
- Juvenile: source-supported fledged stage, paler grey-brown/cinnamon-buff, open-eyed and bilaterally supported; one edit shortened/softened initial adult-like wing and tail.
- Unknown: exact age, iris chronology, first-flight threshold, nest architecture, dependence and quantified sex-size/plumage direction.
