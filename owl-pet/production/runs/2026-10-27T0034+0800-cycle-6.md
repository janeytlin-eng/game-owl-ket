## Research summary

Reused `BARK-AM-2022`, `BARK-ABSA-2020`, and `WHSO-MIKKOLA-2013`; added `BARK-BULLOCK-2026`. Australian Museum supports grey-brown adults with white wing spots, heavily vertical-streaked whitish underparts, yellow eyes, a less-streaked juvenile with a mottled pale nape, and a hollow with loose sticks/wood debris. ABSA gives overlapping adult measurements and says females are only marginally smaller; fledglings retain juvenile plumage and begin an adult-like immature moult at about three months after fledging. Bullock's 2023–2026 field study reports 37–41-day nestling periods and 5–6 months of post-fledging dependence for one family. Museum Victoria's brown-eye wording conflicts with the Australian Museum/Mikkola yellow-eye evidence, so it is logged as a conflict and not used for the plate.
