## Evidence actually read

1. **Miranda, Brooks & Kennedy (2011), Wilson Journal of Ornithology 123(3), printed pp. 446, 450; Table 2 and plumage section.** DOI 10.1676/10-185.1. The sexed museum series gives male wing mean 175.09 mm (range 165–187, n=11) and tail 94.81 (85–112), versus female wing 190.9 (171–210, n=10) and tail 102.6 (92–117). Ranges overlap, so the plate may show a subtle female folded-wing/tail difference but not a deterministic standing-height/body-size ratio. Diagnostic *megalotis* traits include scapular stripes and tarsal feathers onto the upper foot; typical plumage is intermediate ashy-brown/brownish. Only 2/21 specimens were red morph, so v3 uses the common intermediate morph.
2. **Avibase taxonomic concept page, accessed 2026-11-07.** AviList 2026, IOC 15.2 and Clements/eBird histories use Philippine Scops Owl; HBW/BirdLife histories use Luzon Lowland Scops-owl. V3 updates the visible plate name to the current main-list name while preserving the older alias and old combinations *Scops megalotis*, *Lempijius megalotis* and *O. m. megalotis* in the record.
3. **The Owl Pages field-photo record, Mount Data, Luzon, April 2012.** Identified juvenile is naturally supported on a moss/lichen branch. This supports an older perched juvenile stage only; it does not supply exact age, sex, iris chronology or flight threshold.
4. **Marshall (1978), printed p. 26; Sanchez (2012), pp. 98–100; Woods (2003), p. 29; Mikkola repository transcription p. 170; Macaulay ML201585161 metadata.** Reused for rufous juvenile/arboreal context, adult soft parts and Greater Luzon habitat. The Macaulay page remained bot-blocked, so metadata was not treated as pixel evidence.
5. **2026 PNA/DENR juvenile-rescue leads.** Search results identify recent juveniles, but PNA redirected to Facebook login and DENR returned HTTP 403 in this execution. They remain unverified leads and were not used to invent feather chronology.

## Design boundary

- Adults: common intermediate grey-brown morph; orange-red iris; horn/ivory bill; pale facial disc with dark rim; scapular stripes; ashy-brown underparts; tarsi feathered onto upper foot.
- Sex: near-equal standing height and mass impression. Female may have only subtly longer folded wing/tail; overlap forbids a fixed size multiplier.
- Juvenile: older, branch-supported, rufous-buff/muted brown fluffy stage with compact soft developing wing and short tail. No hatchling, no nest-interior detail, no flight claim, no adult-hard remiges.
- Habitat: Greater Luzon lowland/foothill secondary forest; mossy natural support.

Exact juvenile age and sex; hatching appearance; complete feather sequence; iris-change chronology; locomotor/first-flight threshold; quantitative live-body sexual dimorphism; subspecies-specific juvenile differences.
