## v2 issue and v3 boundary

- v2 research already corrected orange colour contamination and chose the correct 35–43-day stage. v3 standardizes the persistent production order to adult male, adult female, juvenile and makes the “fully feathered but flight unproven” distinction explicit.
- Adults remain near-equal in height; female may be subtly fuller only as a restrained illustrative convention because no robust quantitative ratio was found.
- Adult palette: neutral grey-brown with dense fine vermiculation, short low ear tufts, pale grey facial discs, dark brown/olive-neutral eyes and barred flights; no sunset-orange wash.
- Juvenile: fully feathered greyish parent-like young aged 35–43 days, compact lower stance, formed folded wings but softer contour, shorter tail, both feet supported among exposed roots. No flight pose, no white chick down, no hard oversized adult remiges.
- Environment: open Gambian riverside/Sahel woodland with exposed Neem roots; no active nest, eggs or aqueduct in this post-departure plate.
- Exact sex ratio, exact age within the range, flight capability, first-flight threshold, iris chronology and population-wide colour variation remain unknown.

## Iteration rule

A source saying “fully feathered” permits formed juvenile wings even when the generic project rule avoids mature remiges. It does not permit a flight pose or adult-identical feather crispness without evidence. This rule is applied here and must be visually rechecked after generation; it is not yet generalized as globally validated.
