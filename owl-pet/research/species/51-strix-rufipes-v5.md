## Sources actually read

1. **RLO-VUKASOVIC-2006-P172-174** — Vukasovic, Escobar, Tomasevic & Estades, 2006, *Journal of Raptor Research* 40(2):172–174, DOI 10.3356/0892-1016(2006)40[172:NROROS]2.0.CO;2. Full article read from the LEVS mirror. A single owlet occupied a roughly 30 cm top-opening cup cavity in a dead broken *Nothofagus glauca* trunk 3 m above ground in second-growth hualo forest near pine plantations. The authors estimated it at no more than one week using other *Strix* growth patterns; it remained in the cavity, was brooded by day, fed at night and pecked the cavity wall. This estimate is indirect, not a known hatch date.
2. **RLO-ESCOBAR-2022-P106-110** — Escobar et al., 2022, *Journal of Raptor Research* 56(1):106–110, DOI 10.3356/JRR-20-116. Institutional abstract re-read. Two central-Chile nests produced a 30 d incubation and 34 d nestling period in a small sample. This constrains stage duration, not the selected chick's exact age or feather map.
3. **RLO-SIMBIO-MMA-5064-2026** — Chile Ministry of Environment SIMBIO species record, accessed 2026-11-05. Confirms current name, historical combinations, 33–38 cm adult size, dark eyes, round untufted head, broad rounded wings, sex-linked plumage with a dark male and markedly paler female, rufous thighs and dense mature forest association.
4. **RLO-MMA-2015** — official Chilean classification dossier, repository-reused. Supports adult sexual dichromatism and mature-tree cavity context.
5. **RLO-MIKKOLA-2013-P333** — repository-indexed printed p.333. Separates a whitish downy chick from the later warm-buff indistinctly barred mesoptile. The uploaded guide was not accessible in this execution, so this is reuse only and not a fresh page read.

## Evidence boundaries and conflicts

- The “no more than one week” estimate in the 2006 paper is inferred from other *Strix* growth patterns; label the chick **ESTIMATED ≤1 WEEK**, not an exact hatch age.
- The 34-day nestling period is a two-nest population observation and does not supply an image of every developmental day or an independent-flight threshold.
- Adult sexual dichromatism is strong: male darker, female markedly paler. No quantitative sexed standing-height ratio was found, so the pair remains near-equal in height; the female may look slightly fuller but not substantially taller.
- Use the directly documented top-opening decayed *Nothofagus glauca* cavity for the selected chick. Do not combine eggs, ground nests, stored prey or a later mesoptile into this one plate.
- Exact chick iris, feather-by-feather sequence, sex, hatch date and locomotor capacity remain unknown. Use dark/indeterminate partially shaded eyes, down-dominant body, no formed remiges or tail, and continuous cavity-floor support.

## Design specification

- One landscape concept plate, exactly three owls: adult male, adult female, estimated ≤1-week chick.
- Adult male: round untufted head, dark eyes, rusty-grey facial disc with white brows and moustachial marks, deep dark reddish-brown upperparts, ochre underparts with bold dark horizontal bars, rufous feathered thighs/legs.
- Adult female: same identity but **markedly paler** overall—warm ochre/rufous brown, paler barring—near-equal height and only subtly fuller.
- Chick: very small, squat white-to-cream down ball, soft uneven down, tiny bill, dark/indeterminate eyes, no visible adult-like layered wing, no long tail. Both feet and belly supported inside the top-opening decayed cavity; one gentle wall-pecking lean is allowed, never flight or branch-standing.
- Environment: temperate Chilean *Nothofagus* forest, old broken hualo trunk, damp moss and decayed wood, subdued overcast forest light. Adults on separate natural branches beside the cavity; all feet visible or clearly supported.
- Layout/title: exact top title `RUFOUS-LEGGED OWL · STRIX RUFIPES`; exact lower labels `ADULT MALE`, `ADULT FEMALE`, `CHICK · ESTIMATED ≤1 WEEK`.
- Preserve the established semi-realistic hand-painted owl concept-plate style, muted natural palette, clean readable labels and no game UI.
