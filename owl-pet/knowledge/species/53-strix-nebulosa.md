# 53 · Great Gray Owl · *Strix nebulosa*

## Scope
- Current working name: *Strix nebulosa* Forster, 1772.
- Planned stage: adult male, adult female, and a recently nest-departed, preflight juvenile using a leaning tree for supported climbing.
- Status: RESEARCH_READY; not scientifically verified.

## Evidence actually read
1. Ulev 2007 USDA Forest Service FEIS species review, DOI 10.2737/feis-species-review-stne: juveniles leave the nest at 26–28.5 days, cannot fly for 7–14 days, and climb leaning or deeply fissured trees with talons, wings and bill; adult habitat and nest-site synthesis.
2. Bull & Henjum 1990, USDA Forest Service GTR PNW-GTR-265, DOI 10.2737/PNW-GTR-265: study of 24 pairs, 107 juveniles and 71 nesting attempts; nests on stick platforms, broken snags and artificial platforms; recently fledged young require nearby perches.
3. Cornell Lab All About Birds life-history account: 26–29 day nestling period; hatching down; old raptor/raven nests, broken tree tops, mistletoe brooms and platforms.

## Design boundaries
- Adults: large round-headed owl without ear tufts; yellow eyes; broad concentric gray facial discs; pale moustachial arcs and black chin/bow-tie mark; long gray-brown, finely barred and streaked plumage. Female only subtly fuller; do not force a large height difference.
- Juvenile: recently nest-departed but preflight; pale gray/off-white down still dominant with only short soft developing wing feathers; tail extremely short; body braced against deeply fissured leaning conifer bark, using feet and bill for support. No airborne or unsupported pose.
- Boreal/montane mature conifer forest with meadow opening; broken-top snag or old stick nest visible but not a cavity.
- Do not claim an exact individual age, subspecies, or fully known continuous plumage sequence.

## Unknowns
- Exact individual age, regional/subspecies plumage variation, sex-specific standing dimensions, and the precise continuous replacement of nestling down.
