## Primary source re-read in full

### RUF-RIDDELL-2021-P221-228

William Riddell, “Breeding behaviour and diet of Rufous Owls *Ninox rufa* in the Darwin Botanic Gardens,” *Australian Field Ornithology* 38:221–228 (2021), DOI 10.20938/afo38221228: https://afo.birdlife.org.au/afo/index.php/afo/article/viewFile/2242/2264

Printed pp.221–228 and Figures 1–4 were re-read. The author observed one successful 2018 event during five seasons. The male was longer/robust with a flatter crown and more prominent pale patch between the eyes (p.223). The owlet still had white down on its head when first seen; it flapped at the hollow edge on 27 August, likely left on 1–2 September, was observed high above the hollow before flight, walked branches and stretched wings on 4 September, and first made short flights on 6 September. Figure 2 captions the juvenile 2–3 days after leaving the hollow. Nestling period was 44–48 days; dependence continued about ten weeks. This supports a fully branch-supported recent fledgling with formed wings/tail and a small balance stretch, not an airborne display or universal timing. One pair/one chick in an exotic garden tree cannot set universal cavity, pattern or sex ratios.

## Conflict screened out

An Australian Raptor Care and Conservation web page was read as a lead but not admitted as a quantitative source: its prose says females are smaller/lighter while the same page lists approximate female mass greater than male mass and gives an implausible 1100 cm wingspan. Those values conflict internally and with ABSA/primary evidence, so they are not used for v5 scale or anatomy.

## Problem — evidence — modification — check plan

1. **Adult barring:** v4 reads as broad, evenly spaced zebra bands. ABSA/Mikkola/species accounts support many fine, narrow, broken pale bars over rufous. V5 requires thinner, irregular, interrupted cream marks with visible rufous between them; reject spots, broad continuous stripes and vertical belly shafts.
2. **Sex direction:** v4 male-larger direction is valid but perspective can amplify it. Riddell and ABSA support a longer, more robust, flatter-headed male and smaller female—not a fixed standing-height percentage. V5 uses a visibly fuller/longer male on a near-common support baseline, without making the female miniature.
3. **Juvenile wing/action:** v4 has a strongly ordered adult-like wing stack. Riddell directly supports branch walking/stretching 2–3 days after leaving and short flights two days later. V5 retains formed wings/tail and a supported two-point branch walk, but softens coverts into irregular rufous-brown juvenile mottling under residual white down; no adult-perfect fine barring, airborne pose or exact individual age label.
