## Evidence and design boundaries

### Adults
The 2020 paper identifies the smallest regional eagle-owl by short barred tufts, pale rufous-sandy colour, orange-yellow eyes with a broad pale ring, a plain tawny facial disc framed by dark line and white rim, dark-grey bill, much white throat, upper-breast-restricted streaking, clearer rufous barring below and pale feathered tarsi/toes. Its Table 1 gives female wing 340–390 mm (mean 367, n=20) and male 325–368 mm (mean 346.5, n=20). This supports female-larger direction but not a fixed standing-height ratio: render near-equal visible height with the female only modestly fuller/longer-winged.

### Juvenile
Sandgrouse p.72 describes an estimated c.40-day juvenile as creamy-sandy with very pale newly developed flight feathers, still-developing breast marks, large yellow irides, pale legs and medium dark claws. It came from a hole in a moderate-elevation cliff in an arid rocky wadi. Sex, exact feather count and independent-flight ability are unreported. Render a supported older nestling with compact developing folded wings ending well above the feet, short tail, bilateral rock support and no flight action. Do not import the later full-sized young’s stage.

### Name mapping
Current: *Bubo ascalaphus* Savigny, 1809, valid in ITIS. Historical literature sometimes lumped it as *Bubo bubo ascalaphus*; preserve that only as a search synonym. No ITIS synonym is asserted.
