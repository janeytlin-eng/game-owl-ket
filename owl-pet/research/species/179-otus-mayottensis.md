# #179 Mayotte Scops Owl — *Otus mayottensis* — v1 research

- Current name: *Otus mayottensis* (Benson, 1960)
- Historical/original combination: *Otus rutilus mayottensis*
- Other historical museum label: *Scops Humbloti* Oustalet (nomen nudum; not a valid available name)
- Production target: adult male, age-unknown older immature, adult female
- Accessed: 2026-10-29
- Scientific status: false

## Evidence boundary

Mikkola printed p.139 / uploaded PDF p.141 remains the adult anchor: 24 cm, about 120 g, short tufts, mottled grey-brown adults, contrasting nuchal collar, sparse pale scapular areas, streaked/barred throat, vermiculated underparts, yellow eyes, greyish-horn bill, feathered tarsi and pale toes. Female wing is slightly longer; no whole-body female-larger ratio is established.

Voisin & Voisin (MNHN), printed p.16 / PDF p.8, record that MNHN C.G. 1884-2066 from Mayotte was determined by Rasmussen as an immature *Otus rutilus mayottensis*. This confirms a species-specific older immature specimen but gives no age, plumage description, soft-parts, feather chronology or locomotor capability.

BirdForum Opus states “juveniles similar to adults” and cites HBW Vol.5, while the same account says breeding information is absent. Because the page does not map the sentence to a specific primary passage, it is secondary, medium-low confidence design evidence. It supports only an age-unknown older immature with adult-like overall identity and softer/less regular contour—not a neonatal chick, precise iris, nest or flight claim. A dated Oiseaux.net adult record from Combani Forest Reserve supplies locality and adult-photo metadata but no juvenile evidence.

## Design decision

Render an age-unknown older immature on a fully supporting branch, not in a nest: adult-like grey-brown identity, softer contour, less regular markings, compact formed rounded wing partly softened by contour feathers, partly hidden tail, both feet gripping and belly supported. Keep visible juvenile eyes neutral/shadowed because juvenile iris colour is not established. Adult sexes share plumage and are near-equal in apparent mass; the female may only suggest slightly longer folded wings.

## Unknowns

Exact age, sex, juvenile iris/soft parts, developmental sequence, first flight, nest site/material, clutch, incubation and population-wide morph proportions remain unknown. Mikkola's “Juvenile Unknown” is retained as a warning against inventing a chick stage. Science remains false.
