## Why v3

V2 is a strong three-bird plate, but the adult male reads visibly larger/taller than the female despite the evidence record finding no quantified sex dimorphism. V3 therefore keeps the species identity and direct recent-fledgling stage while making the adult pair genuinely near-equal in displayed scale.

## Evidence actually read

### Direct Honshu recent-fledgling record
Nohara & Honda 2025, printed pp.75–78 / PDF pp.1–4, DOI 10.24715/smnh.19.0_75, was read together with adult Fig.1 and young Fig.3.

- Adult: ear tufts, orange iris, feathered toes and brownish-white hind-neck patch (printed p.76).
- Young: whole body down-covered, clear tufts, iris not determinable (p.76).
- On 25–26 June 2024 it perched on Japanese cedar at about 9–10 m, moved little, and retained incompletely grown flight and tail feathers; authors inferred recent nest departure (pp.76–78).
- Nest was not found. Adult defence supports nearby breeding, not a reconstructable nest.

### Breeding context
Toyama, Kotaka & Koizumi 2015, journal pp.841–847 / HUSCAP manuscript pp.1–27, was re-read. It supports forest breeding, natural cavities / boxes and population-level breeding timing in Okinawa. It does not supply nominate Honshu plumage or a juvenile developmental plate, so no colour or exact-age transfer is made.

### Institutional account and photographic guide
Tokyo Zoological Park Society's current species account was read: forest habitat, c.25 cm, May–July tree-hollow breeding and white-down hatchlings. The hatchling fact is not transferred to the older Saitama recent fledgling.

Mikkola 2013 printed p.158 / uploaded PDF p.160 is reused from retained repository evidence: long natural tufts, grey double collar, orange-red eyes, small pale scapular marks, barred wing/tail and dark herringbone underparts. The uploaded volume itself was unavailable this execution, and no fresh page claim is made.

A Featherbase specimen result was found but direct retrieval failed; it remains an unverified lead and is not used for sex dimorphism.
