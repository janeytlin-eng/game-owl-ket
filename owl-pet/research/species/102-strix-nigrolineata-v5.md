## Evidence actually read

### `BWO-GERHARDT-GERHARDT-1997-USFS`
Gerhardt & Gerhardt, “Size, Dimorphism, and Related Characteristics of Ciccaba Owls From Guatemala,” *2nd Owl Symposium*, printed pp.190–196. The USDA Forest Service PDF was read directly. One breeding Tikal pair of Black-and-white Owls measured female 535 g, wing 29.3 cm, tail 18.7 cm and male 436 g, 28.6 cm, 16.5 cm (pp.191–192). The paper says mass and tail differed more than wing, and uncaptured pairs appeared similarly different, but only one pair was measured. This supports a visibly fuller/heavier female and slightly longer tail, not a 23% taller illustrated bird or a universal ratio.

The same paper reports all examined pellets contained insect remains and 74% also vertebrate remains (p.192), and four measured eggs averaged 33.8 ± 2.3 g with one egg in each of four clutches (p.192). No prey or egg is added to this identity plate.

### `BWO-GERHARDT-ETAL-1994-FOOD`
Gerhardt et al., “The food habits of sympatric *Ciccaba* owls in northern Guatemala,” *Journal of Field Ornithology* 65(2):258–264. The original PDF abstract and methods/results were read. It covers one nesting Black-and-white Owl pair; pellets consistently contained insect material and frequently bat remains. This is ecological context only and does not supply juvenile morphology or a sex-size population sample.

### `BWO-GERHARDT-1994-JSTOR` — re-read
The primary breeding paper record states that four Black-and-white Owl nests at Tikal were in epiphytes in large live trees, mean height 20.5 m, one egg each. The 27–33-day nest-departure statement in the abstract belongs to Mottled Owl and remains explicitly excluded from this species.

## Fact → design inference boundary

- **Fact:** the only measured Black-and-white Owl pair had a 22.7% mass difference, 2.4% wing difference and 13.3% tail difference, female larger; other pairs were visually similar but unmeasured.
- **Inference used:** near-equal crown height, female clearly fuller in body and subtly longer-tailed. The mass difference is not converted into a height multiplier.
- **Fact:** Tikal's four recorded nests were epiphyte platforms high in large live trees.
- **Inference used:** a broad living bromeliad/orchid platform is a regional evidence-backed setting, not a universal nest claim.
- **Fact:** the read juvenile account supplies pale barred plumage but no exact age, feather sequence, first-flight threshold or iris chronology.
- **Inference used:** supported age-unknown older mesoptile in a low crouch, short soft folded wing and short/concealed tail; no flight.
