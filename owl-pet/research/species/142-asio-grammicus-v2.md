## Sources actually read

### JAM-MIKKOLA-2013-P500 — reused

Heimo Mikkola, *Owls of the World: A Photographic Guide*, 2nd ed. (2013), printed pp.500–501 / uploaded PDF pp.502–503, Library `libfile_916e2244186c81918fe5a0716884b8c9`. Text was read directly on 2026-10-31. The account gives adult length 27–34 cm and wing 197–229 mm, but says sexual size and colour differences are not known. It describes long mottled brown/blackish ear tufts, warm tawny-brown upperparts without white scapular spots, barred flight feathers and tail, dull yellowish underparts with long dark shaft streaks and fine brown vermiculation, hazel-brown eyes, bluish-grey bill/cere, reddish-brown feathering on the tarsi to the toe bases, grey-brown toes and dark claws. The older juvenile is lighter above, with pale grey-brown back and dull cinnamon-buff elsewhere; the p.501 caption identifies a July Jamaican juvenile retaining down on the head. The first down is described as white.

**Limits:** secondary synthesis; no exact juvenile age, flight threshold, iris chronology, sex-size direction, nest description or continuous feather sequence. Page text, not the embedded page photographs, was accessible in this run.

### JAM-HOYER-BIRDSEYE — reused and reopened

BirdsEye Photography, Richard C. Hoyer/WINGS, Jamaican Owl gallery, including adult images and a record labelled “Downy young,” Jamaica, November: https://birdseye.photo/photos/review/3591/jamaican_owl/ (accessed 2026-10-31). The young appears fluffy cinnamon-buff with diffuse mottling, dark-looking eyes, pale grey bill, short head wisps, compact/mostly hidden wing and tail, and upright support at a tree fork. Adults show tawny underparts with longitudinal dark markings, ear tufts and a tree grip.

**Limits:** exact age, sex, capture year, flight ability and developmental sequence are not supplied; the gallery is a photographic comparator, not a morphometric or ageing study.

### JAM-AOS-NACC-2021-C10 — new

American Ornithological Society, North American Classification Committee proposal 2021-C-10, “Submerge *Pseudoscops* and *Nesasio* into *Asio*,” pp.53–54 of the proposal PDF: https://americanornithology.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/2021-C.pdf (accessed 2026-10-31). The proposal transfers *grammicus* and *clamator* to *Asio* and supports the repository's current combination *Asio grammicus*.

**Limits:** taxonomy only; it is not evidence for plumage, sex differences or development.

### JAM-POSSO-SALOMAO-2025 — new

Posso & Salomão, “Phylogenetic relationships in the genus *Asio* Brisson, 1760 (Aves: Strigidae) based on osteological characters,” *Zootaxa* 5584(3):394–408 (2025), DOI https://doi.org/10.11646/zootaxa.5584.3.5; abstract read at https://www.mapress.com/zt/article/view/zootaxa.5584.3.5 on 2026-10-31. The study uses 35 osteological characters in 36 specimens representing seven species and supports synonymising *Pseudoscops* and *Nesasio* with *Asio*; its osteological analysis places *A. grammicus* as sister to *A. clamator*.

**Limits:** osteological/taxonomic evidence only; it does not authorise visible plumage or juvenile details.

### JAM-GRAVES-2007 — new

Gary R. Graves, “Observations on the Jamaican Owl (*Pseudoscops grammicus*),” *Journal of Caribbean Ornithology* 20:53–55 (2007), Smithsonian-hosted full PDF: https://repository.si.edu/bitstreams/fb710e7c-c30a-457d-8f03-fb87e47110d5/download (accessed 2026-10-31). The full article records an adult day roost in a broad-leaved tree partly shaded by a talipot palm at Hope Botanical Gardens and documents prey/pellet contents including large invertebrates, frogs, lizards, rodents and passerines.

**Limits:** useful for leafy tree-roost context only. It does not document a nest or juvenile development, so the concept must not turn the roost into a breeding site.

## Evidence → design boundaries

- Keep adults near equal in displayed skeletal height; the sources do not support a fixed female-larger rule or a sex-specific palette.
- Adult identity: warm tawny-brown; long mottled brown-black tufts; no white scapular spots; hazel-brown irises; bluish-grey bill/cere; dull yellowish-tawny underparts with irregular longitudinal dark shaft streaks and fine brown vermiculation; reddish-brown feathered tarsi to toe bases, grey-brown bare toes, dark claws.
- Juvenile is explicitly an **age-unknown down-remnant perching stage**, not a hatchling and not a proven flier. Use dull cinnamon-buff fluff, pale grey-brown back, diffuse mottling, short head wisps, pale grey bill, compact wings/tail mostly hidden, and bilateral natural support.
- Leafy Jamaican tree context is a roost/perch inference. No nest, eggs, woven material, cavity or breeding claim.
- Do not import the bright yellow eyes, white scapular panels or broad repeating transverse bars of unrelated owl species.
