# #104 Madagascar Long-eared Owl — *Asio madagascariensis* — v3 research

- Accessed: 2026-10-29
- Target: adult male, age-unknown older branching mesoptile, adult female
- Action: staged science revision of v2; v1–v2 retained
- Scientific status: false

## Evidence

Rene de Roland & Goodman 2003 pp.1116–1117/Fig.12.20 remains the development anchor. One studied brood moved around/off its epiphyte-tuft nest at 36 days, but the photographed H. Schütz fledgling is a separate case and cannot be assigned that age. The figure caption explicitly describes molt from white nestling to dark adult plumage. Frank Vassen's dated 4 Dec 2013 Andasibe photograph page was reopened: it supports a white/cream fluffy juvenile with black mask, short tufts, a formed compact wing and branch grip, but gives no age. Mikkola printed p.509 supports orange-eyed adults, sooty-black bills, long widely separated tufts, feathered legs/toes and zoned golden-brown adult pattern. Current taxonomy retains *Asio madagascariensis*; old *Otus madagascariensis* here is historical and must not be confused with Madagascar Scops Owl.

## Limits

Exact juvenile age, iris colour, continuous feather sequence, flight ability, toe-by-toe feather boundary, nest generality and quantified sex-size ratio remain unknown. The Smithsonian specimen search lead was not used for morphology because its page failed to reopen.

## Design

Keep the juvenile as an older branch-supported mesoptile, not a newborn: white/cream down, black mask, short tufts, compact formed but juvenile-soft wings, modest partly concealed tail and two-foot grip. Adult female is only modestly fuller at near-equal display height.
