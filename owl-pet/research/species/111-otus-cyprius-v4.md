## Evidence actually read

### CYSO-MIKKOLA-2013-P122-123 — reused, uploaded guide freshly read
- Heimo Mikkola, *Owls of the World: A Photographic Guide*, printed pp.122–123 / uploaded PDF pp.124–125.
- Broad *O. scops* complex facts: 16–21 cm, 60–135 g; small ear tufts; greyish-brown with black shaft streaks; white scapular outer webs; pale-barred flight and tail feathers; yellow eyes; grey bill; tarsi feathered to toe bases. Downy chick whitish; mesoptile/juvenile adult-like but fluffier with prominent vermiculations.
- Cyprus taxon described under the old combination as a silvery-grey race.
- Limits: broad complex measurements and the guide's generalized female-heavier statement are not treated as Cyprus-specific visible dimorphism; juvenile text is not a known-age growth series.

### CYSO-FLINT-2015 — reused, primary taxonomic/morphology paper re-read
- Flint et al., 2015, “Reprising the taxonomy of Cyprus Scops Owl *Otus (scops) cyprius*…”.
- URL: https://www.britishbirds.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/article_files/V108/V108_N06/V108_N06_P291_307_A006.pdf
- Read: diagnosis/morphology pp.298–301; measurements table p.300; breeding/habitat pp.303–304.
- Evidence: Cyprus birds are darkest, darker grey, heavily black-streaked, with larger white spots on crown/hindneck/mantle/scapulars; ventral streaks much broader, vermiculation coarser and bars narrower than nominate *scops*; no rufous morph though scattered rufous feathers may occur. Specimen wing means were nearly equal (male n=10 153.3 mm; female n=13 153.5 mm); small live samples do not justify a fixed visual ratio. Eggs April–May; chicks late April–July; towns, villages, light woodland and open pine forest to 1,900 m.
- Limits: no continuous known-age juvenile feather sequence; tiny live sex samples; measurements do not prove a visible plate-wide crown-height rule.

### CYSO-IEZEKIEL-ETAL-2021 — new, primary breeding study

- DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/ani11061775
- Evidence: 238 boxes monitored in Paphos Forest, 2015–2018; rural/ecotone/forest sites; boxes mainly on *Pinus brutia* or buildings; occupancy 5–11%; edge/rural occupancy higher; laying, hatching and fledging recorded.
- Limits: supports breeding-site context, not juvenile feather detail, exact locomotor ability or universal nest geometry.
