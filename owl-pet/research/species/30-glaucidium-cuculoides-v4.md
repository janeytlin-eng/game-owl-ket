## Evidence and design boundary

**Source facts.** HKBWS identifies the Hong Kong population as *G. c. whiteleyi*, gives adults as 22–25 cm with round untufted head, yellow eyes, rufous-brown fine barring, barred tail and juveniles spotted on crown/forehead, and reports natural tree holes 3–8 m high, often Chinese Banyan. It describes three undated nest-box stages, ending with a yellow-eyed fledgling in juvenile plumage, and places fledglings near nest sites from 3 May to 3 June. Yip independently confirms actual nest-box use and nestlings in Hong Kong, but not a timed feather sequence. Owl Pages supplies adult soft-part and feather-structure direction only. The Thailand paper is an exceptional failed egg-stage ground-nest attempt and supplies no juvenile evidence.

**Design inference.** V4 remains a full-size re-render of the supported undated yellow-eyed fledgling stage, low at a natural banyan cavity lip with both feet and belly/side supported. The wing is formed but compact and soft-edged; the tail is short and partly hidden. The female is only subtly fuller at near-equal crown height because no sexed linear measurements justify a fixed ratio. Artificial nest-box geometry is not copied into the scene; Yip is used only as independent breeding/nestling support.
