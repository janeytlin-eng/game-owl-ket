## Sources actually read

1. Re-read `SEO-OSTRICH-RSD-2008`: Ansara-Ross et al. (2008), “Sexual dimorphism of four owl species in South Africa”, *Ostrich* 79(1):83–86, DOI https://doi.org/10.2989/OSTRICH.2008.79.1.11.366, Tables 1–2 and Methods/Results. The Gauteng road-kill sample contained nine sexed males and nine females of *T. capensis*. Male/female means were 412.59/469.05 g and 390.0/416.7 mm body length; both tests were significant (p=.003 and .048), but ranges overlapped (mass 370.3–488.2 vs 431.5–535.5 g; body length 360–450 vs 370–440 mm). This is a dead-carcase sample from one region and does not define a standing-height ratio, universal appearance or plumage sex key.
2. Re-read `AGO-WHITTINGTON-2012`: Whittington-Jones et al. (2011), “Monitoring of African Grass-Owl and Marsh Owl populations on the eastern and southern Johannesburg urban edge, 2010–2011”, *GABAR* 22:19–25, pp.19–25. It reports six nestlings in two active nests, tall rank grass/sedges in drainage and wetland habitat, roughly one month incubation, nest departure after about another month and literature-based fledging at about six weeks. It explicitly states that tawny-buff face/underparts cannot reliably identify immature birds because similarly coloured adult females bred in successive seasons.
3. Reused `AGO-PRETORIUS-ETAL-2026-P110`: Pretorius et al. (2026), *African Journal of Wildlife Research* 56:110–124, DOI https://doi.org/10.3957/056.056.0110. Forty-one nests support old, dense *Imperata* wetland patches and a concealed runway/platform; the study does not provide juvenile feather chronology.
4. Reused `AGO-BLSA-RDB-2025`: BirdLife South Africa species account, Identification/Biology sections. Adults are dark olive-brown above with small pale tips and pale spotted underparts; fledglings are darker and lack the adult white dorsal spots. This stage boundary is used without assigning an exact day-age.

## Fact / inference / unknown boundary

- **Fact:** the small Gauteng sample supports higher female mean mass and body length, with substantial range overlap. **Design inference:** the female can be modestly fuller/longer-bodied, but crown height remains close and no fixed ratio is encoded.
- **Fact:** an adult breeding female may have tawny-buff face/underparts, while the same colour is not a reliable age key. **Design inference:** v5 shows one warm-buff adult female as plausible individual variation, not a universal female dichromatism; labels, not colour alone, carry sex.
- **Fact:** recent fledglings are darker and lack adult white dorsal tips. **Design inference:** the central juvenile remains fully supported in a grass runway with compact folded wings, no adult dorsal spotting and no flight pose.
- **Unknown:** exact individual age, continuous feather-tract sequence, first competent flight, range-wide sex scale, frequency of adult female buff colour and exact nest-chamber geometry. Scientific verification remains false.
- **Taxonomy boundary:** current checklist name *Tyto capensis* retained. Historical combinations involving *T. capensis longimembris* apply to the Australasian taxon now treated separately as *Tyto longimembris*; their juvenile timing is not borrowed for #66.
