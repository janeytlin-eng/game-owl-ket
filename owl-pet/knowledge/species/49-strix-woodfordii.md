# 49 · African Wood Owl · *Strix woodfordii*

## Scope
- Current working name: *Strix woodfordii* (A. Smith, 1834); historical combination *Ciccaba woodfordii* retained.
- Planned stage: adult male, adult female, and approximately 25–30-day branching juvenile.
- Status: RESEARCH_READY; not scientifically verified.

## Evidence actually read
1. SANBI “African Wood Owl” (2018), habitat/reproduction sections: rounded forest owl ecology; dense evergreen/deciduous/riparian woodland; natural tree holes; sources include Steyn & Scott breeding work.
2. African Birdlife May/June 2016, PDF pp.46–47 (file page 4): Levubu nests in cocos-palm sheaths or hollow logs 7–15 m high; female leaves nest about 10 days after hatch; young exercise/stretch wings another 2–3 weeks and at 25–30 days climb surrounding branches; caption documents an almost-one-month chick clambering around the nest.
3. The Peregrine Fund species account, lines 53–64: white-down hatchling; branching at about one month; first flight around 46 days; source points to Birds of the World DOI 10.2173/bow.afwowl1.01.1.
4. The Owl Pages species account: 30–35 cm medium owl, rounded head, no ear tufts; adult photo gallery includes sex-labelled examples.

## Design boundaries
- Use a supported 25–30-day branching juvenile, not an unsupported flying chick.
- Juvenile: mostly white/pale down, emerging soft brown wing feathers, short tail; no adult-complete flight-feather layering.
- Adult: rounded head, no ear tufts, dark eyes, rich brown upperparts, white brows/spots and barred pale underparts. Do not invent a large sex-size difference.
- Use generic dense African woodland with a hollow-log/tree-cavity context; no exact subspecies or locality claim.

## Unknowns
- Exact continuous plumage sequence and sex-specific linear measurements remain unresolved.
- The 25–30-day stage is supported for climbing behavior, but individual feather detail remains a conservative design inference.
