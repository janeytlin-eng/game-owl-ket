## Sources actually read

1. **FREO-CLEMENTS-2022-LUMP** — Cornell Clements October 2022 update, lines 1050–1064. It lumps Usambara Eagle-Owl into Fraser's based on vocal similarity and playback response, retains Western *K. p. poensis* and Usambara *K. p. vosseleri* groups, transfers the species from *Bubo* to *Ketupa*, and cites phylogenetic support. https://www.birds.cornell.edu/clementschecklist/introduction/updateindex/october-2022/updates-corrections-october-2022/
2. **FREO-MNHN-MO-1971-66** — MNHN skin MO-1971-66, Côte d'Ivoire/Lamto, collected 1969 at 90 m; sex unknown and age recorded “juvenile?”. Historical label *Bubo poensis poensis*. This is useful physical/taxonomic evidence but does not establish an exact developmental age or living soft-part colour. https://science.mnhn.fr/institution/mnhn/collection/zo/item/mo-1971-66
3. **FREO-OWLPAGES-2020** — page actually reread, lines 54–70: pale-rufous disc/dusky rim, dark brown eyes, bluish-grey bill/cere/toes, rufous-buff barred adults, 39–44 cm and 575–815 g, females larger/heavier; breeding little known and young may depend on adults long after fledging. Secondary synthesis only. https://www.owlpages.com/owls/species.php?s=1300
4. **FREO-MIKKOLA-2013-P284** — repository-verified source reused: printed p.284/PDF p.286 says females average about 200 g heavier; mesoptile is barred light reddish-brown/dull yellow, with blackish eyebrow zone, weak tufts and wings/tail similar to adult. No new page access here.

## Decisions and limits

- v1's 800×533 file is a recovery derivative, not the original full-size master. v2 restores a new 1536×1024 source image while preserving v1.
- Current taxonomy includes *vosseleri*, but the plate explicitly shows western nominate *poensis*; the former Usambara species must not be counted separately.
- “Mesoptile” is directly supported, but exact age and flight ability are not. The label is changed to AGE-UNKNOWN MESOPTILE. Complete adult-like patterned wing/tail is retained as a species-specific evidence exception.
- Females average heavier here, so the female can be fuller; no precise linear size ratio is inferred. Exact nest geometry, continuous feather chronology, flight threshold, living juvenile soft parts and population-wide palette remain unknown. science=false.
