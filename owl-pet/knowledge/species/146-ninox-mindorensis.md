# #146 Mindoro Boobook — *Ninox mindorensis*

## Names and scope

- Current name: *Ninox mindorensis* Ogilvie-Grant, 1896.
- Historical checklist name: *Ninox philippensis mindorensis*.
- This record preserves v1 and documents the evidence-bounded v2 science revision; versions do not increase the unique-species count.

## v1 archive history

- Preserved concept: `design/concepts/146-ninox-mindorensis-v1.png`.
- Review trigger for v2: the bird labeled `OLDER JUVENILE` had long crisp adult-like barred remiges/tail and bright adult-yellow irides, while no species-specific developmental source supporting those claims was found. Adult underpart bars were also overly regular.

## v2 evidence boundary and result

- Adult diagnosis follows Rasmussen et al. 2012 printed p.13: small compact boobook, small bill, mostly feathered tarsus, short white supercilia, pale-brown facial disc, broken barred/speckled crown and nape, extensive white scapular patches, finely barred wing coverts, especially narrow fine underpart bars, yellow iris, pale dull-yellow/greenish bill/cere, dull olive-yellow feet and black claws.
- Sex-size direction is not quantified in the read sources; adults are rendered near equal in skeletal size and are not claimed to show universal female-larger dimorphism.
- No species-specific juvenile age, iris chronology, flight ability or feather sequence was found. V2 therefore uses `JUVENILE · AGE UNKNOWN`, closed/downcast eyes, mostly hidden wing/tail and natural two-foot branch support. This is a conservative design inference, not a developmental reconstruction.
- Initial v2 adult pattern remained too regular. Edit1 broke underpart marks into fine, narrow, interrupted, asymmetrical bars and changed crown dots into broken bars/speckling. Full plate and two crops were re-viewed.
- Review: `PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS`; `scientifically_verified=false`.

## v3 — 2026-11-07 science revision

- Primary adult evidence added: Richard C. McGregor, 1904, *Bulletin of the Philippine Museum* no.3, printed pp.16–17. The adult male has narrow buff barring, dark throat shaft-stripes, large white throat-side/scapular areas, tawny-buff barred underparts, white undertail and a narrowly buff-barred dark tail; comparison says sexes are similar.
- The same source's initial “young bird” impression is rejected by the author’s adult determination and is not used as juvenile evidence.
- Final v3 uses a closed-eyed, wing/tail-concealed, age-unknown juvenile on bilateral branch support. Exact chronology, iris, flight and feather map remain unknown.
