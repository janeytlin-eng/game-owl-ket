## Issue → evidence → modification → recheck

1. **Issue:** the first render's juvenile was smaller and fully supported, but its folded wing rows and tail were too long, regular and adult-ordered for a conservative field-estimated 8–10-week boundary.
2. **Evidence:** Reddy & Ramachandran 2023, printed pp.44–47, documents an estimated 8–10-week juvenile attempting to move among neighbouring branches while its parents watched. The report supports a branch-moving stage and high dry-stick nest, but not a measured remex/rectrix sequence or sustained-flight threshold. Ranade, Ghosh & Prakash 2020, pp.93–94, independently supports high old stick platforms near water but supplies no calibrated plumage chronology.
3. **Modification:** one juvenile-only image edit increased visible down over crown, mantle and body, shortened/softened the folded wing and tail silhouette, reduced crisp adult-like layering, lowered the bird into the broad fork and retained both feet on natural supports. Adults, text, nest and environment were held fixed.
4. **Recheck:** the full 1536×1024 image and juvenile crop were reopened after the edit. Exactly three birds and the required labels remain; both adults retain large dull grey-brown Dusky Eagle-Owl identity, yellow eyes and upright tufts at near-equal scale; the juvenile is visibly smaller, down-softened, wings folded and bilaterally supported; the high old dry-stick nest, waterbody, woodland and rice edge remain coherent. No unsupported foot, flight pose, extra bird or mature-white shoulder row was found.

## Remaining limitations / unknowns

- The 8–10-week age is a field estimate rather than a known hatch-date age.

- Exact remex/rectrix growth sequence, first-flight competence, universal sex-size ratio and subspecies transfer remain unknown.
