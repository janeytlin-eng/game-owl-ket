# 162 · Baja Pygmy Owl · *Glaucidium hoskinsii*

## Taxonomy and scope
- Current name: *Glaucidium hoskinsii* (IOC v15.2), English Baja Pygmy Owl.
- Historical mapping used by the 2025 nest paper: *Glaucidium gnoma hoskinsii*; Mikkola also notes Cape Pygmy Owl.
- Monotypic endemic of southern Baja California. This record does not change the project's fixed 251-species scope.

## Verified source facts
- Mikkola printed p.374 / PDF p.376: 15–17 cm, 50–65 g, compact round untufted head, weak facial disc, pale brows, yellow eyes, greenish-yellow bill, sandy gray/reddish upperparts, finely spotted crown, dark pale-bordered nape false eyes, mottled/streaked pale underparts and short thin-barred tail. Females are said to be heavier and often more reddish.
- Flores & Marrón 2025, pp.228–230 and Fig.2: first documented nest, a cavity 4 m high in *Washingtonia robusta* at 360 m in Cape Tropical Scrub; one near-fledged chick observed 28 May 2023. The viewed photo shows a very fluffy gray-brown, pale-faced chick supported at the cavity entrance.
- IOC v15.2: current full-species treatment and name.

## Design boundary for v1
- Adult pair: near-equal standing height; female only subtly fuller and possibly warmer. No universal fixed dimorphism is claimed.
- Young: near-fledged, cavity-supported, very fluffy and pale-faced; soft rounded developing wing, short/hidden tail, no adult-style regular spot rows, no long hard remiges, no flight pose.
- Setting: the exact low-elevation fan-palm Cape Tropical Scrub nest record, not a generic pine forest.

## Unknown / not inferred
- Exact chick age, iris color, continuous wing/tail sequence, flight ability, universal nest substrate and quantified sex ratio.
- Mikkola's “juvenile undescribed” statement is retained historically and does not override the later primary nest photograph.
