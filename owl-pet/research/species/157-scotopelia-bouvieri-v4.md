## Evidence conflict and design decisions

- The v3 wording “fine black vermiculation” is corrected: Sharpe describes black shaft-lines but **dull tawny vermiculations on a dusky-brown ground**.
- The adult bill is not rendered uniformly bright yellow. V4 uses a muted horn bill with a yellowish base; dark-horn distal area remains visible.
- No quantified sex-size direction was found. Male/female remain near-equal in standing height and differ only in pose/feather fullness.
- Juvenile stage remains the directly reported c.40-day nest young: pale down-rich body, short developing contours mostly buried, tail concealed, both feet/body fully supported on the shallow stick platform. The remarkable long persistence of down is documented, but exact appearance at 40 days is not.
- Nest is an open, sparse branch platform 2.5–3 m above river water with only a few adult feathers; not a cavity, not a massive eagle eyrie.
- Adult eyes are shown blackish/dark based on the reread secondary/field synthesis; Sharpe’s type description does not state iris colour.
