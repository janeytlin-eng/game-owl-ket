## Evidence actually read

- Greeney 2003, Lundiana 4(1):67–68, printed pp.67–68, full institutional PDF: in late October 2000 a juvenile with yellow-cream down roosted for several consecutive days beside an adult-plumaged bird near the bases of large tree-fern leaves, about four metres above ground, inside primary forest with natural disturbance and *Chusquea* bamboo. Another juvenile was sitting in a dirt road at about 20:00 in February 2001. The paper explicitly says nesting sites and breeding biology were unknown and calls the fern location a day roost, not a nest.
- Aves de Perú species account, lines 2021–2039: adult described as dark brown above, rufous-brown below with intricate rufous/brown/grey bars and spots, dark iris, greyish bill, rufous facial discs, humid montane forest at 1900–3500 m; page labels adult and juvenile photographs but gives no juvenile age or locomotor chronology.
- ITIS TSN 686690 search result exposed current valid *Strix albitarsis* and older combinations, but direct open was disabled. It is retained as a partially verified taxonomy lead, not morphological evidence.
- Reused repository record: Scherzinger 1986 p.41 adult dark-eye statement and the prior Greeney boundary. The prior Mikkola orange/deep-orange snippet remained access-blocked and is not promoted above the accessible dark-eye evidence.

## Actual v1 audit and design boundary

The intact v1 was opened at original resolution. It has exactly three supported birds and correct title/labels, but both adults and the juvenile have conspicuous amber-orange irises; adults are paler and more white-faced than the accessible dark/rufous adult accounts, and the juvenile is staged on a generic mossy nest-like platform rather than the documented fern-base roost. V3 will retain the established composition while changing adult irises to dark brown-black, deepening the adult rufous/dark-brown identity, and using a yellow-cream downy, age-unknown juvenile supported beside an adult at a tree-fern leaf base. Juvenile iris is not asserted from Greeney and should be dark-neutral/partly shadowed rather than bright orange. Adult sexes remain near-equal in visible scale because no sex-specific measurement was recovered.

Exact juvenile age, iris chronology, wing tract sequence, flight ability, nest site, toe-feather boundary and visible sex-size ratio remain unknown. The fern-base scene is a roost, not a nest; science stays false.
