## New source actually read

### MOR-STEPHENSON-THESIS-1998

Brent Mark Stephenson, *The ecology and breeding biology of morepork, Ninox novaeseelandiae, and their risk from secondary poisoning, in New Zealand*, MSc thesis, Massey University, 1998: http://hdl.handle.net/10179/9851 and full PDF https://mro.massey.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/a71313ab-b6a0-46b5-84fa-a07309da4b89/content

The repository record, thesis abstract and printed pp.30–33 were read. Thirty-one birds were captured; plumage/morphometrics usually could not determine sex, whereas DNA did. Immatures on Mokoia had a less distinct facial disc but broadly similar plumage to adults. Known-pair field comparison could not reliably detect size; captured females nevertheless had significantly longer wing and bill width and greater mass. This supports near-equal standing height with only restrained female fullness/wing direction, never an obvious universal ratio. It does not provide a day-by-day fledgling feather map.

## Primary paper re-read in full

### MOR-STEPHENSON-MINOT-2006

B. M. Stephenson & E. O. Minot, “Breeding biology of morepork (*Ninox novaeseelandiae*) on Mokoia Island,” *Notornis* 53:308–315, DOI 10.63172/063074aaqgjh: https://www.birdsnz.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Notornis_53_3_308.pdf

Printed pp.308–312 and Figures 1–2 were re-read. One intensively measured chick had white down replaced by darker grey by day 15; pins/brown fluff emerged around day 16; by day 25 the back and secondaries had developed white spots while grey/brown down still covered much of the body; day 27 showed a masked face; rectrices emerged about 10 mm by day 30; the bird fledged day 39 with residual dark down on breast, head and back. Nests were varied (cavities, tree-fern forks/bases, earth bank, boxes) and used leaf litter/wood dust rather than imported material. This validates formed spotted juvenile wing feathers and a short tail, but not a fully adult-ordered wing surface or universal exact age.

## Problem — evidence — modification — check plan

1. **Juvenile wing order:** v4's center bird has a clean adult-like stack with many evenly spaced high-contrast white spots. Primary pp.310–311 confirms white-spotted back/secondaries by day 25 but also residual grey/brown down through fledging. V5 keeps formed wing feathers and some irregular pale spots, while breaking the repeated rows with down-softened covert edges and juvenile grey-brown tone.
2. **Sex scale:** v4 adults are already near-equal. Thesis pp.30–33 shows field sexing by appearance unreliable despite captured female differences. V5 keeps equal standing height/common support, allowing only subtle female fullness and slightly longer folded wing—not a visibly oversized female.
3. **Stage/action:** primary evidence supports a day-39 fledgling with short emerging rectrices and residual dark down. V5 uses a firmly supported low crouch beside the nest fork; no flight, wing spread, baby wing buds or exact-age label.
