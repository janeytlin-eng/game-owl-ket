## New source actually read

### BOO-GOODWIN-2018-P113-116 — primary regional eye-colour study

Warren Goodwin, “Is there a link between eye colour and haemoparasite infection in the Southern Boobook *Ninox boobook* in the Perth Metropolitan Area?”, *Australian Field Ornithology* 35:113–116 (2018), DOI 10.20938/afo35113116. Full article: https://afo.birdlife.org.au/afo/index.php/afo/article/download/2123/2145

Printed pp.113–116 were read. The article states that both sexes and all age classes commonly show yellowish to greenish or greenish-hazel irides, while a relatively high proportion of Perth birds (identified there as subspecies *ocellata*) range from dark greenish-brown to dark brown. The proposed polymorphism, signalling role and any parasite-driven selection are explicitly tentative. This supports a muted yellow-green/green-hazel generic adult eye rather than a uniform saturated lemon-yellow diagnostic. It does not support sex-specific adult eye colour, parasite causation, or transferring Perth frequencies to all mainland birds.

## Re-read species-specific evidence

- `BOO-OLSEN-JUDGE-TROST-2015`, *Corella* 39(3):77. Nine wild just-fledged birds had wing lengths 17.0–24.2 cm and modelled ages 35.8–50.2 days; some branched and others first flew to a nearby tree up to about 4 m. Supports formed folded wings/tail and firm support, not a universal exact age or polished flight pose.
- `BOO-OLSEN-TROST-FUENTES-DEBUS-2020`, *Corella* 44:61–70. Ten broods in six Canberra territories fledged at 33–41 days (mean 37); young initially roosted with parents and remained dependent. Supports a recent-fledgling stage with cautious, fully supported posture; not a feather-by-feather atlas.
- `BOO-VIC-REHAB-2023`, Victorian Government *Wildlife Rehabilitation Guidelines Part B: Birds*, printed p.86. Adult: dark facial discs, green-yellow/yellow eyes, dark chocolate-brown upperparts with off-white spots, rufous-brown underparts and white-streaked belly. Juvenile: whitish crown with central dark streaking, dark face, chocolate-brown profusely white/buff-spotted upperparts, downy white underparts and tawny upper breast.
- `BOO-ABSA-2019`, *Bird in the Hand*, printed pp.1–2. Adult measurement ranges overlap; females tend only slightly larger, especially mass. Juvenile iris changes from powder-blue toward straw-yellow; postjuvenile moult begins about three months after fledging. Measurement means are not a fixed standing-height ratio.

## Problem — evidence — modification — check plan

1. **Problem:** v4 uses uniformly saturated lemon-yellow adult irises. **Evidence:** Goodwin documents common yellowish-green/greenish-hazel and regional dark variation. **Modification:** use muted yellow-green/green-hazel adults, without making the sexes different. **Check:** reject neon-yellow universality and reject dark-eye frequencies as mainland-wide.
2. **Problem:** v4 adult belly pattern reads as broad drop-shaped blotches. **Evidence:** the Victorian official diagnosis specifies a rufous-brown upper breast and white-streaked belly. **Modification:** strengthen narrow vertical white streaks/spots on the lower breast and belly while keeping chocolate upperparts. **Check:** streaks must read vertically, not as uniform rows or broad teardrops.
3. **Problem:** v4 recent fledgling has crisp, orderly adult-like folded-wing spots. **Evidence:** field studies support developed wings/tail at fledging, but the official juvenile diagnosis and age limits do not support adult-perfect repeated rows. **Modification:** retain formed folded wings and tail but soften markings into irregular white/buff spotting, with powder-blue iris, streaked whitish crown, tawny upper breast and downier pale belly. **Check:** feet remain visibly supported; no flight pose, wing buds or mature yellow eyes.
