## Active research and evidence boundary

The full original Rene de Roland & Goodman chapter was reopened at printed pp.1116–1117/Fig.12.20. It follows one 2000 pair/brood: an epiphyte-tuft nest about 9 m high; young self-fed at 25 days, moved off the nest into adjacent trees at 36 days, first received food away at 42 days, later moved 50–70 m and dispersed at 64 days. Fig.12.20 is explicitly a separate Tsinjoarivo fledgling molting from white nestling to dark adult plumage, so its image must not be assigned the measured brood's exact age. One female weighed 770 g; no male weights were available, although the chapter says males are smaller.

Three dated photographs were also reopened: Vassen 2013 Commons and Peter Day 2016 BirdForum juvenile pages support an age-unknown white/cream fluffy young with dark mask, short tufts, formed brown folded wings and secure branch support; Fenerole 2006 Oiseaux.net male supports long narrow flexible adult tufts, orange iris, black bill and feathered lower-leg direction. None supplies a juvenile timetable or population-wide sex ratio.

## Design decisions

- Preserve exactly three birds and the existing current name/number.
- Adults: long narrow flexible widely separated tufts, orange eyes, black bills, warm golden-brown pattern, sparse lower-belly marks, feathered tarsi/proximal toe bases. Female only modestly fuller at near-equal height.
- Young: label age unknown; white/cream mesoptile, dark mask, short fuzzy tufts, compact formed but juvenile-soft brown wings, mostly concealed tail and a cautious two-foot branch step.
- Use the recorded epiphyte fork as a case, not a universal nest.
- Branch movement is not evidence of sustained flight. No spread wings or airborne pose.

## Current/legacy name

Current *Asio madagascariensis*. Historical original combination *Otus madagascariensis* must not be confused with the Western Malagasy Scops Owl now using the same genus-species combination in modern taxonomy.

## Unknowns retained

Exact juvenile age/sex, iris chronology, continuous molt, first-flight threshold, population-wide adult size ratio, toe-by-toe feather boundary and nest generality remain unknown. Scientific verification remains false.
