# #148 *Ninox rumseyi* v3 evidence and design boundary

- Current name: *Ninox rumseyi* Rasmussen et al., 2012; Cebu Boobook / Cebu Hawk-Owl.
- Historical mapping: formerly the Cebu population of *Ninox philippensis spilonota* within the broad *N. philippensis* complex.
- Production intent: preserve v1/v2 and make a v3 science revision. This remains one unique species in the 251-species roster.

## Evidence actually read this cycle

### CEB-RASMUSSEN-2012-FORKTAIL — reused primary species description

Rasmussen et al. 2012, *Forktail* 28:1–20, full PDF, https://www.asianscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/Vocal-divergence-and-new-species.pdf , accessed 2026-11-07. The *N. rumseyi* diagnosis/type account is printed pp.16–17. Adults are medium-large, compact and sleek with rounded head, small bill, long tarsi, short separated whitish supercilia, dark ear-coverts, prominent white throat, narrowly barred upperparts, white dark-edged scapular spots, variable warm underparts, lemon-yellow irides, dull pale-olive bill/cere, bright-yellow feet and mostly dark claws. It does not give a juvenile plumage series.

### CEB-JAKOSALEM-COLLAR-GILL-2012 — reused and corrected primary field study

Jakosalem, Collar & Gill, “Habitat selection and conservation status of the endemic *Ninox* hawk-owl on Cebu, Philippines,” *Bird Conservation International*, DOI 10.1017/S0959270912000317, full HTML read 2026-11-07 at https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bird-conservation-international/article/habitat-selection-and-conservation-status-of-the-endemic-ninox-hawkowl-on-cebu-philippines/632843592EFE90CC32B1279D61BE1BFE . Methods/results lines 528–532 report nine adults: four with brood patches were consistently smaller (wing 175–181 mm, tail 91–99 mm, mass 120–129 g) than five others (wing 184–190 mm, tail 100–109 mm, mass 124–142 g); authors **assumed** the former female and latter male. This corrects v2's “near equal because no direction” inference: v3 may show a subtle male-larger direction, but not an exaggerated or universal rule because sex was inferred from brood patch and comparative biology, not genetic confirmation. One field-classified juvenile measured wing 175 mm, tail 96 mm, mass 118 g, but the paper gives no plumage or flight-threshold description. Lines 596–602 support foliage-covered, branch-rich low-wind roost/forage settings and natural trunk cavities 2–2.5 m above ground; a recently hatched juvenile was observed in 2005 but not described or illustrated, so no hatchling anatomy is inferred.

## Design decisions and uncertainty

- Apply the primary study's within-sample direction cautiously: adult male subtly larger/longer-bodied, adult female subtly smaller; no exaggerated dimorphism.
- Preserve v2 adult identity corrections and dark-claw/pale-base boundary.
- Use an age-unknown, field-classified older juvenile with eyes closed, stationary bilateral branch support, diffuse down and short down-obscured wing/tail. Do not reconstruct the undescribed hatchling.
- Use branch-rich low-wind secondary-forest edge, not an opened cavity or invented nest contents.
- Scientific verification remains false; target outcome is PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS after pixel review.
