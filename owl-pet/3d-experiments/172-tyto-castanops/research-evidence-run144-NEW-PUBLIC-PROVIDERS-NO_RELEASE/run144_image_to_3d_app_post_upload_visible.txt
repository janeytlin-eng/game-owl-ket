02 / FAQ
FAQ

About the free trial, generation speed, export formats and commercial licensing.

10 QUESTIONS
How long does one model take?

Most product shots and still lifes finish within half a minute; high-precision mode is a bit slower, and you can switch precision any time after previewing.

Do I need modeling skills?

No. Upload a reference, wait for generation, download the file — all in the browser; further refinement is up to you.

Which export formats are supported?

All three mainstream formats are supported: two come bundled with textures for direct engine import; one is mesh-only, ready for slicers.

OBJ
GLB
STL
Can I use generated models commercially?

Paid plans include a commercial, client-ready license. Free preview renders are for personal evaluation — upgrade to Pro to ship models in client and product work.

Is it really free without signing up?

Yes. You get 2 free preview renders per day without an account — watermarked, up to 20K faces. That is enough to test any image before you commit.

What are the limits of the free preview?

Free previews are watermarked and capped at 20K polygons. A free account unlocks watermark-free downloads at the same quality; Pro raises the ceiling to 1M faces with full texture maps.

Which AI engines can I choose?

The workbench lets you pick between reconstruction engines tuned for different subjects — hard-surface props, characters or organic shapes. You can switch engines between previews at no cost.

Is the output printable / watertight?

Yes. The mesh reconstruction fills occluded areas and produces a watertight, manifold mesh that slices cleanly in Cura, PrusaSlicer or Bambu Studio. Export STL for printing.

OBJ, GLB or STL — which should I pick?

GLB for web, AR and engines (textures bundled, smallest file). OBJ for Blender, Maya and sculpting pipelines. STL for 3D printing slicers — mesh only, no textures.

How is this different from height-map converters?

Height-map tools turn pixel brightness into a flat relief. This generator reconstructs a full, editable 3D model — including the hidden sides — so results are usable in engines and printers, not just as plaques.
