## New evidence actually read

### RUF-ABSA-2019 — new, HANZAB synthesis

J.W. Hardy / Australian Bird Study Association, *Bird in the Hand: Rufous Owl* (March 2019), https://absa.asn.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Rufous-Owl.pdf , printed p.1.

Actually read morphometrics, ageing and sexing. Nominate *N. r. rufa* adult male wing 361–377 mm and mass 1150–1300 g versus female wing 341–355 mm and mass 935–1020 g; sexes similar in plumage but females smaller. Juveniles fledge in juvenile plumage; transition to adult-like immature plumage soon after fledging, thought complete within 4–5 months. One-page HANZAB synthesis; strong for sex direction and broad age boundary, not a standing-height ratio or continuous feather series. The table also contains other subspecies columns, so v4 does not mix them.

### RUF-NIELSEN-1995 — new, field note

Lloyd Nielsen, “Notes on the Rufous Owl in North Queensland,” *Sunbird* 25(3):71–72 (1995), https://birdsqueensland.org.au/sunbird_issues/articles/Vol_25/Nielsen_1995_v25_3_71-72.pdf .

Printed pp.71–72 actually read. North Queensland *N. r. queenslandica* used open tropical woodland intersected by creek/river gallery forest; nest in an upright paperbark hollow 14 m high. On 29 September a newly fledged young perched precariously in wattle foliage 15 m from the nest while parents perched nearby. Male was visibly larger. This regional note supports branch-capable fledgling and gallery-forest setting; it does not supply exact feather geometry and is not transferred as nominate morphometrics.

## Reused/corrected evidence

- RUF-RIDDELL-2021, primary five-season Darwin field study, AFO 38:221–228, pp.221–223 and 226–227 previously read and abstract freshly reopened: one 2018 young branched 2–5 September and made first true flight 6 September; nestling 44–48 days, dependence about 10 weeks; one successful chick in one exotic Earpod Tree hollow. Strong for stage/action, not a universal nest or exact plumage clock.
- RUF-OWLPAGES-2020: adult narrow rufous barring, yellow eyes, blue-horn bill, male larger; first/second white down; tropical forest/large hollow. Secondary synthesis.
- WHSO-MIKKOLA-2013, printed pp.448–449/PDF pp.450–451: adult male larger/broader flatter crown, fine light bars; white-headed/pale juvenile with dark eye mask. Photographic baseline and old spelling crosswalk.
- Correction: v3 improved adult barring but did not use the ABSA post-fledging moult boundary or the independent Queensland observation of a precarious new fledgling away from the nest. V4 retains complete functional wings/tail and branch walking while preventing adult regular barring on the young.
