## Name mapping

Current accepted species is *Bubo bengalensis* (Franklin, 1831). ITIS TSN 555405 lists the older treatment *Bubo bubo bengalensis* and original combination *Otus bengalensis* as synonyms. This version keeps the current repository name and records both older names for lookup.

## Evidence actually read

1. **BEN-RAMANUJAM-MURUGAVEL-2009-P519-524** — Ramanujam & Murugavel, “Development of young Indian Eagle Owl *Bubo bengalensis*,” *Journal of Threatened Taxa* 1(10):519–524, DOI 10.11609/JoTT.o1762.519-24; full PDF read.
   - Nine chicks, 10–60 days, around Puducherry; preliminary regional study.
   - Printed p.519: natal down whitish; prejuvenile moult about day 14–15; full juvenile plumage by day 35 and still present after day 60.
   - Printed pp.519–520: birds after day 35 could glide/flap away; authors treat day 60 as fully able to fly. This does **not** prove identical flight ability for every c.35-day chick.
   - Printed pp.519–520 and figs.1–3: wing and bill grow steadily, tarsus rises steeply to about day 20, body mass plateaus around days 35–45; single measurements are unreliable for exact ageing.
   - Sex differences were not examined because the sample was small and chicks were hard to sex (printed p.520).
2. **BEN-PANDE-DAHANUKAR-2011-P1677-1685** — Pande & Dahanukar, “Ecological effects on morphometric development of the Indian Eagle Owl *Bubo bengalensis*,” *Journal of Threatened Taxa* 3(4):1677–1685, DOI 10.11609/JoTT.o2609.1677-85; article abstract/full-page metadata read.
   - Sixteen individuals aged 1–58 days on the Deccan Plateau.
   - Wing, tarsus, claw, bill, tail and mass develop in partly decoupled patterns; comparison with southern India indicates geographic developmental plasticity.
   - Therefore exact age, flight capacity and bodily proportions cannot be inferred from one visible trait.
3. **BEN-ITIS-555405-2026** — ITIS report TSN 555405, read 2026-11-06.
   - Valid current name *Bubo bengalensis*; synonyms include *Bubo bubo bengalensis* and *Otus bengalensis*.
   - Taxonomy only; no morphology/development inference.
4. **BEN-MIKKOLA-2013-P274-275** — repository-pinned transcription/reference to Heimo Mikkola, *Owls of the World*, printed pp.274–275; reused, not freshly opened as an uploaded file in this run.
   - Adult identity boundary retained: orange-yellow eyes; brown ear tufts; dark crown; strong white brows and blackish facial ruff; tawny-brown upperparts with black mottling; narrow upper-breast streaking and faint fine lower crossbars; feathered reddish-yellow tarsi/toe bases.
   - Repository record supports near-equal visible adult height and only a subtle possible female folded-wing/tail length cue. No blanket “female larger” rule and no quantified mass ratio is claimed.

## Design boundary applied

- **Adult male / female:** same species proportions and near-equal standing height. Female may read slightly fuller with a subtly longer folded wing/tail, not a taller giant. Orange-yellow eyes, white brows, black facial border, tawny-brown/black-mottled plumage and feathered tarsi are mandatory. Outer toe joints may remain cautiously visible.
- **Juvenile stage:** c.35-day juvenile with complete juvenile plumage but still soft, fresh and less crisply organized than adults; shorter rounded folded wings and short tail; pale fluffy head/body texture; orange eye saturation reduced. It stands/crouches on a broad rock shelf with both feet supported.
- **Action limit:** a documented c.35-day bird may glide, but individual flight competence is unknown. Plate shows a grounded cautious side-step/low brace, no airborne pose, no spread mature flight feathers and no “flightless” claim.
- **Nest/habitat:** rocky semi-desert ravine/ledge, dusty stone and sparse thorn scrub. No invented cavity nest, eggs or hatchling anatomy.
- **Unknowns retained:** juvenile sex; individual c.35-day flight competence; exact standing-height ratio; regional fine-plumage variation; precise exposure of outer toe joints.
