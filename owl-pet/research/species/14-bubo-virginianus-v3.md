## Source facts

- Mikkola printed pp.262–264 / PDF pp.264–266: sexes identical in plumage; female can average heavier; juvenile mesoptile is pale brownish to dull yellow, fluffy on mantle/back, weakly barred below, and has smaller/less apparent tufts. Printed p.264 shows an immature still begging in September.
- Hoffmeister & Setzer (1947), pp.157–173: daily observations of one 1945 brood plus a 1946 brood; about day 45 the young could fly short distances and were about three-quarters grown. Sample and sex inference limit generalization.
- International Owl Center age chart, PDF pp.1, 12–15: at 5–6 weeks dark facial-disc margin is developed and flight feathers are substantially grown; by 6–8 weeks wings are nearly complete while tails are not. Captive-reared sample with ideal food.
- Cornell Bird Cams FAQ: day 40 young can climb/branch; at 6 weeks they move to nearby branches; at 7 weeks only several short tiring flights.
- University of Minnesota Raptor Center: adults have prominent tufts, yellow eyes, white throat, barred underparts, and feet feathered to the toes; immature birds resemble adults, but that broad phrase is not a license to copy adult wing/tail geometry into an earlier brancher.
