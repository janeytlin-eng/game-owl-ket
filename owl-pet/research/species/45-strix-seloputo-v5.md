## Evidence synthesis

NParks supplies the adult identification boundary: untufted head, dark brown eyes, grey bill, dark-brown upperparts with white spots, bold dark-on-buffy ventral barring and feathered tarsi. The dated Pasir Ris records describe one family at roughly 2–4 days after fledging on 23 February 2022: about half adult size, conspicuously downy, walking on branches and using wing flaps to balance. Remiges lengthened over the following weeks while rectrices stayed shorter; branch-to-branch flight was not reported until 11 March. Part 1 dates the first fledging to 18 February and Part 2 to 19 February, so this one-day conflict is retained. A separate 2018 fallen chick walked, flapped and scrambled but could not fly, independently reinforcing the rule that flapping is not flight.

Repository-indexed Mikkola printed pp.322–323 (PDF pp.324–325) leaves adult sex-size difference unknown and describes a pale fledgling whose face and primaries begin to resemble the adult while its back remains distinctive. The uploaded copy was not accessible in this execution, so it is reused rather than claimed as freshly read.

## Design boundaries

- Adults: nearly equal displayed scale; same diagnostic plumage; visual difference comes from pose only.
- Adult head and body: no ear tufts; very dark brown eyes; grey bill/cere; orange-buff face with subtle darker edge; coffee-brown upperparts with black-edged white spots; buff/pale underparts with broad pale and narrow dark barring; feathered legs/toes.
- Juvenile: one-family, 2–4-days-post-fledging design boundary; about half adult body scale; prominent whitish down; pale poorly bounded face; diffuse white-and-dark-brown barred back; short growing primaries; much shorter or visually hidden tail.
- Action: low branch walking, both feet gripping a broad branch, one short wing partly open only for balance; not flying.
- Setting: a rain-tree branch and nearby bird's-nest fern may reference the Pasir Ris family but must not imply universal nest architecture.
- Unknown: exact hatch age, sex-specific growth, independent-flight threshold for the pictured individual, universal nest geometry and population-specific plumage variation.
