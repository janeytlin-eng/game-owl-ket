## Evidence actually read

### STY-LOPES-ETAL-2004 — new primary source
Lopes, Goes, Souza & de Melo Ferreira (2004), *Ornitología Neotropical* 15:423–427, “Observations on a nest of the Stygian Owl (*Asio stygius*) in the central Brazilian Cerrado.” Full text: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237472621_Observations_on_a_nest_of_the_Stygian_Owl_Asio_stygius_in_the_central_Brazilian_Cerrado

Printed pp.423–426 and Fig.1 were actually read. The nest was a 17 cm diameter × 3 cm deep ground depression, thinly lined with dry grass, under a shrub below 70 cm in open cerrado ralo. Eggs hatched 10 April. The young were followed to day 27; one day before predation they were a little over two-thirds adult size, but their remiges and rectrices were poorly developed and completely prevented a flight attempt. Figure 1 documents ten-day nestlings. This is a one-nest dated sequence; it does not prove universal plumage geometry.

## Design boundary and unknowns

V3 must not duplicate v2's >1-month branch-capable juvenile. It uses a 26–27-day, ground-supported nestling with a large body but conspicuously short remiges and rectrices, making flight impossible. A slight raised-wing defensive stance is permissible, but mature primaries, mature tail, branch perch and flight pose are prohibited. Female is only subtly fuller because no exact ratio is supported. Exact feather geometry, individual sex, iris/bill chronology and transfer among populations remain unknown.
