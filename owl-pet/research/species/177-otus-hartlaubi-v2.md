## Evidence read this cycle

### Adult morphology and scale

- Melo et al. 2022, ZooKeys 1126, printed p.7 Table 1 and printed p.27 diagnosis: five São Tomé birds are reported with wing 130–136 mm in the visible rows; the diagnosis gives the species range as 130–139 mm. Tail is 67–69 mm in the visible rows. The entries are unsexed, so these values support a very small, compact, short-tailed adult silhouette but do **not** support a male/female body-size direction.
- Mikkola 2013 printed p.136/PDF p.138 (repository evidence reused; user upload inaccessible in this run): very small owl, very small tufts; female described as a little larger/heavier; deep yellow adult eyes, yellow bill/cere, distal quarter of tarsus unfeathered; grey-brown morph dark and comparatively uniform with large pale scapular areas. Because the newer measured birds are unsexed and no statistical sex comparison is available, v2 keeps the adults near-equal in display scale and records the female-size statement only as a subtle possible fullness cue, not a quantified rule.
- Jones, Burlison & Tye / IUCN 1991 printed pp.72–73: two phases are reported; ear tufts may be absent/very small and the rear/distal tarsus is not fully feathered. This supports low tufts and restrained exposed distal tarsus, not long bare legs.

### Young bird and behaviour boundary

- Mikkola states the downy chick is undescribed and permits only a paler, finely barred mesoptile. Therefore the centre bird is an **age-unknown mesoptile**, not a hatchling and not a dated fledgling.
- IUCN 1991 p.72 reports fledglings in October and infers a breeding period, but supplies no age, mobility, feather sequence, iris, nest geometry or young-bird photograph. This confirms reproduction timing only; it does not upgrade the design to flight-capable or justify a nest.
- Exact age, flight ability, juvenile iris/soft-part chronology, remex/tail sequence and nest site remain unknown. The mesoptile must be fully supported on a broad living branch with both feet in contact and no wing-spread action.

### Habitat boundary

IUCN 1991 pp.72–73 places highest historical density in middle-altitude secondary forest near Fortunato and reports primary/secondary forest records. This authorizes humid São Tomé forest as environmental context only. No cavity, nest, egg or substrate-specific breeding claim is made.

### Taxonomy

ITIS TSN 555374 (accessed 2026-11-01) treats *Otus hartlaubi* as a valid species and records *Athene hartlaubi* as a synonym. IOC 15.1 is cited by ITIS as a current checklist source.
