## Evidence actually read

### NPH-RASMUSSEN-2012 — primary taxonomic/morphology study

P. C. Rasmussen et al., 2012, “Vocal divergence and new species in the Philippine Hawk Owl *Ninox philippensis* complex,” *Forktail* 28:1–20. PDF: https://www.asianscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/Vocal-divergence-and-new-species.pdf

Read pp.1–2 and 11–14, especially the diagnosis and Tables 5/7. The paper splits the old broad complex into seven species. For *N. philippensis* sensu stricto it diagnoses compact sleek plumage, small/buff-tinged pale supercilium, plain dark auriculars with long filamentous extensions, plain brown crown-to-rump, white/pale-buff scapular patches, large white outer-covert spots without barring, weak narrow pale tail bands, prominently longitudinally streaked underparts, yellow iris, pale olive-yellow bill and yellow feet with dark claws. Table 5 pools 68–76 specimens for several measures and is not a sex-specific size series; it therefore does not justify a visible female-larger ratio.

### NPH-SHARPE-1875 — museum catalogue young-plumage description

R. B. Sharpe, 1875, *Catalogue of the Birds in the British Museum*, vol. 2, printed pp.167–168, species account “5. *Ninox philippensis*.” PDF: https://wallace-online.org/converted/supplementary/specimens/1875_BMbirds_WSPEC351.2.pdf

The age-unknown “Young” is described as rufous chocolate above and mostly uniform, except a few buffy-white outer-scapular bars; wing-coverts are uniform like the back, greater coverts distinctly spotted ochraceous/white, primary coverts dark brown, and formed dark quills are notched ochraceous/buffy. The catalogue supplies no age, sex, iris, action capability or locality-specific modern taxon reassessment. Use only the directly described feather state and keep the pose stationary and supported.

### NPH-ITIS-2025 — current name/common-name checkpoint

ITIS TSN 555484, https://www.itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?print_version=PRT&search_topic=TSN&search_value=555484&source=to_print, accessed 2026-11-06. It recognizes *Ninox philippensis* Bonaparte, 1855 as valid and lists Luzon Boobook/Philippine Hawk-Owl/Luzon Hawk-Owl; the record cites IOC v15.1 (2025). This supports current naming only, not plumage or development.

### NPH-PROJECTNOAH-2012 — unverified visual lead, not design proof

Project Noah, photographed 8 June 2012 near Peñablanca, Cagayan: https://projectnoah.org/spottings/owl-3. The page calls it a baby owl and shows a community suggestion of *N. philippensis*, but supplies no expert determination or age. It is retained only as a lead for a low, supported forest-floor/brancher context and is not used to infer exact feather chronology, flight competence or iris.

## Facts, design inference and unknowns

- Fact: modern *N. philippensis* is the compact streak-breasted member of the former broad complex; the adult identity gates above are species-specific.
- Fact: a historical museum “young” has formed quills and a mostly uniform rufous-chocolate dorsal surface, not a generic white down hatchling and not an adult-spotted/banded wing.
- Design inference: depict an age-unknown young bird with formed but soft-looking folded quills, rufous-chocolate uniform coverts, limited greater-covert spots, and continuous two-foot branch support. Keep the tail short/partly obscured and avoid movement claims.
- Sex boundary: show adults near-equal in crown-to-toe height and fullness; no fixed dimorphism claim.
- Unknown: exact age, sex, iris chronology, down retention, body-size direction by sex, flight threshold, nest structure and locality of the museum young.
