# #160 Minahasa Masked-Owl — *Tyto inexspectata*

## v1 — 2026-10-27

- First archived concept, preserved at `160-tyto-inexspectata-v1.png`. It treated the center bird as a documented dependent fledgling.
- Archive integrity is verified, but the scientific wording is superseded by v2.

## v2 science evidence correction — 2026-11-01

- Fitzsimons 2010 p.143 reports that the visible two-adult-plus-juvenile identification was questioned and may have been *Tyto rosenbergii*. The center bird is therefore demoted to an age-unknown older-young design inference.
- Schlegel 1879 and ITIS preserve the current/original name mapping: *Tyto inexspectata* ⇄ *Strix inexspectata*.
- First v2 candidate retained an adult-like long spotted juvenile wing. One targeted edit shortened, rounded and down-softened the wing, removed adult spot/bar rows, concealed the tail and retained both feet on the branch. Full plate and two review crops were reopened.
- Result: `PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS`, `scientifically_verified=false`. Exact stage, iris chronology, feather sequence, flight ability, quantified sex-size direction and nest interior remain unknown.

## v3 science revision — 2026-11-07

- Naturalis 2023 type catalogue p.175 confirms *Strix inexspectata* = *Tyto inexspectata* and RMNH.AVES.88273 as an adult female lectotype, not holotype.
- A 2022 Roland Godon living-adult image from North Sulawesi was read for adult identity only; sex/age are unspecified. Macaulay ML625386016 remains metadata-only.
- Adult sexes stay near-equal because quantitative dimorphism evidence was not found. The centre bird remains an age-unknown older-young design inference because the published visible juvenile identification was questioned.
- Actual juvenile crop exposed layered adult-like remiges. Edit 1 shortened and down-veiled both wings and concealed the tail; full plate plus juvenile and adult/support crops were reopened.
- Result: `PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS`, `scientifically_verified=false`. Exact juvenile age, feather sequence, iris chronology, flight onset, quantified sex-size direction and nest interior remain unknown.
