## Evidence actually read

### CPO-HOWELL-ROBBINS-1995-P17-20
Howell & Robbins 1995, “Species Limits of the Least Pygmy-Owl Complex,” *Wilson Bulletin* 107(1):7–25. https://sora.unm.edu/sites/default/files/journals/wilson/v107n01/p0007-p0025.pdf . Printed pp.17–20 read 2026-11-01.

Facts: recognizes *G. palmarum* as the western Mexican species including *palmarum*, *oberholseri* and *griscomi*. Adults are pale greyish tawny-brown with extensive pale head spots, pale cinnamon/buffy-white wing marks, six or seven pale tail bars and cinnamon-brown underpart streaks; they lack bold white scapular/wing spots. Unlike *G. sanchezi*, *palmarum* is not sexually dichromatic. Wing and tail means are modestly larger in females (wing 85.9 vs 82.9 mm; tail 54.5 vs 52.5 mm) and the paper explicitly reports significant sex differences; use a subtle size direction only. Western Mexico, near sea level–1,500 m, arid to semihumid thorn, tropical deciduous, semideciduous, dry oak and occasional dry pine-oak woodland.

Limits: no juvenile growth chronology or occupied-nest description.

### CPO-BUCHANAN-1964-P104-106
Buchanan 1964, “The races of the Least Pygmy Owl in Middle America,” *Condor* 66:103–112. https://sora.unm.edu/sites/default/files/journals/condor/v066n02/p0103-p0112.pdf . Printed pp.104–106 read 2026-11-01.

Facts: series-based diagnostic detail for the western Mexican forms under historical *G. minutissimum*: brownish-olive/greyish-tawny crown and darker buffy or olive back, extensive pale crown spotting, irregular pale scapular marks, normally six tail bars (range five–six in named samples), white throat/upper-breast patches and buffy-brown underpart streaking; palmarum female wing sample slightly larger than males. Limits: preserved adults, historic subspecies treatment and no young-bird sequence or living soft-part standard.

### CPO-MEDINA-ETAL-2022-P8-9
Medina-Estrada et al. 2022, “Nesting resource availability for cavity adopter birds in a tropical dry forest of Central Mexico,” *Revista Mexicana de Biodiversidad* 93:e933836. DOI https://doi.org/10.22201/ib.20078706e.2022.93.3836 ; PDF https://classic.scielo.org.mx/pdf/rmbiodiv/v93/2007-8706-rmbiodiv-93-e933836.pdf . Printed pp.8–9 read 2026-11-01.

Facts: treats *G. palmarum* as a small obligate cavity adopter (body width 5 ± 0.35 cm from five UNAM specimens) and concludes small cavity adopters could potentially use resources in both deciduous and semideciduous Alto Balsas forest. Limits: the study surveyed cavity availability, not occupied *G. palmarum* nests; it cannot support a nest interior, egg/chick substrate or exact cavity geometry. Use only habitat/cavity-availability boundary.
