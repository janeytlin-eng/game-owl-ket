## Sources actually read or re-read

### SSCO-CURRIE-ETAL-2002-P16-18 — primary live-bird biometric study, directly re-read
- **Authors/title/year:** D. Currie, A. C. Mateman, C. M. Lessells & R. Fanchette, “Sexual size dimorphism in the critically endangered Seychelles Scops Owl *Otus insularis*,” 2002.
- **URL/DOI:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/03078698.2002.9674272 ; https://doi.org/10.1080/03078698.2002.9674272
- **Read:** printed pp.16–18, especially p.17 results/Table 1 and p.18 discussion.
- **Facts:** 31 adults measured and 30 molecularly sexed. Female/male mean wing 174.42/169.12 mm and mass 154.63/132.43 g, but every measurement overlaps. Plumage lightness and three recorded eye colours—bright yellow, dull green-yellow and orange-red—were not sex-linked. Some field-observed fledglings had bright yellow eyes; the authors found no evidence that eye variation was age-related.
- **Design decision:** female may be fuller/deeper-bodied, not conspicuously taller. Both adults use one plumage system and the same bright-yellow eye choice. V4 corrects v3's unsupported age-coded muted juvenile iris by using the directly observed bright-yellow fledgling option while keeping eye colour explicitly non-universal.

### SSCO-CURRIE-ETAL-2004-P123-137 — primary breeding/development study, directly re-read
- **Authors/title/year:** Dave Currie et al., “The breeding biology of the Critically Endangered Seychelles Scops-owl *Otus insularis*: consequences for conservation and management,” 2004.
- **URL/DOI:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E98F97E78DD111E5C95AE52C4D68D30B/S0959270904000140a.pdf/div-class-title-the-breeding-biology-of-the-critically-endangered-seychelles-scops-owl-span-class-italic-otus-insularis-span-consequences-for-conservation-and-management-div.pdf ; https://doi.org/10.1017/S0959270904000140
- **Read:** printed pp.126–130, especially p.126 fledgling characters, p.128 Table 1 and development narrative, pp.129–130 nest table and post-fledging observations.
- **Facts:** recent fledgling c.8–12 weeks is fluffy, barred, a clumsy flier and gives a “whsst” call; five of 11 had recently left the nest. At two monitored nests, a single young left the cavity before it could fly and perched 3–5 m above it; one flew clumsily about a month later. Nestling period was c.4–6 weeks. Eight documented nests were narrow hand-sized fissures/splits 7–25 m high in *Dillenia ferruginea* or *Pterocarpus indicus*.
- **Design decision:** supported low recent fledgling with a short soft folded wing and no airborne action. Because the observed young perched above the cavity, no nest opening, lining, egg or interior is shown.

## Evidence-to-design boundary
- Female mass direction is supported, but linear measurements overlap: show fuller/deeper body and only subtly longer folded wing, never a dramatically larger owl.
- Adults share one brown morph; iris choice is not sex-coded. Fully bare tarsi and long naturally gripping toes remain required.
- Young is the documented recent-fledgling class, c.8–12 weeks, fluffy/barred and flight-limited. Bright yellow iris is directly observed in some fledglings, not a universal age marker.
- Show the young fully supported on a broad mossy branch above the unseen nest area. Do not reconstruct the cavity, lining, egg, hatchling or unseen feather-by-feather details.
- Exact juvenile sex, individual age, down/feather sequence, iris frequency, first-flight day and nest lining remain unknown.
