## Current/old name mapping

Current production name remains *Ninox japonica*. Historical Japanese literature uses *Ninox scutulata japonica* and older broad-complex *N. scutulata*. Lin, Lin & Tseng (2012) explicitly describe the split of Northern Boobook *N. japonica* from the Brown Boobook complex and distinguish nominate migratory *N. j. japonica* from resident *N. j. totogo*. These names are lookup mappings, not additional production species.

## Evidence actually read

1. **JAP-FUKAI-2016-PP22-28** — Norio Fukai, “Age determination of Brown Hawk-Owl (*Ninox scutulata*),” *Bulletin of the Japanese Bird Banding Association* 28:22–28, 2016; J-STAGE full PDF read.
   - Printed pp.22–23: Japanese *N. s. japonica* has heavily overlapping measurements and similar plumage between sexes; external sexing and size-only sexing are unreliable.
   - Printed pp.23–25 and Fig.1: juveniles begin body/covert molt before flight feathers finish growing. Juvenile/first-winter rectrices taper and have a broad pale terminal fringe similar in width and colour to inner pale bands; adult rectrices are broad/rounded with a much narrower, whiter tip. Other plumage differences are less clear.
   - Printed pp.25–26: all examined juvenile/first-winter birds retained the juvenile-type tail; no sex difference was found in this character. This supports a recent-fledgling tail boundary without inventing a fixed sex cue.
2. **JAP-LIN-LIN-TSENG-2012-PP164-166** — Wen-Loung Lin, Si-Min Lin & Hui-Yun Tseng, “Breeding ecology of the Northern Boobook *Ninox japonica totogo* in central Taiwan,” *Forktail* 28:164–166; author-hosted publisher PDF read.
   - Printed pp.164–165, Tables 1–3: ten tree-cavity nests in secondary/artificial forest and suburbs; nestling period 25–28 days; adults measured during breeding showed no significant male–female difference in bill, head, wing, tail, tarsus or mass.
   - Printed p.166: nominate migratory *japonica* and resident *totogo* differ somewhat in wing/tail proportions. Do not transfer Taiwan measurements into a precise Japanese standing-height ratio.
   - Design use: adults remain near-equal in visible height; the scene does not claim a universal female-larger rule.
3. **JAP-ZUKAN-ASADA-2023-166264** — Masahiro Asada, identified field record, Mie Prefecture, photographed 19 July 2023; page opened 2026-11-06.
   - Page states three young fledged from a repeatedly used old shrine-tree cavity and that the photograph was made the morning they fledged.
   - Design use: select an age-unknown recent fledgling, fully branch-supported beside an old broadleaf cavity; no exact day or sustained-flight claim.
   - Limit: field-photo record, not a measured plumage sequence. It controls action/support and recent-fledgling context only.

## Applied boundaries

- Adults: no ear tufts; dark-brown crown/upperparts; bright yellow eyes; modest white forehead/brow patch; buff-white underparts with long broad dark-brown streaks; long barred tail; cream-feathered tarsi grading to yellow toes. Different natural poses, but near-equal crown height and no fixed sex-size cue.
- Recent fledgling: age unknown, sex unknown; fluffy pale-buff head/body, fewer and softer ventral streaks, formed but fresh folded wings with softened edges, no repeated adult shoulder-spot rows; tail shorter than adults, slightly tapered with a conspicuously broad pale terminal fringe. Both feet grip the same fork; body leans slightly while turning its head. No flight, hopping leap or unsupported wing display.
- Habitat: summer Japanese shrine grove/old broadleaf cavity, leafy green canopy and natural branch supports. Do not depict a nest box, exposed eggs or an invented hatchling.
- Unknown: exact individual age, sex, iris-transition timing, continuous tract chronology, first competent flight, exact adult standing-height relation and population transfer between nominate *japonica* and *totogo*.
