## Sources actually read

1. **GHO-HOFFMEISTER-SETZER-1947** — Hoffmeister & Setzer, “The Postnatal Development of Two Broods of Great Horned Owls”, University of Kansas Museum of Natural History 1(8):157–173. Full text read at Project Gutenberg. Daily observations of three young in 1945 plus one in 1946. pp.165–166: young left at 43–49 days; coordinated flight was impossible for one 43-day bird, whereas a 49-day bird glided with occasional flaps; by days 37–39 erupted primary/secondary lengths were substantial; immature down and flight-feather development are both present. This directly rejects both a tiny all-down wing bud and a fully adult-perfect flight pose for a 44–46-day brancher.
   - URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/35118/pg35118-images.html
2. **GHO-MCGILLIVRAY-1989** — McGillivray, “Geographic Variation in Size and Reverse Size Dimorphism of the Great Horned Owl in North America”, *Condor* 91:777–786, DOI 10.2307/1368060. Abstract read. Eighteen variables from 418 specimens; reverse size dimorphism was virtually constant across regions, while overall body size varied geographically/subspecifically. Supports a visibly fuller/slightly taller female but forbids inventing one fixed pair ratio or subspecies-neutral absolute scale.
   - URL: https://academic.oup.com/condor/article-abstract/91/4/777/5189226
3. **GHO-CORNELL-CAM-2022** — Cornell Lab Bird Cams Great Horned Owl timeline. 2022 chick branched at 46 days and made a short limb-to-limb flight at 55 days; 6-week birds can branch/climb and 7-week birds can make tiring short flights. This supports a fully supported branch pose with no powered flight claim.
   - URL: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/cams/great-horned-owl-cam-timeline/

- Juvenile stage is c.44–46 days, branch-supported. It must show both down and substantially developed but irregularly down-veiled folded flight feathers; short incomplete tail; no unsupported leap or sustained-flight claim.

- Unknown: individual sex/age within the stage band, exact regional plumage, pair-specific ratio, continuous feather tract sequence, and flight performance of the depicted individual.
