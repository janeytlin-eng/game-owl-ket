## Evidence actually read
- POWERFUL-MO-WATERHOUSE-2015 — Mo & Waterhouse, 2015, Australian Field Ornithology 32:143–153, primary near-daily field study. Printed pp.145–147 and Fig.1: two fledglings left the hollow on 5 and 14 August; one flew out and crashed into a shrub, both were observed flying by 17 August; adult plumage began developing by December. Applicable to recent fledgling behaviour, not an exact universal feather sequence.
- POWERFUL-ABSA-2019 — Australian Bird Study Association/HANZAB synthesis, one-page PDF p.1. Fledges in juvenile plumage; post-juvenile to adult-like plumage starts about 3–4 months after fledging and may finish by first-year end; females are marginally smaller; morphometric ranges overlap.
- POWERFUL-NSWSC-2008 — NSW Scientific Committee report, PDF pp.1–2. Adults have narrow dorsal bars, V-shaped ventral chevrons, yellow-orange eyes, feathered legs and large yellow feet; juveniles initially whiter/downier on head and underparts; large old-tree hollow, two-month nestling period, prolonged dependence.
- POWERFUL-MV-2026 (reused institutional record) — Museums Victoria description/biology, lines 67–81 opened again: white-headed, lightly streaked young; pale grey bill, yellow eyes, old-growth hollow and prolonged dependence.
- VIC-RAPTORS-2023 (reused government table) — printed p.85/PDF p.10: juvenile white crown/underparts with dark streaks and eye patches; adult off-white bars.
- WHSO-MIKKOLA-2013 (reused uploaded-guide extract) — printed pp.450–451/PDF pp.452–453, as indexed above.

## Problem → evidence → change
- Problem: v2 visually exaggerates the adult male/female gap and leaves the juvenile with dark, adult-like spotted coverts.
- Evidence: ABSA says the female is only marginally smaller and the measured ranges overlap; government/museum/guide sources agree that juvenile plumage retains a white/downier head and underparts, while the primary field study proves a recent fledgling can already fly and perch.
- Change: v3 uses comparable supports, a only modestly fuller male, and a fully winged/tailed recent fledgling with downier white head/underparts, dark eye patches and broader paler wing bars.
- Unknown: exact hatch age, universal linear ratio, day-by-day tract emergence and individual plumage variation. These remain explicit; `scientifically_verified=false`.
