# 55 · Verreaux's Eagle-Owl · *Bubo lacteus*

Status: RESEARCH_READY (concept production cycle replacement 3/6)
Accessed: 2026-10-14

## Taxonomy
- Project label retains *Bubo lacteus* to match the current repository genus convention.
- Current global treatments may use *Ketupa lactea*; this is recorded as a current combination/synonym mapping, not a second species.

## Evidence facts
- Adult: Africa's largest owl; pale gray-brown, finely vermiculated; creamy facial disc edged dark, dark-brown eyes, small ear tufts, fully feathered legs and partially feathered toes. Bright pink eyelids are diagnostic.
- Sex: accessible institutional sources do not provide a verified linear-measurement table. Do not force a dramatic size difference; render the female only modestly fuller/taller.
- Juvenile stage selected: approximately 60-day nest-leaving climber, not yet reliably flying. It should walk/climb on a large branch beside an old stick nest, with short developing wings and down still softening the head/body outline.
- Pink eyelids can appear from about one week after hatch, so the juvenile may show the diagnostic pink lid without adult feather maturity.
- Nest/environment: reuses large nests of vultures, hamerkops, eagles or crows; sometimes tree hollows. Dry wooded savanna/riverine trees.

## Design boundary
- Exactly three birds: adult male, adult female, one juvenile.
- Adults similar in coloration; no invented plumage dimorphism.
- Juvenile supported by branch/nest structure, no flight pose, no long layered adult remiges, no adult-perfect ear tufts.
- Use *Bubo lacteus* on the plate; record *Ketupa lactea* only in research metadata.
- Concept remains pending scientific calibration.
