## Sources actually read or re-read

### CAMBOO-RASMUSSEN-2012-FORKTAIL — primary description, reused and re-read
- Pamela C. Rasmussen et al. 2012. “Vocal divergence and new species in the Philippine hawk owl *Ninox philippensis* complex.” *Forktail* 28:1–20.
- Original PDF: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255964757_Vocal_divergence_and_new_species_in_the_Philippine_Hawk_Owl_Ninox_philippensis_complex
- Printed pp. 3, 15–16, 19; especially diagnosis and type description on pp.15–16.
- Adult facts used: large size; long/deep bill; full lax plumage; weak face border; regular crown bars; bold coverts; large white scapulars; white throat; broad irregular warm underpart bars; gray/whitish or very pale yellow-green iris; mustard eye-ring/bill/legs; tarsi only thinly bristled; mostly pale claws with dark tips.
- Sex evidence: the two type specimens described are female, and the paper says it could not analyze sexual dichromatism. No fixed female-larger or sex-specific plumage claim is licensed.
- Behavior: two birds were documented facing/leaning close during a song display, with white throat puffed/pulsating and wings drooped. V4 uses this as a pose cue for one adult only; it is not assigned to a sex and is not relabeled as courtship or nesting.
- Juvenile limit: the paper states immature Philippine *Ninox* were scarce and excluded from the adult analysis; it gives only a general tendency for immatures to be fluffier and less marked, not a Camiguin developmental sequence.

## Evidence → design decisions

1. **Adults near equal:** sexes are not visually split by evidence; same species identity and near-equal size.
2. **Iris/tarsi conflict resolved toward primary source:** pale gray/whitish to very pale yellow-green iris; thinly bristled tarsi, not fully feathered.
3. **Pose variation is evidence-backed:** one alert adult plus one adult using the documented throat-puff/wing-droop song-display cue. No sex assignment.
4. **Juvenile uncertainty is explicit:** closed/downcast, supported, fluffy/less-marked, wings/tail concealed, age unknown. No nest, no flight and no detailed Camiguin ontogeny claim.
5. **Environment:** wet Camiguin forest atmosphere is contextual, not a nest-site assertion.
