## Fresh evidence read this cycle

### Direct recent-fledgling record from Honshu

Nohara & Honda 2025, *Bulletin of the Saitama Museum of Natural History* 19:75–78, observed and photographed one adult and one young on 25–26 June 2024 in Nagatoro, Saitama. Printed pp.75–78 / PDF pp.1–4 were read.

- Adult diagnosis: ear tufts, orange iris, feathering on the toes, and a brownish-white nuchal patch (printed p.76).
- Young: entire body covered in down; distinct ear tufts; iris colour not determinable from the photograph (p.76).
- The young perched on Japanese cedar at about 9 m and 10 m on consecutive days, moved little, and had incompletely grown flight and tail feathers; the authors inferred it had only recently left the nest (pp.76–78, Fig.3/Table 1).
- The nest was not found. Adult defensive behaviour indicates breeding nearby, not a known nest structure.

This directly resolves v2 to a **recent fledgling**, while keeping exact day-age, flight ability and iris unknown. The drawing may show a short incomplete developing wing/tail, full branch support and a stationary posture; it must not show flight or a nest.

### Breeding/nest context without morphology transfer

Toyama, Kotaka & Koizumi 2015 monitored 53 Japanese Scops Owl broods in Okinawa, including 10 natural cavities and 43 boxes. Printed pp.841–847 / manuscript lines 121–201 show the setting and method; lines 181–200 report 61–64 days from egg-laying to nest departure, with hatching timing estimated rather than individually observed. Because this is an Okinawa population and the paper gives no plumage plate, it supports only the general natural-cavity/forest and nest-departure context—not the nominate Honshu fledgling's colour or exact age.

### Adult identity and taxonomy

Mikkola 2013 printed p.158 / uploaded PDF p.160 is reused from repository evidence: long prominent tufts, grey double collar, red eyes, small pale scapular patches, barred wings/tail and dark herringbone underparts. No quantitative sex difference was found, so adults remain near-equal and same-plumage.

ITIS TSN 1063233 treats *O. semitorques* as valid and lists nominate *O. s. semitorques* plus *pryeri* and *ussuriensis*. ITIS TSN 1063621 treats the nominate as valid; the older *O. bakkamoena semitorques* combination is retained as a synonym. Tokyo Zoological Park states the species breeds May–July in tree cavities and hatchlings have white down; that hatchling fact is not transferred to the older recent fledgling beyond the directly observed extensive down.
