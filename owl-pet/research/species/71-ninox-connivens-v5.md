## New primary evidence

### BARK-BULLOCK-2026-P172-184

- Trevor Bullock, “Breeding and diet of a family of Barking Owls *Ninox connivens* on the mid-north coast of New South Wales,” *Australian Field Ornithology* 43 (2026):172–184, DOI 10.20938/afo43172184.
- URL: https://afo.birdlife.org.au/afo/index.php/afo/article/download/2416/2452
- Primary multi-season field/camera study; full pp.172–184 and Figures 1, 3–6 read 2026-11-05.
- Nestling period was about 37–41 days. On first post-fledging days the young were clumsy while moving in roost trees; by Day 14 they flew more confidently. On Day 30 one juvenile manipulated a piece of paperbark between bill and foot. On Day 33 the juveniles had adult-like wing and tail proportions while retaining a downy collar and thighs plus white rectrix tips. Adult-like ventral plumage emerged around Day 41, and the collar was then almost lost.
- Limits: one family in a peri-urban coastal NSW setting; age counted from observed fledging, not hatch. “Adult-like proportions” does not establish adult-perfect spot geometry, exact feather counts, or universal timing across subspecies. Object play was one observed individual.
- Design use: select the directly documented Day-30 object-play juvenile, fully branch-supported with adult-like-but-not-adult-perfect wing/tail proportions, obvious downy collar/thighs and white tail tips. Do not transfer Day-41 ventral maturity backward.
