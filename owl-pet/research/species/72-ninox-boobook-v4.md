# #72 *Ninox boobook* v4 research and design boundary

- Current name: *Ninox boobook* (Latham, 1801), mainland nominate concept.
- Historical name mapping used by older sources: *Ninox novaeseelandiae boobook* / “Southern Boobook”; repository follows current *N. boobook*.
- Action: science revision; v1–v3 retained.
- Scientific status: not passed. Exact individual age, individual feather chronology, universal sex ratio and geographic variation remain unknown.
- Accessed: 2026-10-29.

## Evidence actually read

### BOO-OLSEN-JUDGE-TROST-2015 — new, primary

Olsen, Judge & Trost, “Ageing Southern Boobook nestlings and fledglings,” *Corella* 39(3):77 (2015). Full author-uploaded article: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281116013_Ageing_Southern_Boobook_nestlings_and_fledglings

Printed p.77 actually read. Nine wild just-fledged birds from daily-observed nests had 17.0–24.2 cm wing lengths (mean 19.1); the known-age model estimated 35.8–50.2 days (mean 39.9). Some branched to a close limb; others first flew to an adjoining tree up to about 4 m. High individual variation and small sample limit universal inference. This directly supports developed folded wings/tail and firm branch support at the selected recent-fledgling stage, not a flight pose or exact age.

### BOO-OLSEN-TROST-FUENTES-DEBUS-2020 — new, primary

Olsen, Trost, Fuentes & Debus, “Behaviour and family association during the post-fledging period in Southern Boobooks *Ninox boobook*,” *Corella* 44:61–70 (2020). University of Canberra record: https://researchprofiles.canberra.edu.au/en/publications/behaviour-and-family-association-during-the-post-fledging-period-/ ; full article: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/346408279_Behaviour_and_family_association_during_the_post-fledging_period_in_Southern_Boobooks_Ninox_boobook

Printed pp.61–64 and results/discussion actually read. Ten broods in six Canberra territories fledged at 33–41 days (mean 37); young roosted with parents in weeks 1–4, became independently feeding at 44–57 days post-fledge (mean 48), and dispersed 44–72 days post-fledge (mean 56). Regional field series; strong for stage/action boundary, not feather-by-feather chronology.

### BOO-VIC-REHAB-2023 — new, official synthesis

Victorian Government, *Victorian Wildlife Rehabilitation Guidelines, Part B: Birds* (2023), printed p.86: https://www.wildlife.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0025/668122/Victorian-Wildlife-Rehabilitation-Guidelines-Part-B-Birds.pdf

Southern Boobook adult diagnosis: dark facial discs, green-yellow/yellow eyes, dark chocolate-brown upperparts with off-white spots, rufous-brown underparts with white-streaked belly. Juvenile: whitish crown with darker central streaking, dark facial discs, upperparts chocolate-brown profusely white/buff-spotted, downy white underparts with tawny upper breast. Government care synthesis; high for practical ID boundary, not a primary growth series.

## Reused/corrected evidence

- BOO-ABSA-2019, J.W. Hardy/ABSA *Bird in the Hand*, printed pp.1–2: nominate adult measurements overlap; females tend only slightly larger, especially mass. Juvenile iris powder-blue changing to straw-yellow; first-immature crown/nape heavily marked; postjuvenile moult about three months after fledging.
- BOO-AM-2022, Australian Museum species account: adult colour, young buff-white/dark-disc direction, tree hollow with sparse wood shavings/leaves/twigs.
- WHSO-MIKKOLA-2013, Mikkola photographic guide, printed pp.455–456/PDF pp.457–458: broad older island/taxonomic framework; used only as photographic baseline and historical-name crosswalk, not to erase mainland boundary.
- Earlier v3 correction retained the blue-grey iris but did not anchor locomotor maturity. V4 adds the primary field boundary above.

## Design inference

A just-fledged mainland Boobook around the observed 37–40-day center is drawn with developed folded wings, visible tail and a stable two-foot branch grip because real young branch or make short first flights at this stage. Youth remains visible through powder-blue/blue-grey iris, whitish streaked crown, dark facial discs, tawny upper breast and softer irregular upperpart markings. Adults remain near-equal in height; the female is only subtly fuller.
