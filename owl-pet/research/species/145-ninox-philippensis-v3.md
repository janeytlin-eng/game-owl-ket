## Current name and historical concept

Current roster name remains *Ninox philippensis* (Luzon Boobook). ITIS TSN 555484 lists the valid species and the subspecies *centralis*, *philippensis* and *ticaoensis*. Cornell’s 2013 Clements update records the split of the former broad Philippine Hawk-Owl complex and retains these three within *N. philippensis*. Rasmussen et al. (2012) treated philippensis/proxima/centralis/ticaoensis as one diagnosable species while warning that some subspecific limits are weak. These sources support name/concept mapping only where noted; they do not enlarge the 251-species scope.

## Adult evidence actually read

Rasmussen et al. (2012), printed pp.13–14, diagnose a small compact boobook with sleek plumage; small buff-tinged whitish supercilium; plain dark-brown auriculars with long filamentous extensions; poorly defined facial disc; uniform brown crown to rump without barring or speckling; a large white/pale-buff dark-edged scapular patch; one or two large white spots on larger outer wing coverts without barring; weak pale tail bands narrower than the dark bands; narrow finely streaked white throat; and prominent narrow/long dark to rufescent-brown ventral streaking on a white/rufous background. Soft parts are bright yellow iris with paler lemon outer ring, pale olive-yellow cere/bill, bright yellow tarsi/toes and mostly black claws with pale bases. Figure 6a is an adult from Dolores, Quezon, Luzon.

The paper does not establish a fixed display ratio between adult male and female. v3 therefore keeps them nearly equal in displayed size and does not convert pooled/specimen measurements into a universal female-larger rule.
