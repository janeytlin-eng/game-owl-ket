## Evidence read and applied

### BFOW-KFBG-2021
- **Title/author/institution/date:** “What secrets can we discover at a Brown Fish Owl’s nesting site?” / Aiko Leong / Kadoorie Farm and Botanic Garden / 1 October 2021.
- **URL:** https://www.kfbg.org/en/KFBG-blog/post/What-secrets-can-we-discover-at-a-Brown-Fish-Owls-nesting-site
- **Section/figures read:** article text and the two captions “nestling with well-developed feathers shortly before it left the tree nest” and “juvenile … more mobile and exploring the branches close to the nest” (web article has no page numbers).
- **Facts:** one New Territories pair reared one young; the young had well-developed feathers shortly before leaving the tree nest, then became more mobile and explored nearby branches; the nest material contained wood/bark fragments.
- **Limits:** no hatch date, exact age, sex, measured feather lengths, continuous plumage series or first-flight test. Captioned photographs support stage and natural support, not a universal timetable.
- **Design use:** age-unknown Hong Kong late nestling/just-mobile brancher, fully supported on a branch immediately beside the tree nest; formed but down-softened folded wing and developing tail; no flight claim.

### BFOW-BINDU-BALAKRISHNAN-2015
- **Publication:** Bindu & Balakrishnan, *Journal of the Bombay Natural History Society* 112(2), 2015, pp.97–98; DOI 10.17087/jbnhs/2015/v112i2/104938.
- **Fact reused:** Kerala nestling period 43–49 days in measured nesting attempts.
- **Limit:** Kerala population chronology does not date the Hong Kong individual or prescribe exact feathers.

### BFO-INDIANBIRDS-2013
- **Publication:** Vyas et al., *Indian BIRDS* 8(6), 2013, pp.147–151.
- **Facts reused:** 15–20-day chicks were active with fluffy facial down and smaller body/wing feathers; ear tufts became visible later; nest departure occurred after roughly five to six weeks and young remained parent-dependent in the canopy.
- **Limit:** Gujarat *leschenaultii* sequence is context, not a Hong Kong day-age assignment.

### BFOW-ITIS-2025
- **URL:** https://itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?print_version=PRT&search_topic=TSN&search_value=555419&source=to_print
- **Facts:** valid current *Ketupa zeylonensis*; original combination *Strix zeylonensis*; four listed subspecies including *K. z. orientalis*.
- **Limit:** taxonomy only; it does not prove which subspecies the photographed Hong Kong individual represented.

## Design boundary

- Adults: large warm rufous-brown fish owls; shaggy horizontally oriented ear tufts; golden-yellow eyes; shallow facial discs; clear white throat; pale underparts with dark-brown vertical streaks and broken waves; bare granular dusky-yellow tarsi and strong toes.
- Sex: accessible sources do not support visible dichromatism or a measured display ratio. Keep near-equal crown height; female only subtly fuller through pose.
- Juvenile: age unknown; well-developed late nestling/just-mobile brancher from the New Territories evidence. Nearly adult-sized but placed lower, paler and more rufous, with down-softened head/torso margins, smaller horizontal tufts, formed folded wings with subdued irregular juvenile pattern, and a short-to-medium developing tail. No crisp adult covert rows or perfect remige layering.
- Support/action: both feet grip a broad branch immediately beside the tree nest; cautious branch exploration or alert begging, wings folded; no launch or flight.
- Environment: humid New Territories woodland near a stream; large old tree nest/fork with wood and bark material. No fish/prey prop.
- Unknown: exact age, sex, subspecies, continuous feather chronology, first-flight threshold, quantified adult size ratio and regional transfer.
- Target result: PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS; `scientifically_verified=false`.

## Iteration hypothesis

V3 used the Kerala/Indian chronology to correct an over-short late-nestling wing. V4 tests a locality-and-stage gate: a Hong Kong institutional photograph can support a well-feathered, branch-exploring young bird, while the absence of age and feather measurements forbids assigning a day-age or adult-perfect wing. A close wing/support check is required after generation.
