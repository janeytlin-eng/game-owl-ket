## Sources actually read this run

### MAG-FINSCH-1898-P163-172 — new primary museum-series source

- Otto Finsch, “Über *Scops magicus* (S. Müll.) und die verwandten Arten,” *Notes from the Leyden Museum* 20 (1898):163–180, plates 9–10.
- URL: https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/508788/NLM1898020002009.pdf
- Pages read: printed pp.163–172, especially pp.163–164 and 170–172.
- Scope: comparative inspection of 94 museum specimens; *Scops magicus* complex, including Batjan/Halmahera *S. leucospilus*, younger male, downy young, wing/tail development and tarsal feathering.
- Facts: plumage and marking vary widely; the paper says dependence of colour stages on age or sex could not be established. Halmahera material includes a younger male with paler belly and still-barred concealed white shoulder marks. Downy young with wing and tail feathers at differing developmental states show fine dark cross-barring, and their remiges can be as distinctly cross-banded as adults. Most tarsi are feathered nearly to the toe bases, but some specimens, including nest-plumage birds, have only the upper approximately two-thirds feathered and the rear surface bare. Tail barring is highly variable and not safely represented as one universal exact count.
- Limits: historical prepared series, old broad complex and no known-age live growth sequence; exact age, iris, locomotor capacity, first flight, dependency and population-wide sex-size direction remain unknown. The paper cannot turn one younger male into a universal juvenile phenotype.

## Corrected inference boundaries

1. **Juvenile wing pattern:** v4 treated visible barred remiges as an adult-like error. Finsch directly describes fine dark crossbars on developing wing/tail feathers and states the remiges may be as distinctly barred as adults. V5 may show a short, partly visible, developing barred wing, but not a full hard adult wing or a flight claim.
2. **Tarsal feathering:** v4 converted one modern numeric summary into an exactly measurable exposed distal band. The museum series shows variation. V5 shows feathering nearly to the toe bases with only a small irregular distal exposure; it is illustrative, not a 6–9 mm measurement claim.
3. **Adult pattern counts:** historical tail/shoulder barring varies substantially. V5 uses irregular sparse shoulder marks and broken bars, not exact repeated rows or a fixed band count.
