## Evidence boundary

- Adults: 15–16 cm, rounded head without ear tufts, yellow bare parts, brown adult morph with fine crown/nape dots, paired false eyes, irregular breast band, barred/spotted sides and short four-banded tail.
- Sex: available weights point to heavier females, but provide no linear height separation. v4 therefore keeps near-equal crown height and only subtly fuller female body.
- Juvenile: select the institutionally defined late-nest/end-of-nest-life stage. Crown/nape plain or extremely finely streaked; underpart pattern weaker than adults; bird fully supported at a natural cavity lip.
- Unknown: exact age, iris-change timing, continuous feather sequence, flight threshold and sexed linear measurements. No flight action or scientific-pass claim.
- Nest/environment: humid Andean cloud forest and a natural mossy cavity; no claim of a measured nest microhabitat.

## Search outcome

Targeted searches used current and historical names with juvenile, nestling, fledgling, development and flight terms. No primary known-age developmental series was located. An eye-orbit morphology paper contained one adult specimen but did not address the design gap and was rejected for this card. Macaulay/eBird media leads were not used as development evidence.

## Correction and iteration rule

Earlier “female heavier” evidence had been easy to over-render as a taller female. v4 corrects that translation: mass direction may support a subtly fuller body, not a categorical height difference. The juvenile must not inherit neat adult crown dots, repeated bright covert spots or mature tail/flight-feather order.
