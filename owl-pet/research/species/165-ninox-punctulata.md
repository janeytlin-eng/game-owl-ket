## Evidence ledger

### Adult identity

- Small, rounded head; no ear-tufts.
- Dark-brown eyes, dark facial disc, narrow white eyebrows and a large white throat half-collar.
- Dull reddish-brown/gray-brown upperparts with dense small white spots on crown, neck, mantle and wings.
- Narrow pale tail bars; variable brown-and-white upper-breast bands/oval, white lower belly and pale-barred flanks.
- Greenish-yellow bill; feathered heavy tarsi; yellowish-gray toes and dark claws.

### Sex and size boundary

Mikkola reports similar colour and a slightly larger female, but only one sexed measurement was available. The plate therefore keeps the adults nearly equal; the female may be subtly fuller but there is no fixed ratio, plumage exaggeration or invented sex-specific colour.

### Juvenile stage

Mikkola explicitly describes the mesoptile as dark brown above and below, with eyebrows mixed dark brown and white, and states that the wing and tail feathers are as adult. This is a species-specific exception to the normal prompt safeguard against adult-like juvenile remiges. The plate may show adult-patterned folded wing/tail feathers, but the body remains softer/fluffier, the posture is supported, and no claim is made about exact age, flight ability, juvenile iris or a continuous molt sequence.

### Habitat and support

Primary forest near streams is preferred; open woodland, cultivation and settlements also occur. The primary Fitzsimons et al. (2012) study places a known sheltered roost beside rainforest, river and agriculture and supports ecological flexibility. The concept uses one continuous humid stream-side forest-edge scene with a broad mossy branch or ledge. It does not invent a nest, eggs or cavity geometry.
