## Evidence actually read

### `BWO-GERHARDT-1994-JSTOR`
Gerhardt et al., “Breeding Biology and Home Range of Two Ciccaba Owls,” *Wilson Bulletin* 106(4):629–639, DOI 10.2307/4163480. The JSTOR record/abstract states four Black-and-white Owl nests at Tikal were all in epiphytes in large live trees, mean nest height 20.5 m, each with one egg. The 27–33-day fledging result belongs to Mottled Owl, not this species and is explicitly excluded.

### `BWO-AVESCR-2025`
Aves de Costa Rica species account (accessed lines 38–44) describes sooty-black adults, narrow pale flight/tail bars, pale-flecked facial-disc border, barred nuchal collar, finely black/white barred underparts, orange-brown/chestnut iris and orange-yellow bill/feet. Juveniles are described as white above with tight dark-brown barring, black wing coverts with some white bars, whitish face and buff-white barred underparts. It records two adults and a juvenile in December but gives no exact age, sex or capability.

### Reused primary evidence
`BWO-GERHARDT-1997` supplies one measured Tikal pair (female 535 g, wing 29.3 cm, tail 18.7 cm; male 436 g, 28.6 cm, 16.5 cm) and similar visual direction in unmeasured pairs. This supports a restrained fuller-female direction, not a population formula. `BWO-MIKKOLA-2013-P339` is reused from repository extraction; uploaded guide unavailable.

## Design boundary
Use the actual older-juvenile plumage description but keep wing/tail/action conservative because exact age and development are absent. Show the regional Tikal epiphyte platform; do not generalize it as the only nest type. Adults retain near-compatible standing height with only subtle female fullness.
