1. The first render gave the juvenile a long, hard layered folded wing with repeated pale/orange spots. Riekert p.426 supports emerged wing/body/tail feathers by day 22, but not a mature regular adult panel.
2. One center-only edit shortened and down-softened the folded wing, removed the regular rows and shortened the tail while retaining a genuinely formed fresh wing. Adults, exact labels, nest box, calling pose and support were preserved.

## Source comparison

- **Riekert 1986 p.425:** measured pair both 140 mm wing chord, plumage almost identical; female mass higher. The plate therefore does not make the female taller or colour-code sex.
- **Riekert 1986 p.426:** day-22 body feathers including tail largely emerged; young calling/scratching at entrance. Plate action and broad feather state pass. Exact tract geometry is not claimed.
- **Brown et al. 1987 abstract:** 25–28-day nestling periods in two pairs conflict with universalizing the earlier day-23 departure. The label explicitly says “22-DAY STUDY NESTLING,” not “22-day fledgling” or a universal threshold.
- **Melo et al. 2022:** mainland taxon scope retained; broad museum variation is not used as sex calibration.

## Iteration rule

When a primary study supplies a dated behavioral/feather milestone but not photographs or tract measurements, the dated stage can be depicted and labelled as a **study stage**. Render only the documented broad state/action; keep feather geometry conservative and mark population transfer, sex and flight competence unknown. A one-pair mass difference may inform slight fullness only when equal wing chord and near-identical plumage are preserved.

## Remaining unknowns

Exact feather-tract geometry, juvenile sex/subspecies, sustained-flight ability, cross-population departure timing, universal adult mass ratio and whether the selected study-stage appearance transfers unchanged beyond the Windhoek population remain unknown. Repository verification cannot convert this concept into scientific approval.
