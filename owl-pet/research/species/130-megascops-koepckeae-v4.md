## Scope

## Sources actually read
- **KOEP-ITIS-686665-2026 (new):** Integrated Taxonomic Information System, TSN 686665, taxonomy and nomenclature section, accessed 2026-11-06. Valid species *Megascops koepckeae*; synonym *Otus choliba koepckei*. https://www.itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?search_topic=TSN&search_value=686665
- **KOEP-MIK2013-222 (re-read):** Heimo Mikkola, *Owls of the World: A Photographic Guide*, Library PDF pp.224–225 / printed pp.222–223. Adult identification: 24 cm, 110–148 g, short distinct tufts, no sexual-size data/apparently monomorphic, yellow eyes, bluish–greenish horn bill/cere, no clear flight-feather barring, no white nuchal collar; nestling pale gray down and bluish-gray bill. The facing-page pair caption says no visible sex colour/size difference in *hockingi*. This does not establish a precise nominate sex ratio.
- **KOEP-ALARCON-2023 (re-read):** Alarcón et al., 2023, *Neotropical Biodiversity* 9:93–114, DOI 10.1080/23766808.2023.2237229, *M. koepckeae* species section/Fig.1c. Two Loja fledglings retained gray-white barred nessoptiles, brown chest, black facial-disc edges, white eyebrows and yellow eyes; perched 3–4 m on *Podocarpus* branches with parental care. Exact age and flight threshold are not supplied.
- **KOEP-ORDONEZ-FREILE-2019 (re-read):** Ordóñez-Delgado & Freile 2019, pp.25–29, DOI 10.18272/reo.vi5.1193. Ecuador records; species larger than *M. roboratus*, lacks white nuchal collar and has stronger ventral herringbone. Habitat includes Eucalyptus woodland and humid montane forest. The 2014 bird found beside a palm does not establish a palm nest.
- **KOEP-MACAULAY-200950241 (new, limited):** Cornell Macaulay Library asset ML200950241, 7 Aug 2018, *M. k. hockingi* fledgling in Apurimac; media notes report tree perching and flight. Institutional media metadata; applies to southern subspecies and one older fledgling only. It is not used to assign flight ability or colour to the Loja nominate juvenile. https://macaulaylibrary.org/asset/200950241

## V3 issue and v4 design boundary
Direct v3 inspection shows both adults still carry repeated pale scallop rows on the folded wings, while the juvenile shows a long, coherent adult-like folded panel. V4 will retain the accepted three-bird composition but replace adult wallpaper spots with irregular shaft streaks/transverse branches and reduce the juvenile wing to a compact, down-veiled formed cluster. Adults stay near equal in displayed height. No exact age, universal nest form, sex ratio or flight claim is made.
